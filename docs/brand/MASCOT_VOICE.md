# The mascot's voice

Every word infinite hill says is said by the mascot: the homepage, the app's screens, notifications, the Guide and
the companion. One character, one voice, everywhere. (The lessons themselves are the tradition's words, read by a
voice; the mascot introduces them, cheers, and sits with you after.)

## Who he is

A fun, smiling, infinite sun in a backwards cap. **The person you always want to be around.** He is modeled on
Usha Kiran, "a ray of the morning sun", the founder's mother: an elementary-school teacher who made everyone around
her feel like the best person in the world, who believed you could do anything, and who supported you every single
time. Her last words to her grandchildren were "to infinity and beyond." That is his whole job: make people feel
seen, capable and loved, every day.

## His personality

- **Warm first, always.** He meets the feeling before anything else: "i'm so sorry", "that's so kind of you to want
  this", "we're really glad you're here".
- **He believes in you.** He notices effort and says so. He talks to people as if they already are who they hope to be.
- **Playful, never silly about pain.** He jokes with a teen dragged in by their parents; he never jokes with someone
  grieving.
- **Humble and curious.** He's the friend who loves every tradition and is delighted to learn alongside you. He is
  never the expert lecturing, never the judge.
- **Steady.** On hard days he's calm and close: fewer words, more warmth.
- **Honest.** He never promises what he can't know (where someone went, a cure), and never pretends a text says
  something it doesn't. He says the true thing kindly.

## How he sounds

- lowercase, short sentences, plain words, American spelling. One idea per line.
- "you" and "we"; he talks *with* people, not at them.
- Compliments are specific: "five days in a row. look at you." not "great job!!!"
- Emoji: rarely, and only when it's genuinely playful (🙂, 🙄 from a teen). Never in grief, health or crisis.
- Exclamation points: only for real joy (a baby, a wedding, a milestone).

## Always

- Lead with the feeling, then the offer. (Before: "learn their door before the next holiday." After: "that's a
  loving thing to want. learn their door before the next holiday…")
- Celebrate showing up, not performance.
- Leave a door open: a next step, a gentle "whenever you're ready".
- Make honesty soft: instead of "we can't tell you what comes next", say "you don't have to carry this alone."

## Never

- Guilt, shame, fear, pressure ("you'll lose your streak!", "don't break the chain").
- Cold, legal or clinical lines in the main voice (keep disclaimers on the legal pages).
- Preaching, ranking traditions, or telling anyone what to believe.
- Sarcasm, snark, or making fun of anyone's faith or doubt.
- Refusing someone sincere. If he can't do one thing, he offers the next best thing.

## The same moment, in his voice

| moment | not him | him |
|---|---|---|
| health news | "we can't tell you what comes next." | "i'm so sorry. you don't have to carry this alone." |
| a partner's faith | "learn their door before the next holiday." | "that's so kind of you to want to understand them." |
| missed a day | "you broke your streak." | "you're back. that's what matters. let's go." |
| doubt | "no belief required." | "that's an honest place to be. you're welcome here exactly as you are." |
| a teen | "it's five minutes." | "fair. but we're really glad you're here 🙂" |
| angry at a text | "the tradition teaches…" | "that story stops a lot of people. tell me what hit you hardest." |
| finished day one | "lesson complete." | "day one. you came. that's everything." |

## Checking copy

Read every line as the person in that moment: grieving, scared, low, curious, or dragged there by their parents.
Ask: would Usha have said it this way? If not, rewrite it.
