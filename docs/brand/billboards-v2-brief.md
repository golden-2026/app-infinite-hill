# Billboards v2: the brief

Creative director, 2026-11-10. This replaces the v1 joke boards. Status: proposal. Every "every faith says" claim goes to the Keepers before anything is made public.

**The one thing to remember:** the shock is never the picture. The shock is a true sentence that catches the reader doing exactly what their own faith told them not to do.

The owner was right about v1. They were a beverage campaign wearing a faith brand's clothes. Below is what the bar actually is, what killed v1, three directions and one recommendation.

## 2. Kill list: what made v1 bad, written as rules

1. **No punchlines on peace.** A joke releases the tension that the line is supposed to create. "It's a whole thing." is a shrug. These boards should make you go quiet. They should not make you smirk.
2. **No commonality-through-snacks.** "We share fries" shrinks the hardest thing humans do (making peace with people who hurt them) into a shopping trip. If the act costs nothing, it isn't the idea.
3. **No faith person as a prop.** A turbaned Sikh man eating a hot dog by a flaring grill, under "Fire tried. I'm still here.", is the worst image in the set. Many Sikhs keep dietary rules. Fire next to a Sikh man can read as the 1984 killings or post-9/11 hate attacks. A brand that says it honors every faith must never use one as set dressing for a gag.
4. **No scripture riffs.** "Love your neighbor. Even the loud one." is a pun on Jesus's words. Quote the teaching straight or don't quote it.
5. **No grinning.** Glossy, smiling young models are stock photos. Nobody on these boards smiles at the camera. Dignity, not delight.
6. **No brand-color costumes.** Lime hoodies turn people into merch models. People wear their own clothes. Lime lives only in the type.
7. **No fake first-person testimony.** An AI-generated person saying "I bow to people I disagree with" is an invented testimonial, and that breaks a standing owner rule. First-person lines run only over real people saying their own true words, with consent. AI photoreal is allowed for internal mockups, or for scenes that make no claim to be anyone's real story.
8. **No tension, no board.** Every image needs two forces pulling against each other: hurt and grace, judgment and truth. A pool float has no stakes.
9. **No lime on lime, no lime on photos.** Lime type sits on black, or black type sits on lime. Never lime type over an image.
10. **No unbroken type.** Every line is broken by hand and tested at phone width (375px) and at 14x48 thumbnail size before anyone sees it. If it overflows, the line is too long. Rewrite the words; don't shrink the type.
11. **Never claim the faiths are the same.** "All religions say the same thing" offends the exact Christian viewer we lead with, and it tilts toward one theology. We claim only one specific shared command per board, checked by every Keeper.
12. **No board that could hurt the wrong reader.** Example killed below: "every faith says honor your father" would tell an abuse survivor to call their abuser. Read every line as the most hurt person who will drive past it.

## 3. Three directions

### Direction A: "EVERY FAITH SAYS"

**The idea:** each board names one command every faith shares, then a second line catches us breaking it this week.

**Why it shocks:** it's an accusation the reader can't argue with, because their own tradition agrees with it. The second line is so specific it feels like we read their phone.

**Why it's not preachy or one-faith:** the target is "us", including the brand, never a group or a faith. No faith is ranked, and no faith is said to be the same as another. Only one shared command is claimed per board. He Gets Us can't run this line. Only a brand that holds eight paths can.

**Christian-first:** the launch board is "love your enemy". A Christian reads it as Jesus's words and then learns the whole world was handed the same assignment.

| # | Headline (setup / turn) | Image |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | EVERY FAITH SAYS LOVE YOUR ENEMY. / MOST OF US JUST BLOCK THEM. | Documentary black and white. A woman in her 30s sits on the edge of a bed at night, lit only by her phone. Her thumb is over a contact (a first name only, no "Dad" or "Mom"). Side light from the screen, everything else in shadow. 35mm, eye level, high-ISO grain. She isn't crying. She's deciding. |
| A2 | EVERY FAITH SAYS FORGIVE. / NOBODY WANTS TO GO FIRST. | Two adult brothers in a church parking lot after a funeral, both in black suits, about ten feet apart. One holds car keys. Neither looks at the other. Flat overcast light, long lens from across the lot so we feel like we're intruding. Black and white. |
| A3 | EVERY FAITH SAYS WELCOME THE STRANGER. / WE CHECK THE DOORBELL CAM FIRST. | The grammar of a doorbell camera: fisheye, rain on the lens, a timestamp burned in, no brand logo. A delivery driver in his 50s, soaked, looks up into the lens with a tired, decent face. He is fully dignified; the camera is the one being judged. Black and white with a slight cold cast. |
| A4 | EVERY FAITH SAYS FEED THE HUNGRY. / HE'S BEEN ON THAT CORNER ALL YEAR. | **No photo.** Type only, placed within sight of a real corner where people ask for help (like Amnesty's location trick). We never photograph unhoused people to sell an app. Phone version: type only. |
| A5 | EVERY FAITH SAYS YOU'RE NO BETTER THAN ANYONE. / YOU JUST THOUGHT OF SOMEONE. | **No photo.** Black board, type only. The reader's own mind supplies the image. That's the hardest-hitting board in the set. |

**Killed while writing:** "honor your father" (could hurt abuse survivors), "we built drive-thrus" (cute), "every faith says the same thing" (theologically false and alienating) and "we unfollowed them" (a joke, not an accusation).

**Layout system:**
- **14x48 bulletin (about 3.4 to 1).** Black field. On photo boards, the black-and-white photo takes the left 45%, cut hard with no fade. The setup line runs in white Anton at about one-eighth of the board's height. The turn line runs in **lime #EEFF6A Anton at about a quarter of the board's height** (roughly 3.5 ft letters, readable at about 500 ft). Lime touches only the turn line. The wordmark "infinite hill" sits bottom right in white at about 4% of the board's height, with "making religion yours again." in small type beneath it. No Finn: a cartoon on an accusation board turns it into a joke. Leave a 6% margin on all sides.
- **Words:** the turn line is 6 words or fewer. The setup line repeats across the series, so drivers learn it after one board and only read the turn.
- **Phone (1080x1350 feed, 1080x1920 story).** Photo on the top half or none. The setup line is small and white. The turn line is lime and broken by hand to 2 or 3 words per line, filling the width with 64px margins. It must read at a 375px-wide preview. If a word won't fit, change the word.

### Direction B: "THE HARD SEAT" (true stories)

**The idea:** real people say, in their own words, the hardest kind thing they've done for someone who hurt them, the Humans of New York model at billboard volume.

**Why it shocks:** the act is almost unbearable to read ("I SAVED A SEAT FOR THE ONE WHO HURT ME"). Your first reaction is "I couldn't." That's the hook.

**Why it's not preachy or one-faith:** nobody tells you what to do. One person tells you what they did. The faith shows in a detail (a kippah, a cross necklace, a kara) and is never explained. Across the series every path is represented. The only line that ties them together is the endline.

**Hard rule:** these five lines show the **shape** only. Not one can run until it's replaced by, or confirmed as, a real person's true story, with their consent, photographed for real. Inventing these would be fake testimonials. Sourcing goes through the Keepers and faith communities, the same way the owner recruits.

| # | Headline (their words) | Image |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | I SAVED A SEAT FOR THE ONE WHO HURT ME. | Christian lead. A woman in her 60s sits at her own kitchen table, holiday-set, with one empty chair pulled out beside her. She looks straight into the lens, not smiling: steady. Window light from one side, medium format, black and white. The pulled-out chair is the whole story. |
| B2 | I DRIVE THE MAN WHO FIRED ME TO CHEMO. | A man in his 40s sits behind the wheel of his own car in a hospital parking structure, engine off, both hands on the wheel. Hard fluorescent overhead light. Shot through the windshield, black and white. The passenger seat is empty: he's waiting. |
| B3 | I TAUGHT THE BOY WHO BULLIED MY SON TO DRIVE. | A dad in his 50s in the passenger seat of a beat-up sedan, a teenage boy at the wheel, both facing forward, an empty lot at dusk. Shot from the back seat. Their faces are only half visible: dignity for the kid too. |
| B4 | WE FED THE MEN WHO VANDALIZED OUR GURDWARA. | Run only if a real community lived this and agrees to tell it. The langar line: steel trays, volunteers' hands serving, the backs of two young men being served. No Guru imagery in frame. Steam, warm tungsten turned to black and white. Keeper sign-off required. |
| B5 | I PRAYED FOR HIM. I'M STILL ANGRY. BOTH ARE TRUE. | A young man in his 20s on his own front steps at dawn, hood down, eyes open, looking just off camera. Cold blue light turned to black and white. Honest, not resolved. This board keeps the series from becoming saintly. (Prayer here is his choice and his act; the board asks nothing of the reader.) |

**Layout system:** the black-and-white portrait fills the full board. The quote sits in a **solid black bar** across the bottom third, in white Anton, all caps, with tight leading. The endline "every faith asks for this." runs small in lime inside the bar, with the white wordmark bottom right. Lime never touches the photo. On phones, it's a 4:5 portrait with the quote stacked in 3 or 4 hand-broken lines in the black bar. Ten words or fewer per quote, edited from the person's own words with their approval.

### Direction C: "SECOND LOOK"

**The idea:** frame one shows a cropped moment that makes you judge someone. Frame two shows the whole scene, and you were wrong. It's The Guardian's "Points of View" made into a board.

**Why it shocks:** it doesn't accuse you. It lets you accuse yourself, live, in three seconds.

**Why it's not preachy or one-faith:** the "judged" people cut across every group, including a Christian in a Jesus T-shirt judged by a skeptic. That balance is what keeps it honest. The endline, "every faith says: look again.", is the only faith line.

| # | Frame 1 / Frame 2 | Image |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | YOU THOUGHT HE WAS STARTING SOMETHING. / HE WAS ENDING IT. | Crop: a big man in a turban grabs a younger man's arm on a night street, and the younger man's face is twisted. Full: he's pulling the kid back from stepping into traffic, a bus blurring past. Sodium streetlight, handheld 35mm, a frame from a film. |
| C2 | YOU ROLLED YOUR EYES AT THE JESUS SHIRT. / HE'S FOUR HOURS INTO A SHELTER SHIFT. | Crop: a guy in his 20s, loud church-camp T-shirt, mid-laugh, a bit much. Full: the same guy at 2 AM, ladling soup in a fluorescent shelter kitchen, tired. (Christian-first, and it turns the skeptic's judgment around too.) |
| C3 | YOU CROSSED THE STREET. / SO DID HE. TO HELP HER UP. | Crop: a hooded young man moving fast toward the camera across a wet street. Full: an elderly woman who has fallen at the curb behind us, groceries spilled, and he's already kneeling. Dusk, rain, wide lens. |
| C4 | YOU MOVED SEATS WHEN SHE SAT DOWN. / SHE WAS GIVING THE KID HER COAT. | Crop: a woman in a hijab sits down hard on a night train, face unreadable. Full: she's wrapping her coat around a shivering teenager across the aisle. Interior train light, documentary. An ordinary Muslim woman on a train, not at prayer. Keeper check. |
| C5 | YOU ALREADY DECIDED ABOUT HIM. / HE STOPPED. NOBODY ELSE DID. | Crop: a man in his 60s, rough face, faded tattoos, an old pickup. Full: on a highway shoulder, changing a tire for a young family whose car has failed. Late golden light, long lens. |

**Layout system:** on phones and digital boards this is two frames, as a swipe or a 4-second rotation. That's where it's strongest. On a static 14x48, split the board: the left 35% is the crop, in black and white, with its line in white. The right 65% is the full scene, in muted color (this is the only direction that uses color, because the reveal needs detail), with its line in lime on a black band. The wordmark sits bottom right. **Weakness:** a static billboard at 65 mph can't really deliver a reveal. This direction is a film-and-social idea first.

## 4. My recommendation: Direction A, "EVERY FAITH SAYS", with B as its proof

**Why A:**
1. **Only we can say it.** He Gets Us spent tens of millions and still had to end on one faith. "Every faith says love your enemy" is the one line our eight paths own and they can't. It's our moat on a billboard.
2. **It meets the owner's goal word for word:** a big statement, commonality you can see, and a push to treat each other better. The shock is the accusation, not a cheap picture.
3. **Christian-first without being Christian-only.** It opens on words every Christian knows by heart, then widens.
4. **It's the most buildable at a top-agency level now.** Two of five boards need no photo, and the rest need three documentary images. No fake testimonials, no faces of holy figures, nothing to fake.
5. **It's loud the way Magna is loud:** black, one lime line, one heavy font, no clutter. It reads at highway speed and on a phone.

**Then B.** When we have real true stories, the owner's favorite line ("I SAVED A SEAT FOR THE ONE WHO HURT ME") runs as the emotional peak, with the A endline under it. C becomes the launch film or Reels series, not a static board.

**Pushback for the owner, plainly:**
- The shock has to come from truth, not from images of violence or from making fun. The moment a board gets a laugh, it stops being taken seriously. That's what happened to v1.
- "Every faith says the same thing" is the line people expect from us. It's false, and it will lose the Christian viewer. We claim one shared command per board, and the Keepers check each one.
- B is the best work in this brief and the slowest to make. It only runs with real people. Don't let anyone generate it to save time.

**Before anything goes public:** Keeper sign-off on every "every faith says" claim (A1, A2, A3, A4 and A5), a legal check on the doorbell-camera look (no brand trade dress) and on the headline system, and phone-size screenshots to the owner.
