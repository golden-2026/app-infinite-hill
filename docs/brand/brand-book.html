01 · our story

every generation had a way through. we walked away.

Every generation before us had a way through hard days. They called it religion. Ours walked away from it, and replaced it with a feed. Now we're divided and confused. But we can learn, together.

Infinite Hill is the best of a video game and AI: a beautiful world you step into every day, and a guide you already know who talks with you, learns who you are and plans your days. When life gets loud, you step in to get grounded and guided. Then you step back into your life, a little steadier. Step in to remember who you are. Step out to live it.

the tagline · locked

making religion yours again.

It sits under the logo, everywhere. Spanish: "la religión, tuya otra vez". It replaced "making religion cool again" on 20 Oct: "cool" felt shallow and could read as a joke about something sacred; "yours" carries the offer (your faith, or your own). Never bring "cool again" back.

the big idea · locked 22 Oct

step in to remember who you are. step out to live it.

The world and your real life keep crossing over: breathe in your car while your screen is the waterfall, ask a pastor anything out loud, then walk into a real church on Sunday. Inside the company: from ego to awareness and back, easier each time. In public we say it in everyday words.

the hero copy · 3 Nov

because you gotta have faith.

"understand yourself. understand each other. pick a faith or build your own faith, with celebrity guides and voices you know." Headline and one line, nothing more.

Before paid ads: the headline echoes George Michael's "Faith". Never use the song or its look without a license, and a trademark lawyer glances at it first.
the offer

pick a faith, or build your own.

Go deep in one faith, or choose "my own religion", where exploring every faith and what they share lives (opt-in, curated to you by the AI). Say "build your own faith", not "create your own religion". The owner thinks many people will pick "my own religion", so it has to be incredible.

sell the want

nobody wakes up wanting to learn religion.

People come for a want: answers when the kids ask, peace at a two-faith table, the meaning behind prayers they already say, something real without the rules, help in a hard moment. Heroes, ads and store pages lead with the want and the outcome. The daily loop is the how, never the pitch.

someone you already know, who knows you.

02 · what everything is for

big statements out loud. one faith at a time inside.

"the goal of everything we're doing is to create these big statements that get people to see commonality and treat each other better."the owner · 10 Nov 2026

That purpose works on two layers, and they must not blur. The brand shouts the common ground. The lessons go deep in one faith. If a lesson ever feels like comparative religion, it's wrong.

layer 1 · the brand

loud, shared, about how we treat each other.

Billboards, the site hero, social. Bold statements with present-day people, Magna-level confidence. We claim one specific shared teaching at a time, checked by every Keeper. Never "all religions are the same".

layer 2 · the lessons

one faith, deep.

Inside a faith, the Guide's answers, stories, places, passport and offers all stay in that faith: no cross-faith answers, no "visit another faith" offers (10 Nov). Switching faith is a quiet link in the You tab. Films and the site show one person going deeper in one faith over days, never "8 of 8 faiths visited". The eight-homes map and the gondola are gone (4 Nov).

where faiths meet

"my own religion".

Cross-faith exploring, "what they share" and the full passport live only here. Every pick is curated to the person. Each piece names its tradition plainly, never blended or ranked. Frame it as "more in common than you think", never "all the same".

the inversion: faith fully real, packaging modern.

The reference is Radhika Das and Jahnavi Harrison's nights: real bhajans, sung by people who look like cool everyday people, with a mixed band (saxophone, guitars, musicians of every background, Hare Krishna monks among them), in a Hackney club, to a crowd that's mostly not Indian. The owner: "it's dope." We're more alike than different, and people feel it through shared experience like music. It's never taught as a side-by-side comparison. Look for the same mix in every creator and guide we cast.

Christian-first in marketing.

Assume the main viewer is an American Christian family. Lead with familiar Christian moments: grace at dinner, church, a gospel choir, Christmas, the lost sheep. Christianity is first in every list. Show every other faith warmly, alongside. Never open a film, page or pitch with Diwali, kirtan or Ganesha. This is marketing order only: the Hindu-parents alpha and the Hindu lesson pilot don't change.

04 · values

what we hold to, every screen.

These came from real decisions in the product, not a workshop. Each one already changes how something works.

your faith is yours.

People choose their path, and everything inside it stays in that faith. Someone who wants to stay in their own tradition never sees another religion unless they choose "my own religion". We never rank a faith or ask anyone to switch.

never pushy. prayer is a choice.

"Pray" can feel intimidating. Wherever prayer appears, say plainly that it's your choice: watch, learn what it means, or join in. Nobody is asked to believe anything, and nobody is asked to sing.

we score learning, never faith.

Points for knowing a word or a story. Never for praying, never for believing. The breath, the sit and the line you carry have no timer and no score. No leaderboards.

honest about what's real.

We never invent a testimonial or a community number, and we show real counts only above a threshold. We don't claim a feature works until a live request proves it. Nothing religious goes public before a scholar of that faith has read it, and unreviewed work is simply not shown: no "draft" labels in anything a user sees.

joy is loud here.

Finn bounces in on your wins, your streak gets lit, drops land with a countdown, a lantern lights when you finish. Serious about faith; never solemn about ourselves.

everyone is welcome, including unsure.

Grew up in a faith and not sure anymore, never had one, practice every day: each gets a way in that fits, and nobody is asked to believe anything.

a company that gives.

Infinite Hill is a for-profit company, so it can pay its own way and never has to beg. Part of what it earns goes to the Infinite Hill Foundation, a separate nonprofit being set up now. Anything someone gives inside the app goes to the Foundation in full. The Foundation funds scholarships so people who can't pay still get the year, free plans for students and youth groups, and the traditions our lessons come from.

How we say it: "part of what we earn goes to the Infinite Hill Foundation". Always add "in formation" until the papers are filed, and don't name a percentage until one is set.

In the app your path's celebrity guide says every line and says "i"; Finn has no lines (14 Nov). On the website the company speaks as "we". "we" only ever means the company. the voice rules

  1. Meet the feeling, then the offer."i'm so sorry. you don't have to carry this alone." Even funny lines stay kind. Never guilt, shame, fear or pressure.
  2. Always motivate.Progress lines celebrate: "you climbed higher", "look how far you've come". Never "the hill never ends", "only one step", "just".
  3. Roast the stale, never the faith.Finn can call out boring faith apps: tired fonts, stock sunsets, guilt notifications, paywalls on prayer, hour-long audio. "your grandma's prayer app called. it wants its font back." Never name a competitor. Never mock a faith, clergy, believers or grandparents: the joke lands on the app, never the grandma.
  4. Say "celebrity guides".Never just "famous guides", which could mean famous scholars. Don't name a celebrity in copy unless that use is approved.
  5. Never say "signed".Name celebrities as guides. Not "signed", "confirmed" or "under contract" about any celebrity or scholar, in anything shared outside the company. Say "a scholar of each faith reviews every class".
  6. The guide talks; Finn celebrates.Your path's celebrity guide asks every question and says every scripted line, with their photo and name. Finn has no lines and is never voiced: still art and a bounce only. The live AI chat is labeled "your [faith] guide · powered by AI", never "Denzel says" (14 Nov; replaces the 10 Nov "mascot chats" rule).
  7. Prayer is a choice.Pair "pray" with choice: "pray, if you want", "watch, learn, or join in".
  8. No sing-along.Never ask anyone to sing, hold a mic, "hold to sing" or "say it back". Music stays; singing along is their own free choice, never prompted or measured.
  9. No romance, ever.No love, flirting or dating angle. When the owner asks for "wow", he means spectacle and jaw-drop.
  10. No draft labels, no limits talk.No "draft", "review pending", "demo" or "stand-in" in anything users see. Never mention daily AI limits.
  11. Never invent people or numbers.No made-up testimonials, quotes or community counts. First-person lines run only over real people saying their own words, with consent.
  12. No AI speaks as God.No invented words for God or put in a holy figure's mouth. Quote real texts with their source, straight: no puns on scripture.
  13. Crisis care, every language.Danger or self-harm always points to 988 (US) or local emergency help.

  3. No faith person as a prop.Never use a believer as set dressing for a gag.
  4. No scripture riffs.Quote the teaching straight or don't quote it.
  5. No grinning.Glossy smiling models are stock photos. Dignity, not delight.

  7. No fake first-person testimony.First-person lines run only over real people saying their own true words, with consent.
  8. No tension, no board.Every image needs two forces pulling: hurt and grace, judgment and truth.
  9. No lime on lime, no lime on photos.Lime on black, or black on lime.
  10. No unbroken type.Break every line by hand; test at 375px and at 14x48 thumbnail. Rewrite the words; don't shrink the type.

the social playbook

tastefully playful, never mocking faith.

How infinite hill shows up online. Drawn from how Duolingo built Duo (Kat Chan, their senior director of brand marketing), and bent to fit a faith app. The short version: our mascot's personality comes from what he actually does in the app, we show up where people are already talking, and a few firm rules let the team move fast.

faith is serious. showing up every day can be fun.the creative platform · the line every post answers to

Duolingo's platform is "learning is hard, so we made it fun", and it's why their owl can get away with almost anything: he's pushy for a good cause. Ours does the same job. Every idea should pass one test: does it make showing up feel lighter without making the faith feel smaller?

the mascot's personality bible

Duo became "unhinged" because the app really did nag, and users turned that into a meme; Duolingo leaned into what was already true. Copying "mascot + chaos" without that root falls flat. Finn's truth is different: in the app he walks beside you, cheers when you finish, sits on a rock with you late at night, and holds a lantern for your friends. So that's who he is online.

he is

a good walking buddycheeky, never crueleasily delighteda bit extra about streakscurious about every traditionquiet when you're hurting

He'll do a dramatic slow-motion run to your door at 7:59 pm with a lantern. He'll wait on a rock for you in the rain, a little too patiently. He celebrates a 3-day streak like it's a wedding.

he never

threatens, even as a jokeguilt-trips about prayerwears religious dress as a costumedoes a tradition's gesture outside itpicks a sidejokes about grief

Duo's "violence for good" works for Spanish. It would read as spiritual abuse next to prayer. Our pushiness is warm persistence, never menace. (Namaste only on the Hindu path, the same rule as in the app.)

His name is Finn (in public copy only after the trademark check). He can also roast boring faith apps, never a faith, a believer or a named rival (see the voice rules).

where we show up: our lane

"Culture is the new marketing funnel": join conversations already happening, like a good party guest. Duolingo's lane is language and cultures (K-pop, K-dramas), and it stays out of elections. Ours is built in:

the religious calendar

Advent, Lent, Ramadan, Passover, Diwali, Vaisakhi, Vesak. The seasonal quests in the app are the same moments, so every post can point to something real to join.

faith in pop culture

Films and shows with faith at their heart, a celebrity talking about what they believe, the holiday movie everyone argues about. We add the story behind the word.

interfaith families

Two traditions at one holiday table, in-laws, kids asking big questions. The Table and the partner path make this ours.

the loneliest generation

Screen fatigue, the search for meaning, "I grew up with it and drifted". Our founding line lives here.

Off-limits: elections and party politics, wars and conflicts between faiths, scandals inside any religion, and "which religion is right" debates.

the rules: a floor, not a ceiling

Duolingo writes firm dos and don'ts for sensitive topics, then lets the team ship without sign-off on every post. We need that more than anyone.

alwaysnever
make fun of ourselves, the mascot and modern lifemake fun of a belief, a practice, a text or a holy person

how the team works

shortest path from idea to post

Small team of people who can think, write, edit and post. Speed matters most for reactive moments.

plan room for the unplanned

Leave space each month for moments nobody saw coming. Duolingo says its biggest hits were unplanned.

partnerships take years

Their Squid Game collab came after two or three years of friendly check-ins. Start warm relationships now with faith-film studios, youth groups, schools and the voices' teams.

hire for taste

Taste is harder to teach than execution. Judge it with a take-home ("10 post ideas for us") and a live riff, not interviews alone.

AI for the spark, humans for the idea

AI averages everything, and good ideas live off the average. Posts are written by people.

protect the team

Creativity needs trust. Look back at the body of work together; don't punish one post that missed.

short video: how it actually gets made

From Zaria Parvez, who started Duolingo's TikTok alone in 2021 and grew it past 8 million followers with almost no budget.

our real competitor is the feed

Not other faith apps. If people are scrolling, they aren't on the hill. That's also our founding line ("ours replaced it with a feed"), so we go where the feed is and bring the lesson there.

start with a real suit and small human moments

Duo's TikTok began with a costume sitting unused in a corner of the office. Get a mascot suit early. The unexpected moment (the mascot in a grocery line, at a youth group, trying to sit still) beats any polished ad.

the account is a sitcom

Every post is an episode; some go viral, some just build the storylines. Give the mascot a small recurring cast and running bits. New followers get dropped in the deep end: trust them to catch up.

the comment section is the brief

When people say what they want to see (or joke about who the mascot looks like), make that. Duo's "crush" on Dua Lipa came straight from comments.

start by commenting

Before we have videos, the mascot can comment on other people's posts about faith, holidays, grandparents and the loneliness of the feed. That's how Duolingo's account started.

steal like an artist

Use trending sounds and formats with our own spin, and credit the creator. Once people know us, move on to storylines of our own.

candy and medicine

The video is the fun part; the app shows up in the caption or a pinned comment. Not every post has to say what the app does.

the group chat test

If you didn't work here, would you send it to your group chat? Did you laugh making it? Show a teenager and someone from another faith before posting.

quality over a posting schedule

Nobody misses a brand account for two weeks. Burnt-out posting makes worse posts.

plan two weeks out, with a veto card

Keep a loose plan. Anyone can bump it for a moment that's happening today.

one owner per platform

About three-quarters of posts get reused across platforms; a quarter get rebuilt for each. Whoever lives on that platform decides.

keep it in-house and scrappy

Duolingo's agency content didn't hit; an employee who cared did. About 95% of their reach was free. Tight budgets make better ideas.

hire troublemakers

People who grew up between cultures are good at this, and for us especially: someone raised in an interfaith family, or bicultural and bilingual, already knows how to speak to two worlds at once. A stand-up comedian on the team is not a crazy idea.

measure it simply

About a million views counts as a hit. Ask new people "how did you hear about us?" in the app, and watch sign-ups the day a video lands. The real sign is when people talk about us in their group chats.

Zaria's biggest lesson from a mistake: a trend that reads as harmless to one group can read very differently to another. For us, that check is "would someone from this faith wince?"

from the feed to the app.

How we bring people from a scroll to a five-minute practice. The best teacher here isn't Duolingo; it's the faith accounts already winning the feed.

what @hindunuggets gets right

About 127,000 followers. AI-made, Pixar-style clips of child Krishna talking with a little girl. Every post starts from a question people actually feel: "why does God ignore my prayers?", "why does everyone else seem happy but me?", "why can't I let it go?". It earns money from books and a subscription. The lesson isn't the animation. It's the order: a feeling first, then the tradition's answer.

lead with a feeling. answer it in thirty seconds. leave a door open.the shape of every feed post
1 · the hook

a feeling, as a question

Something people type into a search bar at 1 am. Never a fact, never a holiday name on its own. "why can't I let it go?" beats "what the Gita says about attachment".

2 · the answer

real text, thirty seconds

Two or three lines from the tradition's own words, with the source on screen. No invented quotes, no "God says" in our own words.

one line into the app

"day 2 of this is in the app." "the practice takes 5 minutes." "a scholar of this tradition checked every word." Each one only once it's true.

four formats

the feeling-first series

One running question series per path, so each community gets its own feed. Same shape, its own texts and look.

15-second voice clips

A path's celebrity guide reads one line, only as their written agreement allows. Never say they're "signed".

Finn as narrator

Finn can guide any path because he belongs to none. He asks the question; the tradition answers it.

"one line to carry" cards

A still card with one line and its source. Made to be saved and sent, the same line the lesson ends with.

pictures: rules for each path

Showing God or a holy figure as a character is at home in Hindu storytelling. It is wrong for Islam and for many Jewish communities. One look can't fit eight doors, so each path has its own floor.

ten posts to start with

Proposal, not reviewed. Not to be posted until a Keeper for that path (or two reviewers, see when a post goes wrong) has checked the wording, the translation and the citation. Bridge lines only once they're true.

Christianity

"why am I so tired all the time?"

Jesus doesn't start with a task. He starts with an invitation: "come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."

Matthew 11:28, King James Version (1611).

bridge: "the practice takes 5 minutes."
Catholicism

"why does nothing feel like enough?"

Augustine felt it sixteen centuries ago: "thou madest us for Thyself, and our heart is restless, until it repose in Thee." The restlessness isn't a flaw. It's pointing somewhere.

Augustine, Confessions 1.1, tr. E. B. Pusey (1838).

bridge: "a scholar of this tradition checked every word."
Hinduism

"why does God ignore my prayers?"

Krishna says the size of the offering was never the point: "whoso shall offer Me in faith and love a leaf, a flower, a fruit, water poured forth, that offering I accept." What's asked for is the love in it.

Bhagavad Gita 9.26, tr. Edwin Arnold (1885).

bridge: "day 2 of this is in the app."
Hinduism

"why can't I let it go?"

The Gita's answer is to hold the work, not the result: "let right deeds be thy motive, not the fruit which comes from them." You can do your part and still put the outcome down.

Bhagavad Gita 2.47, tr. Edwin Arnold (1885).

bridge: "the practice takes 5 minutes."
Islam

"why does everyone else seem happy but me?"

The Qur'an says it twice, so you don't miss it: "lo! with hardship goeth ease, lo! with hardship goeth ease." Not after the hardship. With it.

Qur'an 94:5–6, tr. Marmaduke Pickthall (1930).

bridge: "a scholar of this tradition checked every word."
Judaism

"will this feeling ever end?"

The Psalms don't pretend the night is short: "weeping may tarry for the night, but joy cometh in the morning." Tarry means it stays a while. Morning still comes.

Psalm 30:6 (30:5 in Christian Bibles), JPS translation (1917).

bridge: "day 2 of this is in the app."
Buddhism

"why can't I let it go?"

The Buddha names the loop: replaying "he abused me, he beat me" keeps the anger alive. Then the way out: "hatred does not cease by hatred at any time: hatred ceases by love, this is an old rule."

Dhammapada 1.3–5, tr. F. Max Müller (1881).

bridge: "day 2 of this is in the app."
Sikhism

"why does everyone else seem happy but me?"

Guru Nanak's answer: "Nanak dukhia sabh sansar", the whole world is carrying pain. You're not the only one. The same line goes on to say who finds peace: the one whose support is the Name.

Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 954 (page to confirm), our plain rendering.

bridge: "the practice takes 5 minutes."
my own religion

"why can't I just sit still?"

Pascal saw it in 1670: "all the unhappiness of men arises from one single fact, that they cannot stay quietly in their own chamber." Sitting still is a skill. You can practice it.

Pascal, Pensées 139, tr. W. F. Trotter (1910).

bridge: "the practice takes 5 minutes."
my own religion

"why do I need a vacation from my own life?"

Marcus Aurelius: people "seek retreats for themselves, houses in the country, sea-shores, and mountains," but "it is in thy power whenever thou shalt choose to retire into thyself."

Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 4.3, tr. George Long (1862).

Quotes are from public-domain translations and have to be checked word for word against a scan of that edition before posting. Verse numbers differ between editions; the Keeper picks the one their community uses.

the social playbook · creators

creators as partners, not rivals.

Faith creators already have the audience and the trust. We have the path, the Keepers and the voices. Instead of competing with them for the feed, we give their followers somewhere to go next.

what they bring

An audience that already asks the big questions, and a voice people trust.

what we bring

A daily path, scholars who check every word, and celebrity guides. Their followers get a practice, not just another clip.

the offer, kept simple

a share of what they bring

An affiliate fee or revenue share for each paying member who joins through them. Only once paid plans are live; the number gets set with legal and accounting advice.

a co-branded path page

"Start the Gita path with [creator]": their name and a welcome on a landing page for their followers.

early access

New paths, seasonal quests and features before anyone else, so they have something fresh to show.

the social playbook · when it goes wrong

when a post goes wrong.

The escalation path we promised. Most posts never need it. The ones about a specific faith get checked before they go out, and if one still hurts someone, everyone knows what happens next.

before it posts: who checks

about one tradition

The Keeper for that tradition checks it. If the Keeper can't, two people check it, and at least one of them is from inside that tradition.

about ourselves

The mascot, streaks, modern life: the platform owner posts it. No sign-off needed; that's the point of the rules.

a heavy moment

A tragedy, a death, a conflict in the news: say nothing unless the owner says yes. Silence is usually right.

never posted, by anyone

never

mocking any faithguilt about prayerranking religionsAI voices or likenesses of real people beyond their written agreementpuns on scriptureromance or flirtingnaming a rival appanything like Duolingo's owl, green or wording

The last one covers homage and parody too: no owls in any color, no bright Duolingo green, no borrowed catchphrases (see the IP section of the Duolingo playbook).

the first hour, if a post offends

  1. Pull it. Take it down first, discuss after. Keep a private copy for the look-back.
  2. Say so, plainly. One short post or reply: "we got this wrong, and we've taken it down. we're sorry." No "if anyone was offended".
  3. Don't defend it. No arguing in the comments, no explaining the joke, no deleting critical comments.
  4. Tell the owner. Shaan hears about it within the hour, from us, before he hears it anywhere else. Tell the Keeper for that path too.

who decides

anyone can pull

Anyone on the team can take a post down without asking. Pulling a fine post costs little; leaving up a hurtful one costs a lot.

the Keeper decides on the faith

Whether the content was wrong about the tradition, and what a fix looks like. Their veto stands.

the owner decides the rest

Whether to say more, whether anything goes back up, and any change to the rules.

then a look-back, not a blame

Within two days: what happened, what the rule should have caught, and what changes. We protect the team; we fix the rule.

15 · faces

celebrity guides, keepers, and the guide.

the celebrity guides

A celebrity from each faith is the guide of that path: Denzel Washington for Christianity, Nicole Kidman for Catholicism, Priyanka Chopra for Hinduism, Natalie Portman for Judaism, Mahershala Ali for Islam, Orlando Bloom for Buddhism, Diljit Dosanjh for Sikhism, Justin Bieber for "my own religion". They're a huge draw and belong early in every film and deck.

How we say it: "celebrity guides", or "Denzel Washington for Christianity". Never "signed", "confirmed" or "under contract". Their AI voices and likenesses are used only as each person's written agreement allows. Never imply a creator or celebrity endorses anything they haven't.

the keepers

A scholar of each faith reviews every class, with a veto. Keepers are scholars (people who have spent their careers studying the texts, respected inside the tradition and out), not clergy chosen to serve one community; some are also ordained. Shaan recruits them directly.

How we say it: "a scholar of each faith reviews every class". Never "signed".

the guide

The in-app helper, in the mascot's voice. Stays inside your one faith (cross-faith only in "my own religion"). Warm, brief, answers from your own lessons, never preaches, never refuses someone sincere, and points to real people for deep questions. It works gracefully even with no AI connected.
