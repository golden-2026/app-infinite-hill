# Infinite Hill: project summary (as of November 6, 2026)

Written for importing into an AI assistant as background. Most of the direction below is the founder's current
thinking, not final decisions. Where something is decided, it says so.

---

## 1. In one paragraph

Infinite Hill is a mobile-first app for short daily religious and spiritual practice: about five minutes a day.
Pick one faith to go deep in (Hinduism, Buddhism, Christianity, Catholicism, Judaism, Islam, Sikhism), or build your own
faith ("my own religion"). Each day is a short, exciting lesson built on a real story from the tradition. It teaches
what the religion actually says and connects it to your life today. Celebrity guides and voices you know narrate it,
an AI guide personalizes it, and scholars of each faith check it. The goal is to make religion feel like yours again:
modern, culture-forward and fun, without watering down the faith.

- **Tagline (decided):** making religion yours again.
- **Big idea (decided):** step in to remember who you are. step out to live it.
- **Homepage headline (current):** because you gotta have faith.
- **Homepage line (current):** understand yourself. understand each other. pick a faith or build your own faith, with
  celebrity guides and voices you know.

## 3. Why it exists

- Every generation had a way through hard times. Many people walked away from that and replaced it with a feed. Now
  people feel divided and confused. Kids ask questions their parents can't answer, and people say prayers they don't
  understand.
- People haven't rejected learning about faith. They've rejected stale, rigid, text-heavy ways of doing it.
- Existing single-faith apps (Hallow, Bible Chat and others) serve a purpose, but they're rigid. They offer a fixed
  path, little sense of culture or cool, no celebrity pull, weak community, and no curated journey that adapts to you.
- Culture is already moving: kirtan and bhajan artists fill stadiums with mixed crowds, bhajan nights happen in clubs,
  and faith creators go viral on Instagram and TikTok. Religion as living culture is having a moment. Infinite Hill
  wants to be at the front of it.

## 4. Who it's for

- **Brand target:** Gen Z and younger millennials. They're open-minded and the most likely to make it spread.
- **Marketing order:** Christian first, since the largest group of viewers is Christian. Every faith is shown warmly.
  Don't lead the marketing with Hindu festival imagery.
- **First real testers (alpha):** Hindu parents. The first goal is one excellent week of lessons before anything else.
- **Lead persona (a hypothesis to test):** the family.
  - Partners who grew up in different religions.
  - Parents with two traditions at home deciding what to teach the kids.
  - Parents whose kids ask questions they can't answer.
- **Five personas:**
  - The parent.
  - The person in a hard moment (grief, health news, feeling low).
  - The two-faith couple (about 4 in 10 new marriages cross religious lines).
  - The returner (grew up with a faith and wants it to mean more).
  - The searcher (doubting, spiritual but not religious, curious).
- **Sixteen reasons people come**, shown on the homepage as "which one sounds like you?". Examples: "my partner and
  i grew up in different religions," "our kids ask questions about our religion and we don't have the answers," grief,
  a diagnosis, "i want my own faith to mean more."

## 5. The offer: one faith first

- Eight doors: Hinduism, Buddhism, Christianity, Catholicism, Judaism, Islam, Sikhism, and "my own religion" (once
  called Simply Spiritual). In copy we say "build your own faith."
- **One faith first (decided Nov 4, 2026).** The app, the films and the site show one person going deeper in one faith.
  It must never feel like a comparative-religion tour.
- Exploring other faiths and their common ground is opt-in. It mainly lives inside "my own religion," which has to be
  incredible and carefully curated.
- The deeper mission is unity: "one human race, one hill," a little more love and a little less fighting. We honor
  real differences, never rank faiths and never push conversion.
- **Open to every view.** The founder is personally drawn to a non-dual view, but the product stays open to every
  faith and every school within it and never tilts toward his personal view.

## 6. What exists today

### The app (live as a private test, behind a login)

- Built in Expo (React Native), exported to the web. Four tabs: Today, Guide, Together, You.
- **Five-year curriculum:** daily lesson scripts for all eight doors, about 14,000 lessons in total. Years 1–3 are
  live for all doors, and Hindu years 1–4 have had a full quality pass. Each year goes live only after repeated review
  passes and the founder's okay.
- **Lesson recipe:** a real story from the tradition, then the religion's actual teaching, then modern life. No
  generic self-help tips. Early lessons are light and fun, with at most one famous text name a day; scholarly depth
  comes in later years.
- **Placement:** you can start anywhere in the five years. The app gently moves you back or forward if you were placed
  wrong. A full adaptive placement test (a question bank, understanding rather than word-matching) is planned, with
  Hinduism as the pilot.
- A wellbeing check-in (WHO-5).
- A gentle lane for hard moments: grief, health news, struggling, forgiveness.
- Circles (small groups) and a kids track.
- **Streaks Duolingo-style, but kind:** rest days, and you can earn a broken streak back.
- **AI Guide and companion:**
  - Answers questions in the mascot's warm voice and helps seekers build a path. It never refuses a sincere question.
  - It handles hard texts, hostility and venting with care.
  - It falls back gracefully when no AI is available.
- **Crisis care in every language:** danger or self-harm points to 988 (US) or local emergency help.
- **Mascot:** Finn (name chosen Nov 3, 2026; a trademark check is pending), wearing a backwards cap. Only the approved
  art is ever used. Finn is warm first, says "i," and dances on right answers and streaks. "We" means only the company.

### The "hill" prototype (test link, not live)

- The guide asks "what's on your mind today?": angry at someone I love, a worry, I feel lost, a good day, or your own
  words.
- It answers warmly and picks one story from your faith.
- A globe flight takes you to a real place tied to the story (for example, the Sea of Galilee for the lost son).
- You play the story there, and a passport stamp drops in. 50 places are earnable, each with custom stamp art.
- Opt-in only: "every faith has a story about this. want to hear another?"
- Also: a "rough moment?" flow, an Advent calendar, a shareable "my week on the hill" card, and missed questions that
  come back the next day.

### The website (live as a private test)

- A homepage plus about 18 footer pages in English and Spanish: about, privacy, safety, science, voices, scholars,
  foundation, careers and more.
- A real-research "why bother" section, for example Pew 2019 data on happiness and religious participation.
- Eight real user quotes.

## 7. The new direction (November 2026; current ideas)

### Lessons become episodes

- Every daily lesson becomes a five-minute, cinematic episode. The bar is hero quality for every episode, not cheap
  daily filler.
- Celebrities narrate all episodes, and different celebrities can take different stories. Invited-celebrity pitch:
  "it's for the kids." (Celebrity voices are AI versions, used only with permission.)
- The founder picked episode style "A," called "the night out": loud, bold, music-driven and catchy, the kind of thing
  kids would get into.
- The engine (episode player, games, interactions) stays the same. The lessons change.

### What one full episode contains (built as a demo for Hindu year 1, day 7, on karma)

1. Recap of the words learned so far this week.
2. A cold-open hook ("a robber asked his family one question. the answer changed him.").
3. Six short narrated film clips telling the story (here: Valmiki, the robber who became the poet of the Ramayana).
4. A quick game: what does the story teach?
5. Teaching cards with the real source (Bhagavad Gita 3.5; Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 4.4.5: "as one acts, so one
   becomes").
6. Three layers: what you thought it meant, what it means, what a scholar hears.
7. Match the pairs.
8. Myth or fact ("karma means the universe paying you back": myth).
9. Rebuild the old line from pieces.
10. A friend's text message. Priya snapped at her grandmother and asks "is something bad gonna happen to me now?" You
    pick the best reply.
11. Three breaths (a calm moment, always optional).
12. Word of the day in its own script, with pronunciation (कर्म, KAR-muh).
13. A closing film moment, a cliffhanger for tomorrow and a shareable end card ("you are what you do.").

### Demo episodes built so far

- One per faith in style A, plus the full day 7 lesson:
  - Hindu: Prahlad.
  - Christian: Jonah.
  - Catholic: Our Lady of Guadalupe.
  - Jewish: Jacob wrestling the angel.
  - Muslim: the story of the two brothers, told with no people shown.
  - Sikh: Panja Sahib.
  - Buddhist: Angulimala.
  - My own religion: "maybe," the farmer parable.
- About 230 stories already have films; converting them into episodes is the next possible step. Hindu films are the
  only ones narrated so far.

### Brand

- Win on brand, relevance, being cool and being in tune with the culture. Reference points: Kendrick Lamar for
  cultural pull, and Magna (a loud, lime-and-black drinks brand) for a bold, short, confident homepage.
- **Look:** night navy, brand yellow and lime, and loud type. No rainbow gradients (they can read the wrong way to
  religious audiences). No gimmicky stats. Not restrained, not arthouse.
- **Taste test:** big, loud, music, live crowds, real shock-and-awe wow, viral pop. Packaging is modern, the faith is
  real: real bhajans, cool everyday people.
- **Copy:** punchy and current, never corny, never flat. Finn can roast stale, boring faith apps, but never a faith,
  its people or a named rival.
- A short new homepage exists as a draft: full-screen video hero, headline, the faith buttons, "find your path" and
  "build my own".

## 8. Ideas being explored (not decided)

### From the build partner (Xogito), Nov 6 call

- AI agent at the core: a live, personal path that adapts to what you're into (music, scripture, philosophy) instead
  of one fixed track. Hallow's fixed prayer path is the contrast.
- A TikTok funnel: an Infinite Hill account posts short AI film series by religion and pulls viewers into the app to
  go deeper.
- Community as a major layer, not yet defined: communities of seekers who sometimes meet in real life.
- Moat: taste/brand, curation and community. Building software is now cheap, so taste is what's left.
- Look at Yam, a YC-backed AI romance game where you're the main character and AI characters remember you and react
  to your choices. It shows how "you're in it" drives engagement.
- Open tension: the partner's example jumps across faiths ("you like Hindu music, here's the Sufi parallel"). The
  founder's current rule keeps you inside one faith, with cross-faith only in "my own religion."

### Inspired by Yam (Claude's suggestions)

- **Step in:** you're inside the sacred story as an ordinary person, like the fisherman on the boat or a kid in the
  crowd. Holy figures' words stay fixed and scholar-checked; your choices are about you.
- **Step out:** a recurring cast of everyday people (Priya, a cousin, a roommate, a grandmother) who remember what you
  told them and whose lives move over five years. Each lesson is how you show up for them.
- **Story texts instead of reminders:** "party or nani's? help." from Priya on a Friday night.
- **Choices come back:** what you told Priya on day 3 shows up on day 7.
- **"My own religion" builds an episode around your life:** you type what's going on, and the guide assembles a story
  from your faith plus a crew scene, from approved pieces only.

### Other ideas on the table

- Places on the hill: hundreds of real places per faith, all on one infinite hill (home), each one a lesson; bigger
  places are earned.
- Passport stamps for every trip.
- A celebrity guide for each faith's home on the hill. You talk to the guide out loud.
- Real-life connection: a breathing moment in your parked car, suggestions for a real church, temple or event near you,
  check-ins afterward. Off-app gatherings 18+, never pushed.
- A "hard moment" way in: the app you reach for after a blowup.

## 9. Moat

- Taste and brand: how it looks, sounds and feels. A generic AI build can't copy it.
- Curation: hand-picked stories, places and paths, checked by scholars.
- Celebrities: voices and guides people already know.
- Community: faith is fundamentally about people together.
- Scholar trust: a scholar of each faith (called Keepers, a group still in formation) checks content and can say no.
- Not a language course, and not a clone of Duolingo: Duolingo is the model for habit and fun, not the content.

## 10. Rules and guardrails (decided)

- Every lesson is a real story, then the faith's actual teaching, then modern life. No self-help filler.
- Prayer is always a choice: watch, learn or join in. "Pray" can feel intimidating, so say it's your choice.
- AI never speaks as God, and AI never plays Jesus, prophets or Gurus.
- No faces of Jesus, prophets or Sikh Gurus. No people in Islamic story scenes.
- Religious content is published only after review. No "draft" labels in user-facing copy.
- Never invent testimonials or community numbers. Show real counts only above a threshold.
- Never imply that celebrities or scholars endorse something they haven't. Frame plans as plans.
- Never ask users to sing or hold a mic.
- Never tell users the hill "never ends." Progress lines celebrate.
- Crisis care always points to 988 (US) or local emergency help.
- Privacy: no logging of journal text, Guide questions, chosen faith or practice history. Server keys never go in
  browser code.
- Say "celebrity guides," not "famous guides."
- The mascot's voice is warm and compassionate first, never cold.

## 12. Competition and references

- **Faith apps:** Hallow (Catholic and Christian prayer; polished but "not cool enough"), Bible Chat, Calm and other
  meditation apps.
- **Model for habits and fun:** Duolingo (streaks, short lessons, a mascot with personality).
- **Model for "you're in it":** Yam (AI romance simulation games, YC). Its users say they spend hours a day. For us,
  5–10 loved minutes a day is the win.
- **Model for brand:** Magna (drinkmagna.com), loud and confident. A different category, so borrowing freely is fine.
- **Culture signals:** viral faith creators, stadium kirtan, bhajan clubbing, and AI short-film series on TikTok.
  Nobody does short AI series for religion yet.
