# CAMP 2 · THE STORIES (Days 22–96)
*The ten Gurus as one serial, told in order. The sakhis of Guru Nanak's travels run as shorts inside it. Where the janamsakhis differ, we say "tradition says".*

## Guru Nanak · the early years (Days 22–31)
22. **Born at Talwandi, 1469** · — · Nanak is born to Mehta Kalu and Mata Tripta in a Punjab village now called Nankana Sahib · think of the place you were born · *every story starts somewhere small*
23. **The sister who saw first** · — · his older sister, Bebe Nanaki, is remembered as the first to recognize who her brother was · thank someone who believed in you early · *someone saw you first*
24. **The boy and the teacher** · — · tradition says young Nanak asked his village teacher what the letters really pointed to · ask one real question today · *ask what it means*
25. **The cobra's shade** · — · tradition says that as he slept in the fields a cobra spread its hood to shade his face · rest in shade for two minutes · *something shelters you*
26. **The true bargain** · sacha sauda · sent with twenty rupees to strike a good deal, he feeds hungry holy men and comes home empty-handed · spend something small on someone hungry · *the best bargain*
27. **The storehouse at Sultanpur** · modikhana · weighing out grain as a storekeeper, he reaches thirteen, tera, which also means yours, and keeps saying it · count to thirteen slowly · *tera, tera, all yours*
28. **Three days in the river** · — · he walks into the river Bein to bathe and is not seen for three days · sit by running water, even a tap · *some come back changed*
29. **The first words after** · — · he returns and says there is no Hindu and no Muslim, and the town asks what he means · notice one label you put on people · *beneath the labels, people*
30. **Praying with the qazi** · — · tradition says that at prayer he told the qazi his mind had wandered home to a young horse · give one prayer your whole mind · *where is your mind right now*
31. **Mardana and the rebab** · rebab · a Muslim musician, Bhai Mardana, becomes his lifelong companion; Mardana plays and Nanak sings · listen to one piece of music all the way through · *one plays, one sings*

## Guru Nanak · the travels, as shorts (Days 32–40)
*The udasis: four great journeys, remembered east, south, north and west. Each sakhi is a short.*
32. **Setting out** · udasi · Nanak leaves home for years of travel on foot, with Mardana beside him · walk somewhere new today · *go and see*
33. **Bhai Lalo's bread** · — · at Saidpur he eats the coarse bread of Lalo the carpenter and turns down the rich Malik Bhago's feast · eat one plain meal gratefully · *honest bread is sweet*
34. **Milk and blood** · — · tradition says he squeezed Lalo's bread and milk ran out, and from Bhago's feast, blood · think where one thing you own came from · *how was it earned*
35. **Sajjan's rest house** · — · a man who robbed the travelers who slept in his inn hears Nanak sing and gives it all up · name one thing you'd like to put down · *even sajjan turned*
36. **Water to the west** · — · at Hardwar, seeing pilgrims throw water toward the rising sun, he throws his westward, to water his fields · question one habit, gently · *why do we do this*
37. **Feet toward the Kaaba** · — · tradition says that at Mecca, told his feet pointed at God's house, he asked them to turn his feet where God is not · face four directions, slowly · *which way is god not*
38. **The hand on the rock** · — · tradition says that at Hasan Abdal a boulder rolled down at him stopped at his open palm · put your palm on a wall, steady · *a steady hand*
39. **The yogis on the mountain** · sidh · Himalayan yogis ask why he stays in the world; he answers that you live in it without being stained · do one ordinary chore with care · *in the world, unstained*
40. **Babur comes** · — · Nanak sees the cruelty of Babur's invasion at Saidpur and sings a lament that names the suffering (Asa, ang 360) · name one suffering you've looked away from · *he did not look away*

## Guru Nanak · Kartarpur and the successor (Days 41–43)
41. **The farmer at Kartarpur** · Kartarpur · after the travels he settles by the Ravi, farms the land, and the first Sikh community gathers round him · work with your hands for five minutes · *he came home and farmed*
42. **The tests of Lehna** · — · tradition says a disciple named Lehna carried a bundle of wet grass and did every task the Guru asked without complaint · do one task without a word of complaint · *just do it well*
43. **The sheet of flowers, 1539** · — · tradition says that when Nanak died, Hindus and Muslims both claimed his body, and under the sheet they found only flowers · place a flower somewhere today · *flowers for everyone*

## Guru Angad (Days 44–47)
44. **Lehna becomes Angad** · ang · Nanak names Lehna his successor and calls him Angad, part of my own body · think who carries your work on · *passing the light*
45. **The Guru's script** · — · at Khadur, Guru Angad shapes and teaches the Gurmukhi letters so ordinary people can read the hymns · write your name slowly by hand · *letters for everyone*
46. **Mata Khivi's kitchen** · — · Guru Angad's wife runs the langar and is remembered for serving rich rice pudding to all · give someone a sweet thing · *the kitchen is a teacher*
47. **The wrestling ground** · akhara · Guru Angad encourages exercise and wrestling so body and spirit grow strong together · move your body for three minutes · *the body counts too*

## Guru Amar Das (Days 48–53)
48. **An old man hears a hymn** · — · past sixty, Amar Das hears Guru Angad's daughter Bibi Amro singing Nanak's words at dawn, and goes to find the Guru · listen closely to one song · *it's never too late*
49. **The water carrier** · — · for years he carries water for Guru Angad before dawn; tradition says he stumbled by a weaver's peg and was mocked as homeless · do one humble job faithfully · *faithful in small things*
50. **The well of eighty-four steps** · baoli · at Goindval, Guru Amar Das builds a step-well open to every caste · drink a glass of water slowly · *water for all*
51. **First the row, then the audience** · — · anyone who comes to see the Guru eats first in langar; tradition says the emperor Akbar sat on the floor too · eat once with no one at the head · *first the row*
52. **The preachers, women and men** · manji · he sends out preachers to lead sangats, women among them, and speaks against the burning of widows · honor one woman who taught you · *she is not lesser*
53. **The song of bliss** · Anand · tradition links his great hymn, the Anand Sahib, to joy at a grandson's birth; it is sung at every ceremony · sing or hum something joyful · *bliss, mother, bliss*

## Guru Ram Das (Days 54–57)
54. **Jetha the orphan** · — · an orphaned boy selling boiled grams at Lahore comes to Goindval, serves, and marries Bibi Bhani · offer a snack to someone · *small beginnings*
55. **The pool of nectar** · Amritsar · Guru Ram Das begins the pool and the town that will be named Amritsar, the pool of nectar · look at still water for a minute · *a town around a pool*
56. **The four rounds** · lavan · he writes the four lavan, the wedding hymns that Sikh couples still walk around the Granth to (Suhi, ang 773) · think of a promise you keep · *four rounds, one life*
57. **The youngest son** · — · passing over his elder sons, Guru Ram Das names his youngest, Arjan, as the fifth Guru · notice someone quietly ready · *the quiet one was ready*

## Guru Arjan (Days 58–64)
58. **Letters from Lahore** · — · sent away as a young man, Arjan writes longing letters to his father in verse (Majh, ang 96) · write one line to someone you miss · *my mind longs*
59. **A jealous brother** · — · his elder brother Prithi Chand resents him and makes trouble for years · sit with a family tension, no fixing · *pray for the difficult one*
60. **The house with four doors** · Harmandir Sahib · Guru Arjan builds the Harmandir Sahib low in the pool with doors on four sides, open to all · open a door and leave it open · *open on every side*
61. **Gathering the hymns** · — · with Bhai Gurdas as scribe, he gathers the Gurus' hymns and those of other saints into one book · gather one thing worth keeping · *gather what is true*
62. **The Adi Granth installed, 1604** · Adi Granth · the book is placed in the Harmandir and Baba Buddha reads the first hukam · open any book and read one line · *the word has a home*
63. **Arrest at Lahore** · — · summoned by the emperor Jahangir's order, Guru Arjan is imprisoned and tortured in the heat of Lahore · sit in stillness for one minute · *steady in the heat*
64. **The first martyr, 1606** · shaheed · tradition says he sat through it repeating that the Lord's will is sweet; Sikhs remember him as the first martyr · think of someone who stayed kind under pressure · *your will is sweet*

## Guru Hargobind (Days 65–68)
65. **Two swords** · miri piri · the young sixth Guru wears two swords, one for worldly duty and one for the spirit · stand tall for thirty seconds · *two swords, one heart*
66. **The throne of the timeless** · Akal Takht · he raises a platform facing the Harmandir, where the community meets on worldly matters · look up at the sky, then down · *the timeless throne*
67. **The cloak with fifty-two tassels** · — · released from Gwalior fort, tradition says he left only if fifty-two imprisoned princes could come, holding his cloak · help someone else get free · *no one freed alone*
68. **Home to the hills** · — · after battles with Mughal forces, Guru Hargobind settles at Kiratpur and spends his last years quietly · rest after a hard week · *even warriors rest*

## Guru Har Rai (Days 69–71)
69. **The flower and the robe** · — · tradition says the boy Har Rai brushed a flower with his robe, broke it, and was told to walk gently all his life · walk gently for one minute · *walk gently*
70. **The herb garden** · — · Guru Har Rai keeps a garden of medicines and sends a cure even to the emperor's son Dara Shikoh · care for one plant · *heal whoever asks*
71. **The changed word** · — · his son Ram Rai alters a word of Nanak's hymn to please the court, and the Guru refuses to see him again · keep one word you promised · *don't change the word*

## Guru Har Krishan (Days 72–73)
72. **A Guru at five** · — · the youngest Guru takes up the task as a small child and is remembered for his calm · do one thing calmly · *small, and wise*
73. **Smallpox in Delhi** · — · he nurses the sick during an epidemic in Delhi and dies of it at eight, where Gurdwara Bangla Sahib stands · check on someone unwell · *he served the sick*

## Guru Tegh Bahadur (Days 74–80)
74. **The name of the sword** · — · Tyag Mal, youngest son of Guru Hargobind, fights so bravely he is called Tegh Bahadur, brave of the sword · think where you were brave · *brave, then quiet*
75. **Twenty-two claimants at Bakala** · — · tradition says the trader Makhan Shah found the true Guru meditating in Bakala among many false claimants · look past the loudest voice · *the guru is found*
76. **Journeys east and a new town** · — · Guru Tegh Bahadur travels as far as Assam and founds the town that becomes Anandpur · plant or start one small thing · *build where you are*
77. **The pandits of Kashmir** · — · Kashmiri Brahmins facing forced conversion come to him for help · listen to someone asking for help · *they came to him*
78. **Three companions** · — · Bhai Mati Das, Bhai Sati Das and Bhai Dayala are killed in Delhi for refusing to give up their faith · light a candle for the steadfast · *they did not bend*
79. **Chandni Chowk, 1675** · — · Guru Tegh Bahadur is beheaded in Delhi for defending others' freedom to practice their faith · pray for someone else's freedom · *for another's faith*
80. **Carrying the head home** · — · Bhai Jaita carries the Guru's head to Anandpur, and Lakhi Shah burns his own house to cremate the body · do a brave, quiet kindness · *bring him home*

## Guru Gobind Singh (Days 81–93)
81. **A child at Patna** · — · Gobind Rai is born in Patna in 1666 and grows up playing at archery by the Ganges · remember a game you loved · *the boy by the river*
82. **Who is worthier than you** · — · tradition says the nine-year-old told his father no one was worthier to answer the Kashmiris' plea · encourage someone who faces something hard · *who better than you*
83. **A court of poets at Anandpur** · — · the young Guru gathers poets and scholars, writes himself, and fights at Bhangani in 1688 · read one line of poetry · *pen and sword*
84. **Vaisakhi, 1699: the call** · — · before a great crowd the Guru draws his sword and asks who will give their head · sit with what you'd give everything for · *who will give*
85. **The five beloved** · Panj Pyare · Daya Ram, Dharam Das, Himmat Rai, Mohkam Chand and Sahib Chand step forward, from different castes and regions · say the five names slowly · *daya, dharam, himmat, mohkam, sahib*
86. **Sugar in the iron bowl** · amrit · the Guru stirs water with a double-edged blade while reciting, and tradition says Mata Jito added sugar crystals · add something sweet to plain water · *strength and sweetness*
87. **The Guru becomes the disciple** · Khalsa · he asks the five to initiate him too; men take the name Singh and women Kaur · bow to someone you teach · *wonderful the guru, wonderful the disciple*
88. **Leaving Anandpur** · — · after a long siege and broken promises of safe passage, the family is scattered crossing the flooded Sarsa river · hold someone you love in mind · *scattered, not lost*
89. **The stand at Chamkaur** · — · the Guru's elder sons, Ajit Singh and Jujhar Singh, die fighting at Chamkaur, aged about eighteen and fourteen · sit quietly for the elder sons · *they stood*
90. **The wall at Sirhind** · Sahibzade · the younger sons Zorawar Singh and Fateh Singh, nine and six, are executed for refusing to convert; their grandmother Mata Gujri dies too · hold one minute of silence · *the youngest were the bravest*
91. **The forty who came back** · Chali Mukte · tradition says forty who had deserted returned with Mai Bhago and died at Khidrana; the Guru tore up their letter of leaving · welcome someone back · *you can always come back*
92. **The letter of victory** · Zafarnama · from the wilderness the Guru writes to the emperor Aurangzeb in Persian, rebuking broken oaths · write one honest sentence · *victory is the truth*
93. **Nanded, 1708: the Guru is the Granth** · Guru Granth Sahib · before he dies the Guru tells the Sikhs that the scripture is now their Guru · open the Granth, or a page of it · *the book is the guru now*

## Shorts from the Gurus' sangat (Days 94–96)
94. **Water for the enemy** · — · in battle, Bhai Kanhaiya gave water to wounded soldiers of both sides, and the Guru told him to add ointment too · help someone you disagree with · *every face is his*
95. **The needle for the next world** · — · tradition says Nanak gave rich Duni Chand a needle to return to him in the next world, and Duni Chand understood · look at one thing you own · *what goes with you*
96. **Camp 2 close: one light, ten lamps** · jot · Sikhs speak of one light passing through all ten Gurus, and now resting in the Granth and the Khalsa · light a lamp, look at the flame · *one light, ten lamps*

# CAMP 3 · THE PRACTICES (Days 97–156)
*How-to. The hook is short and the practice is the session. Each week adds something to a small daily rhythm. Every practice is an invitation.*

## Seva (Days 97–103)
97. **Service without an audience** · nishkam seva · seva is service done with no reward in mind, and it is meant to change the one who serves · do one unseen kindness · *no one needs to know*
98. **Start with the shoes** · — · in many gurdwaras one honored seva is minding people's shoes · tidy the shoes by your door · *start at the shoes*
99. **Seva of the hands** · — · washing dishes, sweeping, cooking: the body's seva · wash one dish slowly · *hands can pray*
100. **Seva of the purse** · daswandh · many Sikhs set aside a tenth of what they earn for the common good · work out what a tenth would be · *a tenth, for others*
101. **Seva of the mind** · — · teaching, listening and sharing knowledge kindly are seva too · teach someone one small thing · *give what you know*
102. **When seva feeds the ego** · haumai · serving to be seen feeds the very ego seva is meant to thin · notice wanting credit, and smile · *serve, then forget it*
103. **Your weekly seva** · — · choose one small regular service you can actually keep · pick one weekly seva · *one seva, kept*

## Kirtan · listening first (Days 104–108)
104. **Just listening** · kirtan · kirtan is the singing of the Guru's hymns, and the first practice is simply to listen · listen to one shabad · *just listen*
105. **The mood of a raga** · raag · each hymn of the Granth is set in a raga, a musical mood for the words · notice the mood of one song · *music carries meaning*
106. **Instruments in the room** · — · harmonium and tabla today, and older strings like the rebab and saranda · listen for each instrument · *every sound has a part*
107. **The pause line** · rahao · rahao means pause; it marks the refrain that holds a hymn's center · find the rahao line in a shabad · *pause here*
108. **Listening with the words** · — · follow the Gurmukhi and its meaning as the hymn is sung · read along once · *sound and sense together*

## Kirtan · then singing (Days 109–113)
109. **Humming first** · — · hum quietly with a recording before trying the words · hum for two minutes · *hum first*
110. **Singing Waheguru** · — · the simplest kirtan is singing Waheguru to a simple tune · sing it five times · *the name, sung*
111. **One line** · — · learn one short refrain and sing only that · sing one line · *one line is enough*
112. **Singing back** · sangat · in the gurdwara the sangat sings the refrain back to the singers · sing along once · *voices together*
113. **Kirtan at home** · — · many families sing a shabad in the evening or before sleep · sing one line before sleep · *the home fills up*

## Langar (Days 114–119)
114. **Why the floor** · pangat · in langar people sit in rows on the floor, everyone level, whoever they are · eat one meal sitting low · *same row*
115. **The kitchen door stays open** · — · langar is free to anyone of any background, in many gurdwaras all day · notice who near you could use a meal · *the door stays open*
116. **Rolling rotis** · — · kneading and rolling bread in the langar is seva with dough · make or buy bread to share · *round, warm, shared*
117. **A meal everyone can eat** · — · langar food is kept vegetarian so that anyone can share it · cook one simple vegetarian meal · *everyone can eat this*
118. **Cupped hands** · — · servers walk the rows and people receive with open, cupped hands · receive something with both hands · *receive fully*
119. **A small langar at home** · — · invite someone and feed them, with no agenda · feed one person · *a small langar*

## Simran on the breath (Days 120–127)
120. **Remembrance with breath** · naam simran · simran is remembering the Name; here we let the breath carry it · breathe slowly for two minutes · *breath and name*
121. **Wahe in, guru out** · — · many sound Wahe on the in-breath and Guru on the out-breath · ten breaths this way · *wahe in, guru out*
122. **Satnam on the breath** · — · Sat on the inhale, Nam on the exhale · ten breaths this way · *true name, breathing*
123. **When the mind runs off** · — · the hymns often call the mind restless; the practice is only to come back · return three times, kindly · *come back*
124. **The early hours** · amrit vela · the ambrosial hours before dawn, praised in Japji's fourth pauri · wake five minutes earlier · *the early hour*
125. **A string of beads** · mala · some keep count of simran on a string of beads · count twenty-seven names · *bead by bead*
126. **Simran while working** · — · the Name can run quietly underneath ordinary work · do simran while walking · *hands at work, heart at home*
127. **Your simran** · — · settle on a time and a length that fits your life · choose your five minutes · *your five minutes*

## Nitnem, plainly (Days 128–137)
128. **The daily banis** · Nitnem · Nitnem means daily practice: set prayers at dawn, at dusk and at night · learn the three times · *morning, evening, night*
129. **Japji at dawn** · — · Guru Nanak's Japji is the first morning bani · listen to Japji's opening · *begin with japji*
130. **Jaap Sahib** · — · Guru Gobind Singh's rolling praise of the Timeless, in quick changing meters · listen for two minutes · *the rhythm of praise*
131. **Tav Prasad Savaiye** · savaiya · ten verses saying that kings, armies and rituals are nothing without love · listen to one savaiya · *without love, nothing*
132. **Chaupai Sahib** · — · a prayer asking to be kept under the Guru's protecting hand · read the opening lines · *keep me*
133. **Anand Sahib** · — · Guru Amar Das's song of bliss, part of the morning or evening prayers · listen to its first pauri · *joy, found*
134. **Rehras at dusk** · Rehras · the evening prayer, said as the day's work ends · stop at dusk for two minutes · *put the day down*
135. **Kirtan Sohila at night** · Sohila · five short hymns said before sleep · read the first before bed · *the night song*
136. **How long it takes** · — · the full Nitnem takes an hour or more, and traditions differ slightly on which banis are included · pick one bani to begin with · *start with one*
137. **Your Nitnem, for now** · — · a starter Nitnem is an invitation, not a test · choose one morning bani · *one bani, daily*

## The gurdwara visit (Days 138–144)
138. **Before you go** · — · anyone is welcome; cover your head, take off your shoes, bring no tobacco or alcohol · find your nearest gurdwara · *everyone is welcome*
139. **The flag outside** · Nishan Sahib · a tall saffron flag bearing the khanda marks a gurdwara from far away · look for one · *the flag says come in*
140. **Bowing to the word** · matha tekna · people bow before the Guru Granth Sahib and may leave an offering · bow once, wherever you are · *bow to the word*
141. **The canopy and the whisk** · chaur · the Granth sits under a canopy while an attendant waves a whisk over it, as for royalty · notice how you treat a precious book · *the word is honored*
142. **Karah prashad** · karah prashad · a warm sweet of flour, butter and sugar is given to everyone into cupped hands · receive something sweet · *sweet, shared, equal*
143. **The order of the day** · hukamnama · the day's verse is read from wherever the Granth falls open at the morning service · read today's hukamnama · *today's word*
144. **Sitting in the sangat** · — · you can stay for kirtan, eat langar, and leave quietly whenever you wish · plan a visit · *sit, eat, go in peace*

## The Sikh year (Days 145–155)
*These fire on their date when the calendar allows; otherwise they are taught in this order.*
145. **Vaisakhi: the harvest** · Vaisakhi · a spring harvest day in Punjab that became the birthday of the Khalsa in 1699 · notice what is growing now · *harvest and birth*
146. **Vaisakhi: the procession** · nagar kirtan · the Granth is carried through the streets, led by five Sikhs in saffron · watch one, in person or online · *the song walks outside*
147. **Guru Nanak's gurpurab** · gurpurab · the full moon of Katak marks his birth, with lamps, kirtan and langar · light a lamp for him · *the light that came*
148. **The unbroken reading** · akhand path · the whole Granth read aloud without a break, taking about forty-eight hours · listen for ten minutes · *unbroken*
149. **Guru Gobind Singh's gurpurab** · — · his birth is celebrated in winter with kirtan and processions · read one line of his · *sword and song*
150. **Guru Arjan's martyrdom day** · chhabeel · in the June heat Sikhs hand out cold sweetened water to passers-by · give someone a cold drink · *cool water for anyone*
151. **Bandi Chhor Divas** · Bandi Chhor · lamps are lit to remember Guru Hargobind's release from Gwalior, at Diwali time · light a lamp for someone freed · *light for the freed*
152. **Hola Mohalla** · Hola Mohalla · at Anandpur, the day after Holi, mock battles, gatka and poetry fill the fields · move your body for three minutes · *strength, in joy*
153. **Maghi at Muktsar** · Maghi · the fair at Muktsar in January remembers the Forty Liberated · think of someone who came back · *come back, anyway*
154. **Shaheedi Jor Mela** · — · in December Sikhs gather at Fatehgarh Sahib to remember the younger Sahibzade · sit quietly for the children · *small, and unbending*
155. **Guru Tegh Bahadur's martyrdom day** · Hind di Chadar · remembered each year, he is often called the shield of India · pray for another's freedom of faith · *a shield for others*
156. **Camp 3 close: your rhythm** · — · seva, kirtan, langar, simran, a bani and a visit: now assemble a week · write your weekly rhythm · *you can do this*

# CAMP 4 · THE TEXT — JAPJI SAHIB (Days 157–231)
*Guru Nanak's Japji, verse by verse. Each pauri: first the sound (listen, say the opening Gurmukhi aloud), then the meaning (Macauliffe's translation, paraphrased). Sound first, meaning second.*

## Before the first pauri (Days 157–162)
157. **The Guru's alphabet** · Gurmukhi · Gurmukhi, from the Guru's mouth, has thirty-five letters and a set of vowel signs · trace Ik Onkar in the air · *read with the mouth first*
158. **Sound before meaning** · — · Japji is meant to be heard; we will listen to each pauri before we learn what it says · listen to the whole Japji once · *listen before you understand*
159. **The Mool Mantar, sounded** · — · Ik Onkar, Satnam, Karta Purakh, Nirbhau, Nirvair, Akal Murat, Ajuni, Saibhang, Gur Prasad · say it slowly aloud · *sound it out*
160. **The title word** · jap · after the Mool Mantar comes one word, jap: recite, repeat, remember · whisper the word three times · *repeat, remember*
161. **Aad sach: the sound** · — · aad sach, jugaad sach, hai bhi sach, Nanak hosi bhi sach · say it four times aloud · *aad sach*
162. **Aad sach: the meaning** · sach · true in the beginning, true through the ages, true now, and true hereafter, says Nanak · name one true thing in you · *true then, true now*

## Pauris 1–4 (Days 163–170)
163. **Pauri 1, sound: sochai soch** · — · sochai soch na hovaee je sochee lakh vaar · say the first line aloud · *sochai soch*
164. **Pauri 1, meaning: the wall of falsehood** · hukam · thinking a hundred thousand times cannot reach Him; the wall of falsehood falls by walking in His order (Japji 1) · stop overthinking one thing · *walk in the order*
165. **Pauri 2, sound: hukmee hovan** · — · hukmee hovan aakaar, hukam na kahiaa jaaee · say the first line aloud · *hukmee*
166. **Pauri 2, meaning: all within the order** · haumai · all forms, high and low, joy and pain, come by the order; whoever understands it no longer says I, I (Japji 2) · notice one "I" today · *all within the order*
167. **Pauri 3, sound: gaavai ko taan** · — · gaavai ko taan hovai kisai taan · say the first line aloud · *gaavai ko*

314. **Sawmills of British Columbia** · — · in the early 1900s Sikh men worked sawmills and railways on Canada's west coast · thank a worker you rarely see · *honest work, far away*
315. **Stockton, 1912** · — · Sikh farmworkers open the first gurdwara in the United States in California · find a gurdwara in your region · *a door in a new land*
316. **The Komagata Maru, 1914** · — · a ship of mostly Sikh passengers is turned away at Vancouver and returns to violence in India · welcome one newcomer · *they were turned away*
317. **Bhagat Singh Thind, 1923** · — · a Sikh army veteran is denied US citizenship by the Supreme Court · stand up for someone's belonging · *he belonged*
318. **Britain after 1947** · — · Sikhs settle in Southall, Birmingham and the mill towns, building gurdwaras in old houses · look at a building with new eyes · *a house became a gurdwara*
319. **The turban in court** · — · Sikh families win cases in several countries to wear the turban at school and work · respect someone's head covering · *the right to be yourself*
320. **After September 2001** · — · Balbir Singh Sodhi, a Sikh gas station owner in Arizona, is killed in a hate crime · learn one fact to counter a stereotype · *know your neighbor*
321. **Oak Creek, 2012** · — · six worshipers are killed at a Wisconsin gurdwara, and the sangat answers with prayer and open doors · pray for a grieving community · *the doors stayed open*
322. **Langar where disaster strikes** · — · Sikh volunteers cook free meals after floods, earthquakes and wars around the world · give to one relief effort · *the kitchen travels*
323. **The second generation** · — · children grow up with Punjabi at home, other languages outside, and Gurmukhi classes at weekends · say one word in another language · *two worlds, one heart*
324. **Kirtan in new places** · — · hymns now sound in gurdwaras on every continent, in old ragas and new voices · listen to kirtan from far away · *the song everywhere*
325. **No stranger anywhere** · — · no one is my enemy and no one a stranger; I get along with all (Kanara, ang 1299) · greet a stranger warmly · *no one is a stranger*

## The big lookout (Days 326–331)
326. **Looking back: the stories** · — · ten Gurus, the sakhis, the Khalsa's birth: which story stayed with you · retell one story aloud · *which story stayed*
327. **Looking back: the practices** · — · seva, kirtan, langar, simran, Nitnem and the gurdwara: which one you kept · do your kept practice · *the practice that stuck*
328. **Looking back: the Japji** · — · thirty-eight pauris heard and understood, one by one · read your favorite pauri · *i read it*
329. **Looking back: the depths** · — · themes, Bhagats, rehat, history and grief carried carefully · sit with what was hard · *deep water, crossed*
330. **The good of all** · — · the year ends where every Ardas ends, asking good for everyone · wish good for three people · *for everyone, not just us*
331. **The big lookout** · — · year one is done; ahead lie the ranges: the daily banis, the long story, the ways of the Guru, the Granth itself · look out a window for a minute · *wow*
