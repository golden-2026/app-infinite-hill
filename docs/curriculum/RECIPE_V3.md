# Lesson recipe v3: from "good" to "can't wait for tomorrow"

Owner, 2026-10-13: "I'm concerned our content is just okay and not amazing… a key piece is being trend right and
culture forward so people don't get bored." Year 5 paused at 834 of 2,920 days to adopt this recipe first.

**Evidence.** Deep study of Hallow/Fr. Mike, YouVersion/BibleProject, Sikh and Hindu apps and creators, Muslim and
Jewish apps and creators, Duolingo/Headspace craft, and faith culture now (2025-26); then three blind judges (a
24-year-old seeker, a parent of two, a game designer) scored 23 sessions, 8 of ours unlabeled. Ours averaged 7.7 depth
vs 4.8, 7.0 come-back vs 5.9; our "manifesting, honestly" lesson ranked first of 23. But all three judges spotted our
eight as one product and named the same five weaknesses. Reports: the session scratchpad `deep/` folder (judge-1..3,
craft, culture, hallow, youversion-bibleproject, sikh-hindu, muslim-jewish, ours).

**What stays.** One idea a day, real sources, the claims rule (SCRIPT_GUIDE §9b), the voice (§3), honesty about what
the sources don't say, warm small practices, ~5 minutes, word and carry from the outline, the validator.

---

## The five changes

### 1. Start from their world, then bring in the old wisdom as the answer
The two lessons judges liked best both opened on something the listener does or sees this week (manifesting; "marketing
guru, fitness guru"). Lessons that opened on a verse or a history date read "like a classroom".
- The hook opens with a real, current thing: a feed, a group chat, a habit, a worry, a trend, a song, a fight at
  dinner, an app, a job, a date. Then the tradition arrives as the surprising answer or the older version of it.
- The teach can still be story-led; it just doesn't open cold on scripture or a date.
- Culture, yes; cringe, no: no slang rewrites of scripture, no memes about God, no "fellow kids" voice, no brand names
  that will date in a month, no politics or culture-war topics.
- Titles may name a feeling or a situation ("when you can't forgive someone") as well as a word.

### 2. A weekly rhythm instead of one template
Every day of every path ran the same nine beats at the same lengths; by week 3 it was predictable. Each day now has a
**shape** (new field `"shape"`), on a weekly rotation (day number mod 7), so the frame stays stable and the inside
changes:

| shape | what makes it different | the beat that leads |
|---|---|---|
| `story` | one scene told like an episode, ends on its cliffhanger answered by the teach | hook = a scene, in the middle of it |
| `puzzle` | opens on something odd in the text ("why does this verse say 25 twice?") and solves it | hook = the strange detail |
| `debate` | two real views inside the tradition; the learner states the other side's best case (steelman) | the fork |
| `senses` | a smell, sound, taste, object or movement carries the idea; the practice is physical | the practice |
| `life` | a situation from this week (a fight, a loss, a win, a choice) and what the tradition gives you for it | the chat |
| `wisdom` | one question, the tradition's answer, plus (optionally) how one neighbor tradition says it, ranking none | the teach |
| `review` | no new word: the last six carries come back as a game round, plus the friend's storyline | the games |

The app plays the shape (order and lead game), not the writer. Writers write all fields as now; `shape` tells them
where to spend the words. Lengths per beat may move ±50% inside the 430-640 total.

### 3. One friend per path who remembers you
The chat asker changed role every day (neighbor, classmate, roommate, mom), so nobody became attached. Now each path's
cast friend (cast.ts: Priya, Grace, Sofia, Noa, Amira, Simran, Mai, Zoe) is the one who
texts, every day, with:
- **one trait** (Priya: dramatic, funny, asks "wait, why?"; set per path in `docs/curriculum/CAST_VOICES.md`, to write);
- **memory**: the chat picks up yesterday ("ok so after the Hillel thing i tried it on my brother…");
- **a small storyline per camp** (21 days): a trip, an exam, a fight with a sibling, a wedding coming, a first fast;
  the lessons quietly help them through it. The older relative (Nani, Abuela, Saba, Dadi…) appears once a week.
- `games.chat.who` becomes the friend's key; the app shows their face. Never clergy, never a real person, text only.

### 4. Games that test using it, not remembering it
Games re-asked the same 3-4 facts from a minute earlier, and the right answer was usually the obviously kind one.
- The fork and the chat are **new situations** where today's idea applies ("your two aunts disagree about the wedding
  date — what would the house of Hillel do first?"), not "what did the lesson say".
- Wrong options are what someone who skimmed would pick (a half-truth, the popular version, the opposite school's
  view), never the obviously unkind answer.
- Each lesson's myth includes one item the learner most likely believes now (the popular misreading).
- Wrong answers come back: the app re-asks a missed item at the end of the lesson and on the next `review` day.
- At most 5 game screens per lesson (was up to 8). Cut the ones that only re-check the carry.

### 5. End on a question tomorrow answers, and show the collection growing
"Tomorrow: daily bread" is a topic title, not a reason to come back.
- `tomorrow` becomes a **question or an unfinished line** (≤ 70 characters): "so why did he wash their feet first?",
  "Priya's about to meet the in-laws. what does she say?". The next day's hook must answer it.
- The close shows what you gained today in the collection (a word card, a line, a saint, a name of God, a story card),
  with the count for the camp ("7 of 21").

## Smaller rules (the tics the judges heard)
- Banned or rationed openers and closers: "Your line:" (rotate with 5 alternatives), "Today's word.", "Four turns.
  First…", hooks ending "Today: X", "If you pray…; if not, just…" (once a week at most), "The bell holds the time."
- Practices rotate across a list of 12 kinds; the same kind at most twice in 7 days: write a line by hand, say it out
  loud, smell/taste, a 60-second walk, text someone, do one small act, look at one object, a body posture, listen to
  one sound, a gratitude count, a plan (wish-obstacle-plan), eyes-closed stillness. Eyes-closed at most twice a week.
- A spelled-out giant day number ("DAY ONE THOUSAND FOUR HUNDRED…") reads as guilt in later years: the bell shows the
  camp and day-in-camp ("camp 64 · day 3 of 21") once past day 100.
- One surprising, repeatable fact per lesson ("something you'd tell a friend tonight"), always sourced.
- The meaning of every sacred or foreign word on screen (gloss), always.

## Culture-forward, safely
- Seasonal arcs: when a lesson falls near a festival on its path (from public/daily dated cards), the friend's
  storyline can touch it; the lesson content stays on the outline.
- Music and sound: a call-and-response or sing-along beat is welcome where the tradition has one (kirtan, a psalm
  refrain, a dhikr phrase, a Shabbat song), described, never remixing sacred text.
- Never AI images of deities, prophets or Gurus; hand-drawn cast art only (existing rule).
- Never claim a "revival" or invent numbers; never imply the voices or Keepers are signed.

## Pilot results and fixes (2026-10-13/14)
Three blind judges preferred v3 on both pilot paths, 6 of 6 (averages 8.0 vs 5.7 fresh, ~7.6 vs 6.0 come-back,
6.3 vs 4.2 "still opening it in week 6"). Their fixes, now rules:
- Every day ends on a question, including the review day (a teaser into next week).
- No gimmicks: no jokes that undercut a sacred text, no odd practices (e.g. standing in a bathroom); the practice must
  not fight the breathing timer.
- The debate day lets the two views speak; don't lecture about how to debate.
- The friend's storyline paces believably across days (no overnight turnarounds); see CAST_VOICES.md.
- Hard scenes (a beheading, a massacre, a death) get one gentle line for parents in the hook or review.notes.
- Don't test the same point twice in one lesson.

## Story, teaching, today (owner, 2026-10-15: the rule above all the others)

Every lesson, every path, every year, climbs three steps:

1. **The story, told properly.** A real story from the tradition, told so it's worth hearing: who, what happened, what
   was at stake. A serial is fine, but each day's part must make sense on its own and land something.
2. **The teaching.** What this religion actually teaches or does because of the story: a belief, a practice, a
   holiday, a prayer, a reason families do what they do. Name it plainly ("this is why Hindus start everything with
   Ganesha"). Keep "teachers read it as…" readings labeled as readings.
3. **Today.** How that teaching meets a life now, in a way people can relate to.

The test: **could only this religion's story have taught today's line?** If a self-help app could say it ("take
space for yourself", "the tool can break, the work doesn't have to"), rewrite it. The carry line, the games, the fork
and the friend's chat test the teaching (step 2), never just the life tip. The friend's chat works best as a real moment
of the religion (a friend asks why your family does something; a ritual at the wedding), answered with what you learned.

Example of the failure (Hindu day 22, pilot): the birth of Ganesha became "some things need a guard", and Priya's chat
was about locking her door to practice a speech. The fix keeps the story and lands on a teaching: the god made from
earth by the Goddess is why families shape Ganesha from clay each year, invite him in, and give him back to the water.

## The tie-back (owner, 2026-10-15: "I didn't see the tie back clearly laid out")

The friend's chat only works if the reader sees why a scripture story answers a texting question. After the answer,
the app shows three labeled lines, written in `games.chat.tie`:

- `story`: what happened in the scripture story, one sentence, naming who did what ("Parvati set Ganesha at her door…").
- `friend`: what it means for the friend, by name, in their situation ("Priya can guard an hour to practice…").
- `you`: optional; one short question for the reader's own week. Not shown for now (owner, 2026-10-15: the card read as too busy).

Keep `meaning` too (older screens use it). Lessons without `tie` fall back to splitting `meaning` into
"in the story" and "in real life". The pilot week (days 22–28, Hinduism and Catholicism) has it; year 5 and the
rewrite of years 1–4 add it.

## The unity thread (owner, 2026-10-14: "more alike than we think", one human race)
- Once a week (usually the `wisdom` day), name one thing this path shares with a neighbor tradition (light, giving,
  gratitude, welcoming the stranger, the golden rule, fasting, prayer before meals), in that tradition's own words and
  source, then one honest difference. Never "they're all the same", never ranking, never one tradition's idea dressed as
  everyone's (e.g. "all religions teach you are God" is not neutral).
- About once a month the friend's arc meets the best friend's family or tradition (CAST_VOICES.md).
- The three core families (homepage, 2026-10-14) stay in view: partners of different religions; parents unsure what to
  teach; kids asking questions parents can't answer. When a lesson's idea is one a child would ask about, the chat or
  the carry can give a parent a sentence they could say at dinner.
