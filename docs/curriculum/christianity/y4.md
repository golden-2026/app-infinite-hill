# YEAR FOUR — "many rooms" (Days 1062–1426)
*The schools of thought and the traditions of prayer, explained without ranking. Where Christians disagree, we say who holds what and why, and leave the choosing to the reader and their church.*

## Block A · The big questions, and how traditions answer them (Days 1062–1111)
1062. **Talking about God** · theology · theology means words about God; every tradition does it, and every tradition says words fall short · write one sentence about God · *words fall short, and help*
1063. **Where authority comes from** · sola scriptura · Protestants say scripture alone is the final authority; Catholics and Orthodox read scripture within the church's living tradition · notice who you trust and why · *where do you stand?*
1064. **Four sides** · — · Methodists speak of scripture, tradition, reason and experience working together · check one belief against all four · *four sides of a question*
1065. **How the Bible was gathered** · — · the books were recognised over centuries by use in worship; lists settled around the fourth century · look at one early list of books · *the church read these*
1066. **What inspired means** · inspiration · all-scripture-is-God-breathed (2 Timothy 3:16) is read by some as word-for-word, by others as God working through human writers · read the verse · *god-breathed*
1067. **Four senses** · — · medieval readers found four layers: literal, allegorical, moral, and where it leads · read one story in all four ways · *layers of meaning*
1068. **The way of not-knowing** · apophatic · Orthodox and mystics say what God is not, since God is beyond every idea · say what God is not · *more than you think*
1069. **All-powerful, all-loving** · — · classic attributes: God is almighty, all-knowing, everywhere, and love · think which one comforts you most · *god is love first*
1070. **Pictures of the Trinity** · — · the sun, its light and heat; a lover, a beloved, and love: every picture helps and falls short · draw three circles overlapping · *every picture falls short*
1071. **The filioque** · filioque · Western churches added and the Son to the creed about the Spirit; the Orthodox did not, and the argument continues gently · say the creed both ways · *one word, long argument*
1072. **The divine dance** · perichoresis · Greek fathers described the three persons as dwelling in one another, like a round dance · move in a circle, slowly · *god is a dance*
1073. **Out of nothing** · — · creation out of nothing: the world isn't God and isn't an accident, but a gift · hold something and call it a gift · *from nothing, for love*
1074. **Creation and evolution** · — · many Christians accept evolution as God's way of creating; others read Genesis more literally · look at an old rock or fossil · *god of all time*
1075. **Image and likeness** · — · Orthodox teachers say we have God's image and grow into his likeness · grow a little into likeness today · *image given, likeness grown*
1076. **The first sin** · original sin · Augustine taught that Adam's guilt is inherited; the Orthodox speak of inheriting mortality and a wounded nature, not guilt · notice the pull toward wrong in you · *wounded, not worthless*
1077. **Augustine and Pelagius** · — · can we be good without grace? Augustine said no; Pelagius was judged to say yes · ask for grace for one task · *grace first*
1078. **Predestination** · — · Calvin and the Reformed stress God's sovereign choosing; they find comfort in it · rest in being held · *held by his choosing*
1079. **Free grace for all** · prevenient grace · Arminius and Wesley stressed grace that goes before, freeing everyone to respond · respond to one nudge of good · *grace goes before*
1080. **Why God became human** · — · Athanasius: he became human that we might become godlike; Anselm asked why a God-man was needed · sit with the question why · *he became what we are*
1081. **One person, two natures** · — · Chalcedon: without confusion, change, division or separation · hold two truths at once · *both, not either*
1082. **Victory over death** · Christus Victor · many early fathers saw the cross as Christ defeating death and evil · name one thing he defeated for you · *death is defeated*
1083. **Satisfaction** · — · Anselm pictured the cross as Christ restoring God's honour on our behalf · think of a debt paid for you · *he paid what we owed*
1084. **Substitution** · — · the Reformers stressed Christ bearing the penalty in our place (Isaiah 53:5) · thank God for bearing what you couldn't · *in my place*
1085. **Love's example** · moral influence · Abelard stressed the cross as love so great it changes us · let love change one reaction · *love that changes us*
1086. **Recapitulation** · — · Irenaeus: Christ lives every stage of human life again, rightly, to heal it · pray for your own stage of life · *every age healed*
1087. **A real body raised** · — · the churches hold the resurrection was bodily, not only a feeling · touch your own hands · *a body, not a ghost*
1088. **Justified by faith** · justification · Protestants stress being declared right with God by faith in Christ · receive one thing as a gift · *declared right*
1089. **Becoming like God** · theosis · the Orthodox speak of salvation as union with God, like iron glowing in fire · sit near a warm light · *iron in the fire*
1090. **Grace and cooperation** · — · Catholics stress grace that heals and lifts, and our free cooperation with it · cooperate with one grace today · *grace and yes*
1091. **Can you know?** · assurance · some traditions stress confident assurance of salvation; others a humble hope · rest in trust, not worry · *trust, not fear*
1092. **Growing holy** · sanctification · all agree grace grows us; Wesley even spoke of being made perfect in love · grow one small habit of love · *perfect in love*
1093. **Gifts of the Spirit (1 Corinthians 12)** · charism · wisdom, knowledge, faith, healing, tongues: one Spirit, many gifts, one body · name a gift in someone else · *many gifts, one spirit*
1094. **The fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22–23)** · — · love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control · pick one fruit for today · *fruit, not effort*
1095. **Tongues, then and now** · — · Pentecostals and charismatics pray in tongues; some Christians believe such gifts ceased, others are open · stay open and humble · *the spirit blows*
1096. **Four marks** · — · the church is one, holy, catholic and apostolic, each tradition explaining how · pray for one mark in your church · *one, holy, catholic, apostolic*
1097. **Who decides?** · — · a pope, councils of bishops, synods, or congregations: churches order authority differently · pray for your church's leaders · *order for love*
1098. **Ministers** · ordination · bishops, priests, deacons, pastors, elders; churches differ on ordaining women, and many do · thank a minister · *called to serve*
1099. **Priesthood of all believers** · — · Luther stressed that every baptised Christian has a priestly calling (1 Peter 2:9) · do your work as a calling · *a royal priesthood*
1100. **Baptism, deeper** · — · some say baptism brings new birth; others that it signs a birth already happened · remember your baptism or your beginning · *born of water*
1101. **The Real Presence** · transubstantiation · Catholics teach bread and wine become Christ's body and blood; the Orthodox affirm the change and call it mystery · bow to a mystery · *a holy mystery*
1102. **Other views of the table** · — · Lutherans: Christ truly present in, with and under; Calvin: spiritual feeding; Zwingli: memorial · share bread thoughtfully · *do this in memory*
1103. **Mary across the churches** · Theotokos · most churches call Mary God-bearer; Catholics and Orthodox honour her highly; many Protestants honour her as a model disciple · read the Magnificat · *all generations call her blessed*
1104. **Praying with the saints** · intercession · Catholics and Orthodox ask the saints to pray for them; most Protestants pray to God alone · thank God for a saint · *the cloud of witnesses*
1105. **Images** · — · Orthodox honour icons; Catholics statues; Reformed churches keep walls plain from the second commandment · notice images in your prayer place · *what helps you pray*
1106. **Purgatory and the dead** · purgatory · Catholics teach purification after death and pray for the dead; the Orthodox pray for the dead without that word; most Protestants pray differently · pray for someone who has died · *mercy after death*
1107. **Heaven** · — · less clouds and harps, more being with God and all things made new · picture joy without end · *with him*
1108. **Hell** · — · traditions teach eternal separation; some hold that the lost cease to be; some hope all may be saved · pray for everyone · *his mercy is wide*
1109. **He will come again** · — · all churches say Christ will return; they differ on how and when · live today as if he might · *come, lord jesus*
1110. **In essentials, unity** · — · an old motto: in essentials unity, in non-essentials liberty, in all things charity · be charitable with one disagreement · *in all things, charity*
1111. **Block close: many rooms** · — · the family disagrees, and still says one creed · pray for someone who believes differently · *many rooms, one house*
