# golden — CHRISTIANITY · YEAR ONE · CAMPS 2–5
*The broad Christian path: Protestant, Orthodox and Catholic Christians are all welcome here, and so is anyone curious. Where traditions differ, we say who says what and rank no one. The Catholic path goes deeper into the Mass, rosary and sacraments.*

# CAMP 2 · THE STORIES (Days 22–96)
*The hook becomes a story; the practice stays two minutes; one word every two or three days.*

## Weeks 4–5 · The parables as shorts (Days 22–36)
22. **Why he told stories** · parable · Jesus rarely explained himself; he told a short story and let it sit with people (Mark 4:33–34) · retell a story you love in one sentence · *a story carries what a lecture can't*
23. **The sower (Matthew 13:1–9)** · — · seed lands on path, rock, thorns and good soil; most is wasted, and the harvest is huge anyway · notice which kind of ground you are today · *keep throwing seed*
24. **The mustard seed and the yeast (Matthew 13:31–33)** · leaven · the smallest seed becomes a bush for birds; a pinch of yeast lifts a mountain of dough · do one very small good thing quietly · *small is how it starts*
25. **The lost sheep (Luke 15:3–7)** · — · a shepherd leaves ninety-nine in the open to go after one · message someone who has drifted away · *one is worth the walk*
26. **The lost coin (Luke 15:8–10)** · — · a woman lights a lamp, sweeps the whole house for one coin, then throws a party · celebrate one small thing you found again · *throw the party*
27. **The treasure and the pearl (Matthew 13:44–46)** · — · a man finds treasure in a field and, in joy, sells everything to buy it · name what you'd give everything for · *in joy, not duty*
28. **Workers in the vineyard (Matthew 20:1–16)** · — · those hired at the last hour get a full day's pay, and the early ones grumble · notice one place you're keeping score · *generosity isn't fair. it's better.*
29. **The talents (Matthew 25:14–30)** · talent · two servants risk what they were given; the third buries his out of fear · use one gift you've been hiding · *fear buries things*
30. **The rich fool (Luke 12:16–21)** · — · a man plans bigger barns for his surplus and dies that night · give away one thing you're only storing · *you can't take the barn*
31. **The unforgiving servant (Matthew 18:21–35)** · — · forgiven a fortune, he throttles a man who owes him pocket change · remember one debt you were let off · *pass the mercy on*
32. **The Pharisee and the tax collector (Luke 18:9–14)** · the tax collector's prayer · one prays about his own virtues; the other stands far off and asks only for mercy · pray one completely honest sentence · *honesty is the whole prayer*
33. **The persistent widow (Luke 18:1–8)** · — · a widow wears down an unjust judge simply by never stopping · ask again for something you gave up asking for · *keep knocking*
34. **The great banquet (Luke 14:15–24)** · — · the invited guests make excuses, so the host fills the hall with the poor and the lame · invite someone who's rarely invited · *there's room at the table*
35. **The ten bridesmaids (Matthew 25:1–13)** · — · five bring spare oil for a groom who comes late; five run out · prepare one small thing for tomorrow · *bring a little extra oil*
36. **The sheep and the goats (Matthew 25:31–46)** · — · the king says: when you fed the hungry and visited the prisoner, you did it to me · feed, visit or clothe one person this week · *he wears ordinary faces*

38. **Two pregnant women (Luke 1:39–45)** · — · Mary hurries to her cousin Elizabeth, and the baby in Elizabeth's womb leaps · visit or call someone who'll understand · *joy knows joy*

41. **No room (Luke 2:1–7)** · manger · a census, a long road, and a baby laid in an animal's feeding trough · make room for one unexpected thing · *God arrived in a feeding trough*
42. **The shepherds (Luke 2:8–20)** · — · the first news goes to night-shift workers on a hillside; Mary keeps it all in her heart · keep one moment today, just for yourself · *ponder it in your heart*
43. **Simeon and Anna (Luke 2:22–38)** · — · two old people who waited their whole lives recognize the baby in the temple · honor an elder who has waited long · *waiting is not wasted*

45. **The flight into Egypt (Matthew 2:13–23)** · — · the holy family become refugees, fleeing a king's violence by night · pray for one refugee family · *he was a refugee first*

72. **Thomas (John 20:24–29)** · — · the one who wanted proof gets invited to touch the wounds · say your doubt out loud, gently · *doubt is allowed in the room*

82. **Peter and Cornelius (Acts 10)** · Gentile · a rooftop vision tells Peter no one is unclean, and he walks into a Roman's house · enter a door you've avoided · *call no one common*

85. **Paul in Athens (Acts 17:16–34)** · — · he quotes their own poets: in him we live and move and have our being · find one good thing in a stranger's view · *in him we live*
86. **The shipwreck (Acts 27–28)** · — · two weeks of storm, Paul breaks bread on deck, 276 swim to Malta, and a viper bites harmlessly · eat something steadying in a hard week · *take heart*
87. **The letters as letters** · epistle · Paul's letters are real post: greetings, travel plans, and a list of friends' names (Romans 16) · write one real letter or card · *names matter*
88. **Philemon** · — · a short note asking a slave owner to receive Onesimus as a beloved brother · speak up for someone with less power · *no longer a servant, but a brother*
89. **Love is patient (1 Corinthians 13)** · agape · written to a quarrelling church, not a wedding: love is patient, kind, not puffed up · be patient with one difficult person · *the greatest of these*

90. **What a saint is** · saint · Paul calls every believer a saint; Catholics and Orthodox also honor holy people named by the church · name one person whose goodness helped you · *ordinary people, made holy*

# CAMP 3 · THE PRACTICES (Days 97–156)
*How Christians actually live it day to day. Where traditions practice differently, we say so.*

## Weeks 15–16 · Ways to pray (Days 97–108)
97. **What prayer is** · prayer · less about getting words right, more like talking and listening to someone who's already there · say one sentence, then stay quiet one minute · *talk, then listen*
98. **A simple pattern** · ACTS · many Protestant churches teach adoration, confession, thanksgiving, supplication as a shape for prayer · pray one sentence of each · *a shape helps*
99. **The Jesus Prayer** · — · Orthodox Christians repeat Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, with the breath · say it ten times, slowly, with your breath · *have mercy on me*
100. **Praying the Psalms (Psalm 23)** · psalter · for three thousand years people have prayed with borrowed words · read Psalm 23 aloud, slowly · *the lord is my shepherd*
101. **Lectio divina** · lectio divina · an old monastic way: read a short passage, notice a word, ponder it, rest · read one verse four times · *one word is enough*
102. **The examen** · examen · Ignatius taught a nightly look back: where was God today, where did I miss him? · review today in two minutes · *where was he today?*
103. **Arrow prayers (Nehemiah 2:4)** · — · before the king answers, Nehemiah fires a silent prayer upward · pray three seconds before your next conversation · *short prayers fly*
104. **Praying for others by name** · intercession · Christians pray for people by name, trusting God hears · name three people and pray for each · *hold them up*
105. **How long, O Lord? (Psalm 13)** · lament · the Bible gives you permission to complain to God · write one honest complaint to God · *complaint is a prayer too*
106. **A window of paint** · icon · Orthodox Christians pray before icons, windows onto heaven, not the thing worshiped · look at an icon or holy image one minute · *a window, not a wall*
107. **The daily office** · collect · Anglicans pray the Book of Common Prayer morning and evening, with short set prayers called collects · pray the evening collect for aid against perils · *lighten our darkness*
108. **In your own words** · — · many evangelical and Pentecostal Christians pray freely and aloud, like talking to a friend · pray out loud for one minute · *just talk*

## Weeks 17–18 · The circle of the year (Days 109–120)
109. **Time as a circle** · liturgical year · many churches walk the life of Christ every year, with colors and seasons · find today on a church calendar · *time can be holy*
110. **Advent: waiting** · Advent · four weeks of candles and longing before Christmas · light a candle and wait in silence · *come, lord*
111. **Christmas: twelve days** · — · Christmas is a season, not a day, running to 6 January · keep celebrating something after the day · *god with us*
112. **Epiphany: the light spreads** · Epiphany · the magi, the baptism, the wedding at Cana: God shown to the world · notice one thing revealed today · *the light is for everyone*
113. **Ash Wednesday: dust** · — · ashes on the forehead and the words: remember you are dust (Genesis 3:19) · touch soil or ash, and remember · *dust, and loved*
114. **Lent: forty days** · Lent · forty days of prayer, fasting and giving, echoing Jesus in the desert · choose one small Lenten thing · *less, so there's room*
115. **Palm Sunday and Holy Week** · — · branches in, a cross by Friday; the week the whole year turns on · walk one slow route today, remembering · *the week everything turns*
116. **Maundy Thursday** · maundy · from mandatum, commandment: love one another, and many churches wash feet · wash someone's dishes · *love one another*
117. **Good Friday** · — · churches strip altars, keep silence, and venerate the cross · keep three minutes of silence at noon · *it is finished*
118. **Holy Saturday** · — · the quiet day; in the old vigil a new fire is lit in the dark · sit in the dark, then light one light · *light in the dark*
119. **Easter is fifty days** · Pascha · Christ is risen! He is risen indeed! Orthodox Pascha often falls on a later date, by the old calendar · greet someone with good news · *he is risen indeed*
120. **Pentecost and ordinary time** · ordinary time · after Pentecost comes the long green season, when faith gets lived in the ordinary · do one ordinary task as prayer · *the ordinary is holy*

## Week 19 · Sabbath and scripture (Days 121–130)
121. **God rested (Genesis 2:1–3)** · — · on the seventh day God stopped and blessed the stopping · stop completely for five minutes · *rest is blessed*
122. **Why Sunday** · Lord's Day · most Christians gather on Sunday, the day of the resurrection; Seventh-day Adventists keep Saturday · plan one restful hour this week · *the first day, a new start*
123. **Made for you (Mark 2:27)** · — · the sabbath was made for people, not people for the sabbath · rest without guilt for ten minutes · *rest is a gift, not a test*
124. **A small sabbath** · — · a phone off, a walk, a meal with no hurry: sabbath can start small · put your phone away for one hour · *stop. it still counts.*
125. **A library, not a book** · canon · Protestants have 66 books, Catholics 73, Orthodox a few more · look at a Bible's contents page · *many voices, one story*
126. **Two testaments** · testament · Christians read the Hebrew scriptures as the first part of their own story · read Psalm 1 · *the story began long before*
127. **A chapter a day** · — · most Bible-reading habits are small: one chapter, same time, every day · choose a time and a place for reading · *small and daily wins*
128. **A lamp for my feet (Psalm 119:105)** · — · people memorize a verse to carry it in the dark · learn this verse by heart · *a lamp unto my feet*
129. **Reading slowly** · — · reading for information and reading for formation are different speeds · read five verses twice as slowly · *slow down to hear*
130. **Tyndale's plow** · translation · Tyndale died so a ploughboy could read the Bible in English; many translations followed, KJV and Douay-Rheims among them · thank someone who taught you to read · *words cost someone something*

## Week 20 · Giving and singing (Days 131–139)
131. **The tithe (Malachi 3:10)** · tithe · many Christians give a tenth; others give differently, but most give regularly · decide one regular gift, however small · *give first, not last*
132. **Two small coins (Luke 21:1–4)** · — · a widow drops in almost nothing, and Jesus says she gave the most · give something that costs you · *it's not the amount*
133. **Give in secret (Matthew 6:1–4)** · alms · don't let your left hand know what your right hand is doing · give anonymously this week · *let it stay secret*
134. **Angels unawares (Hebrews 13:2)** · hospitality · welcome strangers; some have hosted angels without knowing · offer a guest something to drink · *strangers might be angels*
135. **The church sings (Colossians 3:16)** · — · the first Christians sang psalms, hymns and spiritual songs · sing or hum one line of a hymn · *who sings prays twice*
136. **Watts and Wesley** · hymn · two Englishmen wrote thousands of hymns still sung, like When I Survey and And Can It Be · read a hymn verse as a poem · *love so amazing*
137. **The spirituals** · spiritual · enslaved Africans in America made songs of Moses, Jordan and freedom that carried hope and plans · listen to one spiritual · *let my people go*
138. **Holy, holy, holy** · Trisagion · Orthodox churches sing Holy God, Holy Mighty, Holy Immortal, have mercy on us · say the Trisagion three times · *holy, holy, holy*
139. **Gospel, Taizé and worship songs** · — · from Black gospel choirs to Taizé's repeated chants to modern worship bands · listen to one piece of church music · *every style can pray*

## Week 21 · Together and alone (Days 140–150)
140. **Fellowship (Acts 2:42)** · koinonia · the first believers devoted themselves to teaching, fellowship, bread and prayers · share a meal with someone · *you weren't meant to do it alone*
141. **Small groups** · — · Wesley's class meetings asked one question: how is it with your soul? · ask someone how they really are · *how is it with your soul?*
142. **Bear one another's burdens (Galatians 6:2)** · — · carrying each other's loads is how the law of Christ is kept · help carry one load today · *carry each other*
143. **He withdrew (Luke 5:16)** · — · even at his busiest, Jesus slipped away to lonely places to pray · find five minutes alone · *withdraw to return*
144. **The quiet time** · — · many Christians keep a morning quiet time: a Bible, a notebook, a coffee · sit with a notebook for three minutes · *first thing, a little quiet*
145. **Be still (Psalm 46:10)** · — · be still, and know that I am God · sit still for two minutes · *be still, and know*
146. **Why fast (Matthew 6:16–18)** · fasting · Jesus says when you fast, not if: quietly, without a gloomy face · skip one small comfort today · *hunger can pray*
147. **Fasting across the church** · — · Orthodox Christians fast most Wednesdays and Fridays; Catholics fast in Lent; many Protestants fast for a cause · pick one day this month to fast from something · *make space*
148. **The fast God chooses (Isaiah 58:6–7)** · — · loosen the bands, share your bread, don't hide from your own flesh · give what you'd have spent today · *fast to feed*
149. **One thing I know (John 9:25)** · testimony · a healed blind man can't win the argument, but he knows he was blind and now sees · write down one thing you know · *i was blind, now i see*
150. **Your story in three sentences** · — · many churches invite people to tell what God has done; it can be very small · write three plain sentences, no preaching · *your story counts*

## Week 22 · The sacraments, briefly (Days 151–156)
151. **Signs you can touch** · sacrament · Catholics and Orthodox count seven sacraments; most Protestants two ordinances; Quakers keep them inwardly · notice water, bread or oil today · *grace comes in ordinary things*
152. **Baptism, two ways** · — · many churches baptize infants into the family; Baptists and others baptize believers who choose · remember your name was given to you · *you were named*
153. **The table, many views** · Eucharist · Mass, Divine Liturgy, Lord's Supper: Catholics and Orthodox say Christ is truly present; many Protestants say spiritually, or by remembrance · eat bread slowly, giving thanks · *thanksgiving*
154. **Confession and anointing** · — · Catholics and Orthodox confess to a priest; Anglicans may; most Protestants confess to God directly or to a friend (James 5:16) · confess one thing to God · *said out loud, it loosens*
155. **Beads and silence** · rosary · Catholics pray the rosary and keep adoration before the sacrament; the Catholic path goes deeper · hold something in your hand while you pray · *the hands can pray too*
156. **Camp 3 close: a rule of life** · rule of life · monks and Methodists alike write a simple rule: what I'll do daily, weekly, yearly · write three lines of your own rule · *small rules keep big loves*

# CAMP 4 · THE TEXT (Days 157–231)
*The Gospel of Mark, read straight through: the shortest gospel, probably the first written, fast and breathless. A passage a day, in order.*

## Mark 1–3 · The breathless beginning (Days 157–170)
157. **The beginning (Mark 1:1–8)** · gospel · no birth story: Mark starts with a voice in the wilderness and a man in camel hair · read the first eight verses aloud · *the beginning of the good news*
158. **The heavens torn open (Mark 1:9–13)** · — · the sky rips, the Spirit comes down like a dove, then straight into the desert · notice how fast Mark moves · *beloved, then the desert*
159. **Follow me (Mark 1:14–20)** · immediately · Mark's favorite word: immediately they left their nets · do one thing immediately today · *leave the nets*
160. **Authority in Capernaum (Mark 1:21–28)** · — · in the synagogue he teaches with authority, and an unclean spirit shrieks · notice a voice in you that needs silencing · *be quiet, come out*
161. **A mother-in-law and a whole town (Mark 1:29–39)** · — · he heals Peter's mother-in-law, the whole town crowds the door, and before dawn he slips off to pray · rise a little early to pray · *a great while before day*
162. **If you will (Mark 1:40–45)** · leper · a man with leprosy says if you are willing; Jesus, moved, touches him · touch someone kindly, a hand on a shoulder · *i am willing*
163. **Through the roof (Mark 2:1–12)** · — · four friends dig through a roof to lower a paralyzed man to Jesus · do something stubborn for a friend · *friends who dig through roofs*
164. **Levi's table (Mark 2:13–17)** · — · the healthy don't need a doctor; he came for sinners · eat with someone unlike you · *the doctor is for the sick*
165. **New wine (Mark 2:18–22)** · — · you don't pour new wine into old skins · try one new way of doing an old thing · *new wine, new skins*
166. **Lord of the sabbath (Mark 2:23–28)** · — · the disciples pick grain on the sabbath, and Jesus defends them · rest in a way that heals · *made for people*
167. **The withered hand (Mark 3:1–6)** · — · he looks around in anger at hard hearts, and heals on the sabbath anyway · do good even if it's awkward · *stretch out your hand*
168. **Twelve names (Mark 3:7–19)** · apostle · he calls twelve to be with him and to be sent, including the one who'll betray him · write down twelve people who shaped you · *first, to be with him*
169. **Is he mad? (Mark 3:20–30)** · — · his family think he's lost his mind; scribes say he's possessed · sit with being misunderstood · *a house divided can't stand*
170. **Who are my mother and brothers? (Mark 3:31–35)** · — · whoever does God's will is family · thank someone who's family without being related · *family is wider*

## Mark 4–6 · Seeds, storms and loaves (Days 171–185)
171. **The sower again (Mark 4:1–9)** · — · teaching from a boat, he tells of seed thrown everywhere · listen with both ears · *let anyone with ears hear*
