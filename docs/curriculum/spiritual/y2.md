# YEAR TWO — "the porch" · the Stoics in full (Days 332–696)
*Year one quoted the Stoics in passing. Year two reads them properly and in order: Epictetus' Handbook, his Discourses, Seneca's letters and essays, the whole of Marcus, then the wider school and its neighbors. Sources: Long (Epictetus, Marcus), Gummere (Letters), Stewart (Dialogues, On Benefits), Yonge (Diogenes Laërtius), Cicero (Miller, Keyes), Boethius (H. R. James). Paraphrase throughout; section numbers in every hook. The Stoics are one school among many, read on their own terms.*

## Block A · Epictetus' Handbook, in order (Days 332–387)
332. **The handbook for the road** · enchiridion · Arrian, Epictetus' student, boiled his teacher down into a little book meant to be kept at hand · carry one small note all day · *keep it at hand*
333. **What is ours** · — · our judgments, impulses, desires and aversions are ours; body, property, reputation and office are not (Enchiridion 1) · list three of each · *start here*
334. **Desire, gently** · — · for now, don't desire what isn't up to you; only choose and refuse lightly (Enchiridion 2) · hold one wanting lightly · *gently, for now*
335. **It's a jug** · — · if you love a jug, say "I love a jug," and if it breaks you won't be thrown (Enchiridion 3) · name one loved thing plainly · *it's a jug*
336. **Going to the baths** · — · before going out, picture what happens at the public baths: splashing, shoving, theft, and keep your purpose (Enchiridion 4) · preview one outing · *and keep my purpose*
337. **Not things, but judgments** · dogma · people are upset not by things but by their judgments about things (Enchiridion 5) · find the judgment behind one upset · *it's my view*
338. **The horse is handsome, not you** · — · don't boast about your fine horse, as if its beauty were yours (Enchiridion 6) · notice one borrowed pride · *the horse is handsome*
339. **When the captain calls** · — · ashore on a voyage you may gather shells, but when the captain calls, run to the ship (Enchiridion 7) · hold one pleasure loosely · *listen for the call*
340. **Wish it as it comes** · — · don't demand that things happen as you wish, but wish them as they happen (Enchiridion 8) · welcome one small surprise · *as it comes*
341. **The leg, not the will** · — · illness hinders the body, not the will; lameness hinders the leg, not you (Enchiridion 9) · name one limit and what it can't touch · *not the will*
342. **What do I have for this?** · — · for each event, ask which power you have to meet it: patience for pain, endurance for hard work (Enchiridion 10) · match one trouble to one strength · *what do i have for this*
343. **I gave it back** · — · never say "I lost it," say "I returned it" (Enchiridion 11) · rephrase one loss · *i gave it back*
344. **The price of calm** · — · spilled oil, stolen wine: say, this is what peace of mind costs (Enchiridion 12) · let one small loss go cheaply · *the price of calm*
345. **Content to seem foolish** · — · if you want to improve, be willing to look foolish about outside things (Enchiridion 13) · look foolish once, on purpose · *let them think it*
346. **Who holds the leash** · — · whoever controls what you want is your master (Enchiridion 14) · notice one want that runs you · *who holds the leash*
347. **Life as a banquet** · — · when a dish reaches you, take a modest share; if it passes, don't reach after it (Enchiridion 15) · take a modest share today · *when the dish comes*
348. **Beside the weeping** · — · sit with a grieving friend and sigh with them, but don't groan inside; some readers find this cool, others wise (Enchiridion 16) · comfort someone without drowning · *with them, steady*
349. **An actor in a play** · — · you're an actor in a play someone else wrote; your job is to play the part well (Enchiridion 17) · play today's part well · *play the part well*
350. **The raven croaks** · — · when a raven croaks an omen, remember no sign has power over your will (Enchiridion 18) · shrug off one bad omen · *just a bird*
351. **Not my contest** · — · you can be unbeatable if you enter no contest where winning isn't up to you (Enchiridion 19) · skip one comparison · *not my contest*
352. **The insult** · — · it isn't the insulter who harms you but your judgment that you're harmed (Enchiridion 20) · delay one reaction by a breath · *a breath before*
353. **Nothing to envy** · — · keep death and exile before your eyes daily, and you won't grasp or envy (Enchiridion 21) · look at one envy in death's light · *nothing to envy*
354. **Expect to be laughed at** · — · take up philosophy and some will mock you; hold to what seems best (Enchiridion 22) · keep one practice quietly · *hold to it*
355. **Keep your footing** · — · turn outward to please someone and you've lost your footing (Enchiridion 23) · skip one people-pleasing move · *keep my footing*
356. **Faithful and modest** · — · you help your city not by holding office but by being trustworthy and self-respecting (Enchiridion 24) · be reliable in one small thing · *faithful and modest*
357. **The price of lettuce** · — · someone else got invited: they paid the price in flattery, and you kept your coin (Enchiridion 25) · notice one price you didn't pay · *i kept the coin*
358. **When a neighbor's cup breaks** · — · when someone else's cup breaks we say "these things happen," so say it of your own (Enchiridion 26) · treat one trouble of yours like a friend's · *these things happen*
359. **A target isn't set up to be missed** · — · evil isn't built into nature, any more than a target is set up to be missed (Enchiridion 27) · aim at one good · *aim at the good*
360. **Who handed you over?** · — · you'd be furious if someone handed your body to a stranger, so why hand your mind to anyone who insults you? (Enchiridion 28) · keep your mind your own today · *my mind is mine*
361. **Count the cost first** · — · before you begin anything, look at what comes first and what follows, like an athlete before the games (Enchiridion 29) · count the cost of one plan · *first, what follows*
362. **What the role asks** · kathēkon · duties are measured by relationships: father, brother, neighbor (Enchiridion 30) · do one duty of one role · *what the role asks*
363. **Honor, simply** · — · Epictetus on reverence: think rightly of the gods and keep offerings honest and plain (Enchiridion 31) · honor what you hold sacred, simply · *honestly, simply*
364. **No oracle needed** · — · ask diviners about things you can't know, never about whether to stand by a friend (Enchiridion 32) · do one duty without waiting for a sign · *no oracle needed*
365. **Mostly quiet** · — · speak rarely and only what's needed, and not about people (Enchiridion 33) · skip one piece of gossip · *mostly quiet*
366. **Yes means yes** · — · don't laugh much or loudly, and avoid swearing oaths when you can (Enchiridion 33) · keep your word without swearing · *yes means yes*
367. **Ask, don't tell** · — · don't dwell on your own deeds and dangers; others don't enjoy them as you do (Enchiridion 33) · ask more, tell less today · *ask, don't tell*
368. **He missed the rest** · — · if told someone speaks ill of you, say: he didn't know my other faults (Enchiridion 33) · answer one criticism with humor · *he missed the rest*
369. **Wait before pleasure** · — · when a pleasure beckons, pause, and picture both the enjoying and the regret after (Enchiridion 34) · wait ten minutes on one urge · *wait*
370. **No need to hide** · — · if you've judged it right, don't hide doing it (Enchiridion 35) · do one right thing openly · *no need to hide*
371. **The table matters** · — · the biggest portion may suit your stomach, but not the fellowship of the table (Enchiridion 36) · take the smaller portion · *the table matters*
372. **My own size** · — · take a role beyond your powers and you fail it and neglect the one you could have filled (Enchiridion 37) · decline one role that isn't yours · *my own size*
373. **Mind the step** · hēgemonikon · as you watch for nails when walking, watch over your ruling mind (Enchiridion 38) · walk carefully for three minutes · *mind the step*
374. **The foot measures the shoe** · — · the body is the measure of what you need, as the foot is of the shoe (Enchiridion 39) · notice one "enough" · *the foot measures*
375. **Honor the character** · — · girls flattered for looks from fourteen should learn they're honored for self-respect (Enchiridion 40) · praise someone for character, not looks · *honor the character*
376. **Mind first** · — · spending too long on the body is a sign of neglecting the mind (Enchiridion 41) · give five minutes to the mind · *mind first*
377. **It seemed right to him** · — · when someone wrongs you, remember he did what seemed right to him (Enchiridion 42) · try to understand one wrongdoer · *it seemed right to him*
378. **The other handle** · — · everything has two handles; take your brother by "he is my brother," not "he wronged me" (Enchiridion 43) · pick up one conflict by the other handle · *the other handle*
379. **Richer isn't better** · — · "I'm richer than you, so I'm better" doesn't follow (Enchiridion 44) · catch one bad comparison · *doesn't follow*
380. **Quickly, not badly** · — · don't say someone bathes badly, say quickly, until you know his reasons (Enchiridion 45) · describe instead of judging, once · *quickly, not badly*
381. **Show the wool** · — · sheep don't show the shepherd their fodder, they show wool (Enchiridion 46) · show practice, not talk · *show the wool*
382. **In secret** · — · if you live simply or drink only water, don't announce it (Enchiridion 47) · keep one discipline secret · *in secret*
383. **Signs of progress** · prokopē · the one making progress blames no one, praises no one, and claims to know nothing (Enchiridion 48) · check one sign in yourself · *blame no one*
384. **Live the book** · — · proud of explaining Chrysippus? better to live what he wrote, and keep your principles like laws (Enchiridion 49–50) · live one line you've read · *live the book*
385. **How long will you wait?** · — · you're not a child any more, and Socrates became himself by attending to reason in everything (Enchiridion 51) · start the thing today · *how long will you wait*
386. **Lead me on** · — · the Handbook ends with old lines: Cleanthes asking to be led willingly, Socrates saying they can kill but not harm him (Enchiridion 52–53) · say one line that steadies you · *willingly*
387. **Handbook close** · — · fifty-three chapters in fifty-six days; the handbook is now yours to carry · pick three chapters to keep · *carry three*

## Block B · The Discourses of Epictetus (Days 388–430)
*Arrian's notes of the classroom at Nicopolis: blunt, funny, and more tender than the Handbook.*
388. **The classroom at Nicopolis** · — · the Discourses are a student's notes of Epictetus teaching young men · read one paragraph aloud · *a real classroom*
389. **The gift of use** · — · the gods gave us one thing wholly ours: the right use of impressions (Discourses 1.1) · use one impression well · *the gift of use*
390. **Not even Zeus** · — · "I'll chain your leg." "My leg, yes; my will, not even Zeus can conquer" (Discourses 1.1) · find freedom inside one constraint · *not even zeus*
391. **Knowing your price** · — · Agrippinus kept his character though it cost him; each person knows their own price (Discourses 1.2) · refuse one thing beneath you · *i know my price*
392. **The purple thread** · — · Epictetus wants to be the purple stripe in the robe, the small part that makes the rest beautiful (Discourses 1.2) · be the bright thread in one moment · *the purple thread*
393. **Kin to the divine** · — · if we're kin to the divine, why think meanly of ourselves? (Discourses 1.3, 1.9) · stand up straight · *kin*
394. **Not more books** · — · progress isn't reading more, it's how you desire and avoid (Discourses 1.4) · measure progress by one reaction · *not more books*
395. **It fits** · — · eyes fit light and seasons fit crops, he argues, as signs of providence (Discourses 1.6) · notice one thing that fits · *it fits*
396. **Look closer** · — · you'd travel to Olympia to see Phidias' statue, so why not look at the world's order? (Discourses 1.6) · look at one ordinary wonder · *look closer*
397. **Love stays** · — · a father ran from his sick daughter's bedside, calling it love; Epictetus questions him gently (Discourses 1.11) · stay with someone who's unwell · *love stays*
398. **Content with what comes** · — · contentment isn't getting what you want but wanting what comes (Discourses 1.12) · want one thing that came · *content*
399. **Brothers by nature** · — · don't be harsh with your slave; he's your brother, born of the same Zeus (Discourses 1.13) · be gentle with someone who serves you · *brothers by nature*
400. **Never alone** · — · when you shut the door and darken the room, you're not alone: God is within, and your own spirit (Discourses 1.14) · sit alone in a quiet room · *never alone*
401. **Blind, not wicked** · — · would you be angry with a blind man? then pity the one blind about good and bad (Discourses 1.18) · feel pity instead of anger once · *blind, not wicked*
402. **The clay lamp** · — · a thief took his iron lamp, so he bought a clay one, and the thief lost more (Discourses 1.18) · let one small theft go · *a clay lamp*
403. **Not my judgment** · — · the tyrant has power over the body, never the judgment (Discourses 1.19) · name one fear and what it can't touch · *not my judgment*
404. **Test the coin** · — · test each impression the way a money-changer tests coins (Discourses 1.20) · test one thought before spending it · *test the coin*
405. **A good opponent** · — · difficulties show who we are, like a trainer pairing you with a rough young wrestler (Discourses 1.24) · meet one difficulty as training · *a good opponent*
406. **Stand firm** · — · the good lies in the will, so stand firm there (Discourses 1.29) · stay steady under one pressure · *stand firm*
407. **Bold outside, careful inside** · — · be confident about outside things and cautious about your own judgments (Discourses 2.1) · be bold about one outside thing · *bold outside, careful inside*
408. **Play the ball well** · — · players care about handling the ball well, not about the ball (Discourses 2.5) · play one task well, not for the prize · *play the ball well*
409. **Who am I here?** · — · remember who you are, a human, a citizen, a son, a brother, and act from each (Discourses 2.10) · act well from one role · *who am i here*
410. **Start with weakness** · — · philosophy begins with noticing your own weakness (Discourses 2.11) · admit one weakness · *start with weakness*
411. **Without the crowd** · — · the singer is only anxious when he wants the crowd's applause (Discourses 2.13) · do one thing with no audience · *without the crowd*
412. **Habits grow by use** · — · walk to become a walker, read to become a reader (Discourses 2.18) · repeat one good act · *habits grow by use*
413. **Count the calm days** · — · count the days you weren't angry: every day, then every other, then every third (Discourses 2.18) · mark today if calm · *count the calm days*
414. **Show me one** · — · show me someone ill and happy, in danger and happy: show me a Stoic (Discourses 2.19) · be the example in one small thing · *show me one*
415. **No scorekeeping** · — · only those who place good in the will can truly be friends (Discourses 2.22) · be a friend without keeping score · *no scorekeeping*
416. **Real beauty** · — · to a young man with elaborate hair: beauty is in excellence, not in grooming (Discourses 3.1) · compliment someone's character · *real beauty*
417. **Three fields** · — · desire, action and assent: the three areas of training (Discourses 3.2) · pick one of the three for today · *three fields*
418. **Found doing this** · — · let death find me attending to my will, Epictetus says (Discourses 3.5) · do something you'd want to be found doing · *found doing this*
419. **This too trains** · — · how to bear a fever: this too is exercise (Discourses 3.10) · rest well if you're unwell · *this too trains*
420. **Train in private** · — · train in private: drink water in the heat and tell no one (Discourses 3.12) · train one thing privately · *privately*
421. **Alone, not lonely** · — · being alone isn't loneliness; learn to be at ease by yourself (Discourses 3.13) · ten minutes alone, at ease · *alone, not lonely*
422. **The scout** · — · a long portrait of the Cynic as scout and witness, sent to report what's really good (Discourses 3.22) · be a good witness once · *a scout*
423. **In its season** · — · figs come in season and go, and so do the people we love (Discourses 3.24) · thank someone for their season · *in its season*
424. **At home here** · — · Odysseus wept for home on Calypso's island; Heracles was at home anywhere (Discourses 3.24) · feel at home where you are · *at home here*
425. **Free is wanting rightly** · — · the free person lives as they wish, which means wishing rightly (Discourses 4.1) · want one thing wisely · *free is wanting rightly*
426. **Nothing to take** · — · no one could enslave Diogenes, because he'd let go of everything they could take (Discourses 4.1) · loosen your grip on one thing · *nothing to take*
427. **My post** · — · stay at the post you've been given, like a soldier (Discourses 3.24) · stay at one post today · *my post*
428. **Peace of mind** · ataraxia · tranquillity comes from letting go of what isn't yours to control (Discourses 4.4) · sit five minutes in peace · *peace of mind*
429. **Attention, always** · prosochē · once you relax your attention, don't think you can pick it back up whenever you like (Discourses 4.12) · attend fully to one task · *attention*
430. **Discourses close** · — · forty lessons from a blunt, kind teacher who was once a slave · write one line to Epictetus · *thank you, teacher*

## Block C · Seneca: the letters and the essays (Days 431–520)
