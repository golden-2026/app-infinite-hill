# YEAR FOUR — "the thinkers and the schools" (Days 1062–1426)
*Four long companions read slowly: Lao Tzu's Tao Te Ching chapter by chapter, Montaigne's Essays, Emerson, and Thoreau. Then the science of contemplative practice, told plainly and honestly, and a tour of the world's schools of practice, each explained on its own terms and never ranked. Sources: Tao Te Ching (Legge, 1891); Montaigne (Cotton, 1685, rev. Hazlitt, 1877); Emerson and Thoreau (original editions); Patanjali (Woods, 1914); The Cloud of Unknowing (Underhill, 1912); Teresa of Ávila (Stanbrook, 1906); John of the Cross (Lewis, 1864); al-Ghazali (Field, 1909). Studies are cited by author and year and described with their limits.*

## Block B · Montaigne's Essays (Days 1145–1184)
*Michel de Montaigne (1533–1592), a French nobleman who retired to a tower and invented the essay, meaning "an attempt". A Catholic in a time of religious war, endlessly curious about himself.*
