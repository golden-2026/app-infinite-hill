# Christianity: week one, for our Keeper

Thank you for looking at this. Infinite Hill is a phone app for a few minutes of religious and spiritual practice each day, across eight paths. Each day is a short story told in pictures and captions, a few questions along the way, a word from the guide, and one small thing to try that day.

These are the seven lessons a newcomer meets in their first week on the Christianity path. We would be grateful if you would:

- **Confirm or correct the facts:** names, dates, quotes, references and practices.
- **Flag anything disrespectful,** or anything that would feel wrong to people of this tradition, even if it is technically accurate.
- **Suggest better wording** wherever a line could be truer, kinder or clearer.

Each lesson ends with a short "Please confirm" list: the points we most want your eyes on. Please mark each one as fine, or tell us what it should say. You are welcome to write straight into this document, or reply however is easiest for you.

Captions are short on purpose, so a simpler telling is fine as long as it is true to the tradition. A few items mention the pictures that go with a scene; those pictures come with this document.

## Day 1: Let there be light

**Story:** The first page of the Bible

**Story source**

- Genesis 1:1–2:3 (the seven days of creation), with the on-screen labels Genesis 1, 1:3 and 1:31
- Genesis 2:2–3 (God rests on the seventh day and blesses it), behind the last question

_(This lesson came without its own source notes, so we have listed the sources the story draws on. Please correct them if needed.)_

**Opening question from the guide**

> before the story, can i ask you something? ever stared at a blank page and not known where to start?

- "All the time": then this one was written for you. listen to how the bible starts.
- "Right now, actually": hold onto that. the very first page starts exactly there.
- "Not really": good. keep this one for the next new beginning.

**The lesson, in order**

Captions are the lines shown on screen with each picture. Questions are shown where they appear.

1. Before anything existed, the earth was formless and empty, and darkness was on the face of the deep.

> **Question 1** (guess before the story continues): Everything is dark and empty. What gets made first?
>
> A. Land to stand on
> B. Light  **(right answer)**
> C. People
>
> **Why:** Light. Before land, before people, the first word brings light.

2. Then God said, “Let there be light.” And there was light. _(label: Genesis 1:3)_
3. Day by day, the world was made. The sky opened above the sea. _(label: Genesis 1)_

> **Question 2** (guess the words): Again and again, the same words: “God saw that it was ____ .”
>
> A. finished
> B. good  **(right answer)**
> C. perfect
>
> **Why:** “Good.” Not finished, not perfect. Good. You’ll hear it again and again.

4. Land and trees. The sun, the moon and the stars. _(label: Genesis 1)_

> **Question 3** (think about it): Here’s a puzzle readers love: light comes on day one, but the sun isn’t made until day four. What might that be saying?
>
> A. The writers got the order mixed up
> B. The first light comes from God’s word, not from the sun  **(right answer)**
> C. The sun isn’t important
>
> **Why:** Many readers notice this. The sun and moon are made later. The first light comes straight from a word.

5. Fish in the water, birds in the air, and animals of every kind. _(label: Genesis 1)_
6. And finally, people. Again and again, the same words: “God saw that it was good.” The Bible’s first story is a story about starting. _(label: Genesis 1:31)_

> **Question 4** (match the days): The world is made day by day. Match each day to what was made.
>
> Right pairs:
> - day one = light
> - day four = sun, moon and stars
> - day six = animals and people
>
> **Why:** Genesis 1 builds the world in order: light, sky, land, the lights in the sky, fish and birds, then animals and people.


> **The guide teaches:**
> - here’s what i love. the bible’s very first story isn’t a rule. it’s a beginning.
> - christians read it as a promise: God makes something good out of nothing. a blank page isn’t empty. it’s where creation starts.


> **Question 5** (the hard one): Genesis goes on: on the seventh day, God stops and rests. Why would a story about making the world end with rest?
>
> A. God was tired
> B. Rest is part of the making, not a break from it  **(right answer)**
> C. Nothing else was left to make
>
> **Why:** Jewish and Christian readers see the seventh day as holy: the Sabbath. Rest is built into creation itself.


**Today (the step the guide suggests for the day)**

> pick one thing you’ve been putting off. do the first five minutes of it today, before you feel ready.

- "I’ll start today": that’s how every beginning goes. in the dark, one word first.
- "Something came to mind, not today": that counts. you’ve already named it. that’s the first word.

**Please confirm**

1. Caption 1 opens “Before anything existed, the earth was formless and empty.” Genesis 1:1–2 has the heavens and the earth already created and then formless; “before anything existed” may read as a contradiction. Confirm or suggest wording.
2. Question 2 and caption 6 say the same words, “God saw that it was good,” come “at the end of each day.” In the Hebrew text the second day has no “it was good” (the Greek Septuagint adds it). Confirm the wording.
3. The guide says Christians read the story as God making “something good out of nothing.” Creation from nothing is Christian teaching, while Genesis 1:2 pictures a formless deep. Confirm this sits well.
4. Question 3 calls “the first light comes from God’s word, not from the sun” the right answer to the day-one / day-four puzzle. Confirm this reading is fair to offer as the answer.

## Day 2: Abram packs up

**Story:** A story from the Bible

**Story source**

- Genesis 11:31 (Terah’s family settles in Haran)
- Genesis 12:1-3 (the call: leave your country, your relatives and your father’s house; “I will make of you a great nation… you will be a blessing”)
- Genesis 12:4 (Abram went; he was seventy-five years old when he left Haran)
- Genesis 12:5 (he took Sarai his wife, Lot his brother’s son, and all their possessions)
- Genesis 12:6-7 (arrival in Canaan; “I will give this land to your offspring”; Abram builds an altar); the altar is behind the today step
- Genesis 17:5 (renamed Abraham, “father of a multitude of nations”)
- Hebrews 11:8 (he went out, not knowing where he was going), behind the guide’s teaching

**Opening question from the guide**

> before the story, can i ask you something? ever felt too old, or too late, to start over?

- "Yes, right now": then this one is for you. listen closely.
- "I used to": then you’ll know this feeling. listen.
- "Not really": good. keep this one for someone you love who does.

**The lesson, in order**

Captions are the lines shown on screen with each picture. Questions are shown where they appear.

1. Abram lived in a city called Haran. He had a home, his family and his flocks. Life was settled.
2. Then God said to him, “Leave your country, your relatives and your father’s house, and go to the land I will show you.” _(label: Genesis 12:1)_

> **Question 1** (guess before the story continues): God tells him to go. What does Abram get for directions?
>
> A. A map of the road ahead
> B. A guide to walk with him
> C. Just a promise  **(right answer)**
>
> **Why:** Just a promise. No map, no address. “The land I will show you.”

3. No map. No address. Just a promise: “I will make of you a great nation. I will bless you, and you will be a blessing.” _(label: Genesis 12:2)_

> **Question 2** (finish the line): “I will bless you, and you will be a ____ .”
>
> A. great king
> B. blessing  **(right answer)**
> C. rich man
>
> **Why:** “A blessing.” The promise isn’t only for Abram. It’s meant to flow through him to others.

4. So Abram went. He took his wife Sarai, his nephew Lot and everything they had, and set out.

> **Question 3** (guess before the story continues): As he leaves, the Bible stops to tell us one thing about Abram. Your guess?
>
> A. How old he was  **(right answer)**
> B. How rich he was
> C. How far he planned to go
>
> **Why:** His age. The Bible makes a point of it.

5. Abram was seventy-five years old when he left Haran. _(label: Genesis 12:4)_

> **Question 4** (why?): Why might the story bother to tell us he was seventy-five?
>
> A. To explain why he took Lot along to help
> B. To mark when the family records began
> C. To show it wasn’t too late for him to begin  **(right answer)**
>
> **Why:** Many readers hear it that way. He wasn’t young and he wasn’t sure of the road. He started anyway.

6. At last they reached the land of Canaan. God said, “I will give this land to your offspring.” And Abram built an altar there. _(label: Genesis 12:7)_

> **Question 5** (where’s where): Match each one to its part in the story.
>
> Right pairs:
> - Abram = the one who went
> - Haran = the city he left
> - Canaan = the land he was shown
>
> **Why:** He left the settled city for a land he’d never seen, and only learned where it was by going.


> **The guide teaches:**
> - here’s what i love. abram didn’t get a map. he got a promise, and he took the first step anyway.
> - christians read him as a picture of faith: he went out not knowing where he was going. faith here means the next step, not the whole road.


> **Question 6** (the hard one): God later renames him Abraham, “father of many.” Today Jews, Christians and Muslims all honor him. What does that say about the promise?
>
> A. It was only ever about land
> B. It came true far beyond one family  **(right answer)**
> C. It was meant for Abram alone
>
> **Why:** “You will be a blessing.” A man of seventy-five with no map became the father of a great family of faith.


**Today (the step the guide suggests for the day)**

> abram marked his new start with an altar. today, before one new thing you begin, stop for a moment and hand it to God, in your own words or in silence, if you’d like. or simply notice the step.

- "I’ll try that today": that’s how abram’s road started. one step, offered up.
- "I’ll just notice it": that counts. you’ve named the step. the road starts there.

**What the learner keeps:** Abram’s footsteps. No map. Just the next step.

**Please confirm**

1. The last question’s answer reads the later honor of Abraham by Jews, Christians and Muslims as the promise coming true “far beyond one family”; confirm this framing is acceptable.
2. Caption 1 sets Abram in Haran when God calls him. Genesis 11:31 has the family coming from Ur, and Acts 7:2 places the call earlier, in Mesopotamia. Confirm that starting in Haran is fair for a short telling.

## Day 3: The son who came home

**Story:** A story Jesus told

**Story source**

- Luke 15:11–32 (the parable of the father and his two sons), with the on-screen label Luke 15:22–24
- Luke 15:1–2 (the Pharisees and scribes grumble that Jesus welcomes sinners and eats with them), behind the last question
- Leviticus 11:7 and Deuteronomy 14:8 (the pig as unclean), behind question 2

_(This lesson came without its own source notes, so we have listed the sources the story draws on. Please correct them if needed.)_

**Opening question from the guide**

> before the story, can i ask you something? ever messed up so badly you didn’t think you could go back?

- "Yes, I have": most of us have. hold onto that feeling. this story was told for it.
- "Someone I love has": then keep them in mind while you hear this one.
- "Not really": good. just listen. you might need this one later, or someone you love might.

**The lesson, in order**

Captions are the lines shown on screen with each picture. Questions are shown where they appear.

1. Jesus told a story. A young man said to his father, “Give me my share of what’s coming to me.”

> **Question 1** (think about it): Back then, you got your share when your father died. So asking for it now was a bit like saying…
>
> A. “I’m ready to grow up.”
> B. “I wish you were gone.”  **(right answer)**
> C. “Help me start a business.”
>
> **Why:** That’s how many readers hear it: a deep insult. Now watch what the father does.

2. So the father divided what he had.
3. Not many days later, the younger son packed everything and left for a far country.
4. There he wasted it all on wild living.
5. Then a severe famine came over that country, and he began to be in need.
6. He took a job feeding pigs. He longed to eat what the pigs ate, and no one gave him anything.

> **Question 2** (did you know?): Jesus’s listeners would have winced at the pigs. Why?
>
> A. Pigs were sacred animals
> B. Under Jewish law, pigs were unclean  **(right answer)**
> C. Only slaves worked with animals
>
> **Why:** Jesus was speaking to Jewish listeners. Feeding pigs for a foreigner was about as low as a Jewish son could fall.


> **Question 3** (your guess): What finally makes him decide to go home?
>
> A. He misses his father
> B. He’s starving and has nothing left  **(right answer)**
> C. His father sends for him
>
> **Why:** Honestly? Hunger. His reasons weren’t noble. Keep that in mind when you see what his father does.

7. Then he came to himself.
8. He set off for home, practicing his speech: “Father, I have sinned. Make me one of your hired servants.”

> **Question 4** (why?): Why does he plan to ask to be a servant, not a son?
>
> A. Servants got paid, sons didn’t
> B. He felt he’d lost the right to be a son  **(right answer)**
> C. His brother had taken his place
>
> **Why:** He thought he’d used up his place in the family. Hold onto that.

9. But while he was still far off, his father saw him.

> **Question 5** (guess before the story continues): His father sees him on the road. What does he do?
>
> A. Waits at the door, arms crossed
> B. Sends a servant to ask why he’s back
> C. Runs to him  **(right answer)**
>
> **Why:** He runs. In that culture a grown man running in public was undignified. He didn’t care.

10. He was moved with compassion. He ran, threw his arms around him, and kissed him.
11. The son began his speech. The father didn’t let him finish.

> **Question 6** (guess his words): “This son of mine was dead, and is alive again. He was lost, and is ____ .”
>
> A. forgiven
> B. found  **(right answer)**
> C. home
>
> **Why:** “Found.” Listen for it now. It’s the line the whole story turns on.

12. “Bring out the best robe. Put a ring on his hand. Let’s celebrate. He was lost, and is found.” _(label: Luke 15:22-24)_

> **Question 7** (match the gifts): Each gift said something. Match them.
>
> Right pairs:
> - the best robe = you’re honored
> - a ring = you’re family again
> - a feast = we celebrate you
>
> **Why:** A servant would get none of these. The father gave him back the place he thought he’d lost, before the speech was even done.


> **The guide teaches:**
> - here’s the part i love. the son had a whole speech ready. his father never heard the end of it.
> - christians read the father as a picture of God. not waiting at the door with a lecture. running down the road.


> **Question 8** (the hard one): Luke says Jesus told this story to people complaining that he welcomed sinners. Who was it really for?
>
> A. The sinners, so they’d behave
> B. The complainers, who weren’t glad  **(right answer)**
> C. His friends, as a warning about money
>
> **Why:** Luke 15 opens with religious leaders grumbling about the company Jesus kept. The story keeps going for them: the older brother, who won’t come to the party.


**Today (the step the guide suggests for the day)**

> is there someone you’ve drifted from? today, send one short, kind message. no speech needed.

- "I’ll send one today": that’s the whole thing. one step down the road.
- "Someone came to mind, not today": that counts too. keep them in mind. the door stays open.

**Please confirm**

1. Question 1 says asking for the inheritance early was “a bit like saying ‘I wish you were gone.’” This is a common reading, not words in the text. Confirm it is fair as the right answer.
2. Question 5’s “why” says a grown man running in public was undignified in that culture. Confirm this cultural note.
3. Question 7 gives a meaning to each gift (robe = honor, ring = family again, feast = celebration). These meanings are interpretation; the text also mentions sandals and the fattened calf. Confirm.
4. The last question gives “the complainers” as who the story was really for. Confirm this reading of Luke 15:1–2 is fair as the one right answer.

_Anything else you would change, in your own words:_

## Day 4: Nets out one more time

**Story:** A story about Jesus

**Story source**

- Luke 5:1-3 (crowd at the lake of Gennesaret, i.e. the Sea of Galilee; fishermen washing nets; Jesus teaches from Simon’s boat)
- Luke 5:4 (“Put out into the deep, and let down your nets for a catch”)
- Luke 5:5 (“Master, we worked all night and caught nothing; but at your word I will let down the net”)
- Luke 5:6-7 (nets begin to break; partners in the other boat come; both boats fill and begin to sink)
- Luke 5:8 (“Depart from me, for I am a sinful man, Lord”)
- Luke 5:10 (James and John, sons of Zebedee, Simon’s partners; “Don’t be afraid. From now on you will be catching people”)
- Luke 5:11 (they left everything and followed him)
- Related, not used in captions: John 21:1-11 (a second night of empty nets and a full catch after the resurrection)
- Luke 5:9 (amazement seized him and all who were with him)

**Opening question from the guide**

> before the story, can i ask you something? ever worked all night, or all year, and had nothing to show for it?

- "Yes, right now": then sit with me a minute. this one starts right there.
- "I’ve been there": then you’ll know how simon felt. listen.
- "Not really": good. keep this one for the next long night.

**The lesson, in order**

Captions are the lines shown on screen with each picture. Questions are shown where they appear.

1. Simon was a fisherman on the Sea of Galilee. He’d fished all night and caught nothing. Now he was on the shore, washing his nets.
2. A crowd pressed in to hear Jesus. He stepped into Simon’s boat, asked him to push out a little, and taught from there.

> **Question 1** (guess before the story continues): When he finishes teaching, Jesus asks Simon to do something. What is it?
>
> A. Take him across the lake
> B. Go back out and fish again  **(right answer)**
> C. Lend the boat to his friends
>
> **Why:** Go back out. After a whole night of nothing, in broad daylight.

3. “Put out into the deep water, and let down your nets for a catch.” _(label: Luke 5:4)_

> **Question 2** (guess his words): “Master, we worked all night and caught nothing. But ____ , I’ll let down the nets.”
>
> A. because you say so  **(right answer)**
> B. if the fish come back
> C. just this once
>
> **Why:** “Because you say so.” He’s tired, he knows the lake better than any teacher, and he does it anyway.


> **Question 3** (guess before the story continues): Simon lets the nets down in broad daylight. What happens?
>
> A. Nothing, again
> B. A few small fish
> C. So many fish the nets start to tear  **(right answer)**
>
> **Why:** So many the nets began to break, and it took two boats to hold them.

4. The nets filled until they began to tear. They signaled to their partners, James and John, and both boats filled until they began to sink.
5. Simon fell at Jesus’s knees. “Go away from me, Lord. I’m a sinful man.” _(label: Luke 5:8)_

> **Question 4** (why?): Simon just had the best catch of his life. Why does he say “go away from me”?
>
> A. He was angry the nets had torn
> B. He felt unworthy to be near him  **(right answer)**
> C. He was afraid the boats would sink
>
> **Why:** Luke says amazement seized him. Up close to something holy, Simon felt small. Watch how gently Jesus answers.

6. Jesus said, “Don’t be afraid. From now on you’ll be catching people.” _(label: Luke 5:10)_
7. They brought the boats to shore, left everything, and followed him.

> **Question 5** (who’s who): Match each one to their part in the story.
>
> Right pairs:
> - Simon = fished all night for nothing
> - James and John = partners in the other boat
> - “catching people” = Simon’s new calling
>
> **Why:** Simon is later called Peter, “rock,” and becomes a leader of Jesus’s first followers.


> **The guide teaches:**
> - here’s what i love. simon knew fishing better than any teacher. he said so. then he let the nets down anyway.
> - christians read it as a picture of trust: “because you say so.” peter’s fresh start began with one more try he didn’t feel like making.


> **Question 6** (the hard one): Simon was the expert. Jesus was a teacher, not a fisherman. In this story, what turned the empty night around?
>
> A. Trusting enough to try once more  **(right answer)**
> B. Simon finding a better fishing spot
> C. Simon’s years of skill
>
> **Why:** Same lake, same nets, same tired man. What changed was “because you say so.”


**Today (the step the guide suggests for the day)**

> pick one thing you’ve given up on: a habit, a goal, a conversation. many christians bring it to God first, a short prayer or a quiet moment, if you’d like. then give it one more honest try, the way simon did: “because you say so.”

- "I’ll try once more": that’s the whole story. nets out one more time.
- "Something came to mind": that counts. simon explained first too. then he went.

**What the learner keeps:** Simon’s boat. One more try, out in the deep.

**Please confirm**

1. Question 1 and question 3 say Simon fished again “in broad daylight.” Luke implies it was day (after the night, while Jesus taught a crowd) but doesn’t say so. Confirm.
2. Question 2 renders Luke 5:5 “at your word” as “because you say so,” and the guide repeats it. Confirm the paraphrase.

## Day 5: Born again

**Story:** A night talk with Jesus

**Story source**

- John 3:1 (Nicodemus, a Pharisee and a ruler of the Jews)
- John 3:2 (he came to Jesus by night: “Rabbi, we know that you are a teacher come from God”)
- John 3:3 (“Unless one is born anew, he can’t see the Kingdom of God”; KJV “born again”)
- John 3:4 (“How can a man be born when he is old? Can he enter a second time into his mother’s womb?”)
- John 3:5-8 (born of water and Spirit; “You must be born anew”; “The wind blows where it wants to…”)
- John 3:10 (Jesus calls him “the teacher of Israel”)
- John 19:39 (Nicodemus, “who at first came to Jesus by night,” brings myrrh and aloes for the burial)
- Greek pneuma = wind, breath, spirit (used in John 3:8); anōthen = again / from above

**Opening question from the guide**

> before the story, can i ask you something? ever wished you could start your whole life over? not a new job. a new you.

- "Yes, I have": then you’re in good company. a very important man felt that way too.
- "Some days": then keep that day in mind. this one happens at night.
- "Not really": good. you’ve probably heard the phrase. here’s where it starts.

**The lesson, in order**

Captions are the lines shown on screen with each picture. Questions are shown where they appear.

1. Nicodemus was a respected teacher, a leader people looked up to. One night, he came to see Jesus.

> **Question 1** (think about it): Why might an important teacher visit Jesus at night?
>
> A. Jesus only taught after dark
> B. Maybe so no one would see him  **(right answer)**
> C. It was the custom for every visitor
>
> **Why:** John doesn’t say. Many readers guess he didn’t want to be seen, since other leaders were wary of Jesus.

2. “Teacher,” he said, “we know you’ve come from God.”
3. Jesus answered, “Unless someone is born again, he can’t see the kingdom of God.” _(label: John 3:3)_

> **Question 2** (guess before the story continues): Jesus says you must be born again. How does Nicodemus take it?
>
> A. He nods. He’s heard it before
> B. He walks away offended
> C. He takes it literally and is baffled  **(right answer)**
>
> **Why:** He pictures a grown man being born a second time. Listen to his question.

4. Nicodemus was baffled. “How can a grown man be born again? Can he go back into his mother’s womb?”
5. Jesus said this birth is of the Spirit. “The wind blows where it wants. You hear it, but you don’t know where it comes from or where it’s going.” _(label: John 3:8)_

> **Question 3** (finish the line): “The wind blows where it ____ . You hear it, but you don’t know where it comes from.”
>
> A. is sent
> B. wants  **(right answer)**
> C. always has
>
> **Why:** “Where it wants.” Free, unseen, and you still know it’s there.


> **Question 4** (why?): Why does Jesus talk about the wind?
>
> A. It happened to be a windy night
> B. The Spirit, like wind, is known by what it does  **(right answer)**
> C. To warn him trouble was coming
>
> **Why:** In Greek, the language John wrote in, one word, pneuma, means both wind and spirit.

6. Nicodemus appears again near the end of John’s gospel. When Jesus died, the man who first came at night came again, carrying spices to help bury him. _(label: John 19:39)_

> **Question 5** (match them): Match each one to what it means in the story.
>
> Right pairs:
> - Nicodemus = came at night with questions
> - the wind = felt, not seen, like the Spirit
> - born again = a new life from God
>
> **Why:** His night visit wasn’t his last. Something in him kept changing.


> **The guide teaches:**
> - here’s what i love. nicodemus had status, learning and answers. and he still came at night with a question.
> - “born again” comes from this story. for christians it means a new life from God, a gift you receive, not something you earn.


> **Question 6** (the hard one): The Greek word Jesus used for “again” can also mean “from above.” What does that add to what he meant?
>
> A. The new birth comes from God, not from trying harder  **(right answer)**
> B. You have to be baptized a second time
> C. Only people in heaven are born again
>
> **Why:** Nicodemus pictured a second birth of the body. Jesus meant a birth from above, by the Spirit, something God gives.


**Today (the step the guide suggests for the day)**

> tonight, like nicodemus, bring one honest question you carry about life or faith. write it down, ask someone you trust, or, if you like, pray it. your choice.

- "I’ll bring my question": that’s exactly what he did. jesus didn’t send him away.
- "I’m not ready yet": that’s okay. he came at night. there’s no rush.

**What the learner keeps:** A breath of wind. Unseen, and still it changes everything.

**Please confirm**

1. Pictures set the talk on a lamp-lit rooftop; John doesn’t say where. Art only, not in captions.
2. The lesson leaves out John 3:5, “born of water and the Spirit,” which many churches (for example Catholic, Orthodox and Lutheran) connect with baptism. The last question marks “You have to be baptized a second time” as wrong. Confirm the lesson’s “born again” explanation sits well across churches.

## Day 6: The man up a tree

**Story:** A story about Jesus

**Story source**

## Day 7: Even God rested

**Story:** The first week of the Bible

**Story source**

- Genesis 2:1 (the heavens and the earth were finished, and all their vast array)
- Genesis 2:2 (on the seventh day God finished his work and rested from all his work)
- Genesis 2:3 (God blessed the seventh day and made it holy)
- Exodus 20:8 (“Remember the Sabbath day, to keep it holy”); Exodus 20:11 ties it to creation
- Hebrew shavat / shabbat: to stop, to cease (the verb in Genesis 2:2)
- Jewish Sabbath: Friday sunset to Saturday sunset, with candles, blessings over bread and wine, and a pause from work
- Mark 16:2 and Luke 24:1 (the resurrection on the first day of the week); Revelation 1:10 (“the Lord’s day”)
- Some Christian groups (for example Seventh-day Adventists) keep Saturday

**Opening question from the guide**

> before the story, can i ask you something? when did you last stop, with nothing to do? not scrolling. not catching up. stopping.

- "I honestly can’t remember": then this one is a gift for you. take your time with it.
- "This week, actually": good. hold onto that feeling. the bible calls it holy.
- "I don’t really stop": you’re not alone. listen to how the very first week ends.
