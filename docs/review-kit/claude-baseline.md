# Claude's baseline review

Written 2026-10-03 by Claude, before any outside expert has looked, so it can be compared with them later. It covers
all 80 sampled lessons in `lessons/` and the 30 Guide test questions. **Read the caveat first:** Claude's own writing
workflows produced these lessons, so this is partly self-grading. It tries to be harsh, and it found real problems,
but where a scholar disagrees, trust the scholar, especially on accuracy and insider respect.

Scores use `rubric.md` (1 to 5). Accuracy is scored from reading only; Claude did not look anything up, and many
lessons' own notes say their quotes and hadith numbers were "cited from memory". Spot checks of about a dozen
references (hadith numbers, sutta numbers, Gita and Upanishad verses) all looked right, but that is the same kind of
memory that wrote them.

## The headline

**The content is better than "basic": it's mostly accurate, careful and warm. The teaching design around it is the weak
part.** The spoken teaching usually gets the text right, names its sources, and often lays out more than one reading
(Advaita and other Vedanta schools on the Mundaka; Sunni and Shi'a readings of Hasan's treaty; Valmiki versus Tulsidas
on Angada; Jewish readings of the binding of Isaac). What's thin is everything that turns a nice 6 minutes into
learning that lasts: the games, the review, the practice, and the way lessons build. And quality drops from year 1 to
year 3, where the seams of the writing process start to show.

**Average scores (80 lessons):** accuracy **4.0**, depth **3.4**, memorable **3.6**, insider respect **3.9**, voice
**3.9**, learning design **2.9**. Overall **3.6**.

By the README's bar this is "fix specific things", not "rewrite how lessons are made": accuracy and insider respect clear
3.5 everywhere, but learning design sits right at the edge (2.9 overall, 2.4 in year 3), and the same problems repeat
on every door.

## Door by door

**Christianity (3.7).** The strongest door. Lessons stay close to the text, quote the King James carefully, and use
real scholarship lightly (Mark's "could do no mighty work" against Matthew's softer version; the Abimelech/Achish
puzzle in Psalm 34). Weak spot: year-3 days lean on filler (day 1,000 is a meditation on the word "thousand"), and many
lessons are a well-told retelling with a moral attached rather than an argument.

**Catholic (3.7).** Solid and liturgically grounded: lessons tie texts to where Catholics actually meet them (Holy
Thursday's Mandatum, Isaiah 53 on Good Friday, kneeling at the Creed on Christmas). Overlaps heavily with the
Christianity door (both teach John 1:14's "pitched his tent"), so a family on both doors would hear repeats. Dante gets
one lesson for the whole Divine Comedy, which is a highlights reel.

**Judaism (3.6).** Good use of Rashi, the Mishnah and midrash, and honest about disagreement (the Akedah, Hebrew in
prayer). Some year-2 lessons turn into numbered lists ("First, the law. Second, who eats. Third, why Sinai? Fourth, the
week behind us"). Day 1,000 on Herzl is the weakest lesson sampled: the "claims rule" stripped even the year (1897),
the practice turns political Zionism into "dream one practical dream", and the real debate is postponed to tomorrow.

**Islam (3.7).** Careful and respectful: hadith are cited by collection and number, Pickthall is quoted, scholarly
disagreement (abrogation of 8:61; the meaning of Yusuf's "sign") is named without taking sides. Small inconsistency in
honorifics ("peace be upon him" on some days, ﷺ on others). Year 3's "The Hui of China" ends in a practice of planning
to cook noodles, which feels like a travel segment.

**Hinduism (3.5).** The best texts-and-schools content of any door (Gita 6, Mundaka 3.2, Yoga Sutra 1.13 to 14,
Ashtavakra Gita), with accurate Sanskrit. But it shows the process seams most: titles like "Patanjali 1.1 to 1.16: now,
yoga ... (6 of 7)", and lines to carry such as "Bhishma Parva (the Gita re-read in place, 6 sessions)" and "the
Ashtavakra Gita complete". Year-2 Ramayana days are faithful plot recaps (Hinduism #8 is almost all plot). Day 1 is
pitched at yoga-class outsiders, which a practising Hindu may find a little touristy.

**Buddhism (3.6).** Accurate Pali, fair to Theravada and Mahayana, good use of suttas (MN 22's raft, MN 149, SN 3.25).
Strong first week (the third truth with the fire that went out). Jataka and year-3 days drift into motivational
morals ("dig once more"; "notice one title, of a book, a song, a job, and ask what whole story it might be holding").

**Sikhism (3.4).** Warm, accurate on practice (seva, simran, Ardas, nitnem), but the thinnest on text: day 1 cites the
Guru Granth Sahib only as "passim", and several days are history (World Wars) or recaps rather than Gurbani. "Rehras
close" is a 16-day recap read as a list ("so dar, what is that door ... Then So Purakh ... The pool of fire and water")
that only makes sense if you remember all 15 previous days.

**Simply Spiritual (3.6).** Well sourced (Seneca, Epictetus, Plato, Rumi, a Hasidic tale, a psychology study) and the
most "think about this" of all the doors. But it borrows freely from other doors' material (Psalm 19, Rumi, Simcha
Bunim), so a scholar of each source should check it. Day 180's fork marks the wrong behaviour as the right answer ("you're
the lion ... what do you do?" right answer: "leap at him"; the reveal then says Rumi's point is the other option).

## The five most common weaknesses

These show up on every door. The numbers in brackets come from checking all 8,488 written lessons in years 1 to 3, not
just the 80 samples.

### 1. Every day has exactly the same shape

All 8,488 lessons have the same seven parts in the same order, the same five games, three true-or-myth items and one
three-option fork. Every practice ends "The bell holds the time." (8,488 of 8,488). About 4 in 10 practices are
imagining rather than doing (3,229 practices say "picture" or "imagine"). Examples: "Picture handing it over" (Islam #7,
the Eid meat for a neighbour); "picture yourself bringing it: the walk there, the handing over" (Christianity #9);
"Picture a small heap of stones between you" (Judaism #3). A daily habit can survive a fixed shape (Duolingo's is
fixed too), but here the sameness is in the content beats, not just the screens, and by year 2 it reads as a formula.

### 2. The games test the last two minutes, and many can be guessed

The fork ("what would you do?") is really a recall question, and the right answer is usually the obviously kind or wise
one, next to silly alternatives: "chase them off; it's your field / let them eat; it's open to all / sell it to them at
cost" (Judaism #6); "give your name and enjoy the thanks / smile, say it was nothing, and keep washing / ask them to post
about it" (Sikhism #1); "proof you're bad at this / a sign to stop for the day / the practice: the effort of coming back"
(Hinduism #5). Worse, **the right answer is option 2 in 7,450 of 8,488 forks (88%)**, and the lesson builder appears to
show fork options in their written order (`packages/content/generated/logic.js` passes the fork through unshuffled;
worth confirming in the app). Nothing ever asks about a lesson from weeks earlier.

### 3. Little real review, so little builds up

"Looking back" is almost always yesterday's line plus a "did you...?" question (8,413 of 8,480 start with "Yesterday";
only 37 reach back further than that). Example: "Yesterday: Al-Bayyinah, and the verse that said all that was asked.
Your line was sincere and upright. Did you do one good thing that no one will ever know about?" (Islam #4). End-of-week
look-backs list titles ("pleasant places, the heavens declare, my God my God, whom shall I fear...") rather than asking
the learner to recall anything. There's no spaced review and no check that day 60 is still known on day 400, which is
exactly what the handoff's "learning engine" item is meant to add.

### 4. Every lesson ends in a life tip, and some trivialize the material

Each lesson turns its story into a small personal action. Often that's lovely (Lazarus becomes "let yourself feel one
grief instead of rushing past it"). But it also flattens serious material into self-help: the siege of Lanka becomes
"in one disagreement, make a plain offer of peace" (Hinduism #8); Herzl and the Zionist Congress become "dream one
practical dream" (Judaism #10); Nichiren's teaching on the Lotus Sutra becomes "notice one title, of a book, a song, a
job" (Buddhism #10); the Hui Muslims of China become "find one recipe for hand-pulled noodles and save it" (Islam #10).
An insider may feel their tradition is being used as a source of tips.

### 5. The seams of the writing process show, mostly in years 2 and 3

Outline labels leak into what learners see and hear. In 1,435 lessons (17%) the "line to carry" is just the title or
word repeated, and in 4,326 (51%) "today's word" is a phrase of three words or more, so the "word strand" stops being
words. Examples: carry "Bhishma Parva (the Gita re-read in place, 6 sessions)" (Hinduism #9); carry "the Ashtavakra
Gita complete" (Hinduism #10); today's word "Rehras close" (Sikhism #6) and "Meditations close" (Simply Spiritual #8);
the title "Patanjali 1.1 to 1.16: now, yoga; yoga is the stilling of the mind's turnings; practice and non-attachment (6
of 7)" whose word is "Patanjali 1.1" but whose lesson is about 1.13 and 1.14 (Hinduism #5). This is the clearest sign
of "not well thought through", and it's fixable mechanically.

**Also worth fixing:** depth has a ceiling. The teaching part is about 256 spoken words a day (two minutes), and most
lessons stop at "remember and understand" plus "apply it to your life"; few ask the learner to weigh or argue anything.
The "claims rule" (no unsourced numbers or dates) keeps lessons honest but makes history vague (no 1897 for Herzl).

## Placement: what the simulation shows

Claude re-ran the placement logic from `apps/app/src/lib/placement.ts` against the real question bank, 20,000 times per
door (script kept in `atlas/review-kit/placement-sim.mjs`):
- **Beginners** start at day 1 after 2 questions. Good.
- **Random guessers** start at day 1 about 93% of the time; the rest skip only to day 22. Good.
- **Test-wise guessers** do much better than they should: the right answer is the longest option in 123 of 182 story
  questions (68%, against about 33% by chance). Always picking the longest answer skips camp one about a third of the
  time on Hinduism, Judaism and Catholic.
- **Experts who never used the app** are the open question. The "word" questions take their wrong options from the same
  lesson's other match pairs, so some wrong options are also true (Hinduism: "Valmiki's Angada" with the wrong option
  "Vali's son, sent as the last messenger"), and some answers only make sense in our lessons' phrasing (adhyasa vs "the
  rope, misseen"). An expert could be placed too low.
- **Simply Spiritual** has no placement questions at all.

## What surprised Claude

- The accuracy and fairness are genuinely good; the writers' notes show heavy checking. The worry "basic and not
  thought through" fits the course design (games, review, practice, progression) far more than the content.
- 88% of fork answers sit in the same position.
- The Guide prompt asks the Guide to say scholars review the lessons, which isn't true yet.
- The English Guide has no crisis hotline rule, while the companion does.
- Year 3 scores lower than year 1 on all six dimensions (overall 3.4 against 3.7); the later, faster writing shows.
