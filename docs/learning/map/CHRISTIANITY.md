# Five-year map: Christianity (2026-10-06)

Step 1 only: a plan to review. Nothing in `docs/curriculum/christianity` is edited here.

## 1. What the research says this audience needs

1. **The Trinity, explained simply.** The single most-cited "I say it but can't explain it" gap. (AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_CHRISTIANITY §2 #17, §"Top 20 moments" #1)
2. **Communion and baptism: what they're actually doing, and why churches disagree on the how.** Near-universal memory, rarely explained meaning. (AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_CHRISTIANITY "Top 20" #2, #4; REDDIT.md #19-21)
3. **Christianese decoded** — "quiet time," "testimony," "on fire," "let go and let God." Zero doctrinal risk, instant recognition. (AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_CHRISTIANITY §4, "Top 20" #8)
4. **"Is it okay to doubt?"** Answer yes, early, with Thomas, Job, the Psalms. The single most useful reassurance in every Reddit thread read. (REDDIT_REPLIES.md §3; REDDIT_ROUND2.md §3)
5. **"I feel nothing when I pray" / prayer feels fake.** The top practice complaint on r/TrueChristian, ahead of doctrine doubts. (REDDIT.md §2, patterns #1)
6. **Is therapy or medicine a sin?** Asked repeatedly; needs a clear, warm no. Touches our own crisis-care rule. (REDDIT.md §2, patterns #2)
7. **Fear of hell outlasts belief**, often with OCD, even after someone has left the faith. Never use fear as a teaching tool. (REDDIT.md §2, patterns #3)
8. **"Where do I start reading the Bible?"** The most-repeated practical question on r/Christianity (10+ separate threads). (REDDIT.md §2)
9. **The Rapture: is it even biblical, and why was I scared of it as a kid?** Real, documented childhood fear from Sunday school scare tactics. (AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_CHRISTIANITY §2 #31-33; "Top 20" #7)
10. **"I'm done with the Church, not Christ."** A named, trending split between the institution and the person; church hurt is common and needs to be believed, not argued with. (REDDIT_TRENDING.md §2; REDDIT_ROUND2.md §3)
11. **Pastors and money** — the "$5,000 sneakers" pattern. A real, large skepticism this audience holds toward institutional Christianity; describe the pattern, never a named living leader. (AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_CHRISTIANITY §1 Lane B, "Top 20" #14)
12. **The "is it a sin?" edge questions** (tattoos, alcohol, gambling, sex before marriage) are GotQuestions' actual highest-traffic searches, more than doctrine. They need an honest FAQ container, not a verdict machine. (AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_CHRISTIANITY §2, "Top 20" #11)
13. **Light, almost charming head-scratchers** — "Who was Cain's wife?", dinosaurs and the Bible — are genuinely common and make good light, early hooks. (AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_CHRISTIANITY §2 #7, #13; "Top 20" #15, #20)
14. **Hard Old Testament texts and slavery**: when raised, defending them loses people fast; "it's disturbing when people try to justify" was the top-voted reply on one thread. Needs a dedicated, honest, later lesson — not avoidance. (REDDIT_REPLIES.md §3)
15. **Speaking in tongues and other charismatic gifts** need the *range* of Christian views stated fairly (cessationist, continuationist, Catholic discernment), never the loudest subreddit's verdict. (AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_CHRISTIANITY "Top 20" #6; REDDIT_ROUND2.md §3)
16. **Women pastors and the Bible / homosexuality and the Bible** are GotQuestions' #1 and #2 most-searched questions of all — proof the audience is asking — but sit on live denominational and political fault lines and need late, careful, explicitly reviewed treatment, never a quick early lesson. (AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_CHRISTIANITY §2 #1-2; "What to avoid early")

## 4. Sensitive ground

- **Women pastors, homosexuality and the Bible, sexuality and gender more broadly:** real, top-searched questions, but directly on live political and denominational fault lines. Keep in Year 4 as planned; expand only to name the question and the honest range of views, never a verdict. **Needs Keeper review before anything ships**, specifically a Keeper who can confirm the "range of views" summary is fair to all sides represented in our eight-door audience.
- **The Rapture, hell, and "left behind" fear:** carries documented real fear, sometimes clinical (OCD). Needs the 988/local-emergency-line treatment the owner already requires for danger or self-harm content, and a tone pass to make sure no day uses fear as a teaching device. **Keeper review required** on tone, not just facts.
- **Slavery and Old Testament violence:** needs a historian's and a pastor's eye together — easy to either defend (loses trust fast, per the research) or wave away (feels evasive). **Keeper review required**, ideally someone comfortable naming the hard parts plainly.
- **Speaking in tongues and other charismatic gifts:** already handled with a fair range of views; just confirm any new or edited day keeps cessationist, continuationist and Catholic-discernment positions all represented, since Pentecostals and charismatics are hundreds of millions of Christians worldwide and shouldn't read as a fringe view.
- **Pastors and money:** describe the pattern, never a named living pastor or church — this path already avoids "gotcha" framing toward real people, and that rule matters even more here since the research's own example (PreachersNSneakers) is itself about a real account.

## 5. What stays

- Week 1's rebuild (communion, Trinity, born again, baptism, testimony, Holy Spirit, the Bible) already matches the research's own top-ranked list almost exactly — no changes needed there.
- The Gospel of Mark read straight through (Camp 4) is a strong, distinctive choice and needs no rework.
- The "family of churches" block (Y1 days 282-296) already describes every tradition in its own words and ranks none — exactly the right model for the denomination-difference questions.
- The ethics and virtue days (Y4 Block C) are warm, concrete and non-shaming — keep as is.
- The "hardest questions" block (Y4 Block G) already covers doubt, church hurt, depression and grief with real warmth; it just needs some of its best material pulled earlier (see #2 and #5 above), not replaced.
