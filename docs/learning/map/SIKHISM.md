# Five-year map: Sikhism

Step 1 only — a plan to review. Nothing in `docs/curriculum/sikhism` is touched here.

## 1. What the research says this audience needs

1. **Meaning before vocabulary, script or dates.** The audience already lives Sikhi (gurdwara, langar, kara, "Waheguru") but can't translate what they say or do; gurdwara Khalsa-school order (Punjabi/Gurmukhi/history first) is exactly what didn't stick. (`AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_SIKHISM.md` §3, §"what to avoid early")
2. **"Can I still be Sikh with cut hair?" is the single biggest identity question**, asked constantly and usually with guilt; the best answers welcome, never rule. (`research-extra/REDDIT.md` §6, `REDDIT_REPLIES.md` §7)
3. **Explaining yourself to outsiders is a daily skill this audience needs**: "is that a hat?", "what's under there?", gang-color myths, kirpan-as-weapon/TSA questions. (`AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_SIKHISM.md` §2, §4)
4. **Being mistaken for Muslim, and the post-9/11 family memory of hate crimes**, is a live, painful thread, now compounded by a fresh wave of attacks topping r/Sikh in 2026 (a UK bus-driver attack drew 1,192 comments, the most-discussed post in the whole research set). (`AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_SIKHISM.md` §2, §4; `research-extra/REDDIT_TRENDING.md` §6)
5. **Caste is a live wound, not just history.** A 2026 news story (family tried to kill a daughter for marrying a "lower caste" Sikh) drew replies split between "it's culture, not Sikhi" and "it's a Sikh problem whether we like it or not." (`research-extra/REDDIT_ROUND2.md` §7)
6. **The language gap is shame, not just inconvenience.** Most of an estimated 20 million Sikhs worldwide can't speak Punjabi or read Gurmukhi fluently; this is the majority experience, and essayists describe real shame about it. (`AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_SIKHISM.md` §2, §3)
7. **Words said constantly, never translated**: Waheguru, the Mool Mantar, Ardas, Japji Sahib — taught "meaning first, word second, date last." (`AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_SIKHISM.md` §1, §"how the best creators hook people")
8. **Story over doctrine, with a diverse cast.** The Panj Pyare of 1699 (a scholar, farmer, water-carrier, printer, barber) is the most-cited "start here" story across camp curricula. (`AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_SIKHISM.md` §"how the best creators hook")
9. **Show the physical practice, don't just explain it.** Turban-tying tutorials (1M views/24h) outperform lecture; "practice makes perfect, nobody should judge your pagg" is the warmest thread in the whole round-2 sample. (`AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_SIKHISM.md` §1; `research-extra/REDDIT_ROUND2.md` §7)
10. **Fake "photos" of the Gurus and AI-made content are spreading**, and the community itself is asking for more critical thinking. New in 2026; not in older research. (`research-extra/REDDIT_ROUND2.md` §7, §"new in 2025-26")
11. **Nitnem feels too long to start**; people want a step-in path, not the full hour. (`research-extra/REDDIT.md` §6)
12. **1984 and Khalistan are real, painful, and actively discussed in Sikh youth organizing** — handle later, with care, no side-taking; never in a first week. (`AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_SIKHISM.md` §"honest gaps", §"what to avoid early"; `research-extra/REDDIT_TRENDING.md` §6)
13. **Langar and seva are what outsiders remember and love** — the single easiest, least controversial "hook" moment for anyone, Sikh or not. (`research-extra/THREADS_AND_COMMENTS.md` §6)
14. **Meat/vegetarianism and alcohol are recurring practical questions** people want answered plainly, without implying one answer is "more Sikh." (`research-extra/REDDIT.md` §6)
15. **Welcome beats gatekeeping, every time it was tested.** The most-upvoted replies in Hindu, Sikh and Buddhist threads welcomed; "you're not a real Sikh" replies were downvoted. (`research-extra/REDDIT_REPLIES.md` §"patterns across all paths")

## 3. Proposed changes, ranked by impact

1. **Move an explicit "can I still be Sikh?" reassurance to week 1 or very early year 1.** What: a short, warm day answering "I cut my hair / haven't taken amrit / don't know Punjabi — am I still Sikh?" using the Rehat Maryada's belief-based definition and "sehajdhari, keshdhari, amritdhari — all welcome," without ever saying "not a real Sikh." Where: new day inside week 1 (after day 18, five Ks) or as y1 day ~19–20, pulling forward the substance now sitting at day 272. Why: the single most-repeated, most guilt-laden identity question across every Reddit source; currently answered 80% through year 1. (`REDDIT.md`, `REDDIT_REPLIES.md` §7) Size: small edit (rewrite one day, cross-reference day 272 so it doesn't just repeat).

2. **Write the "explaining yourself to outsiders" script as its own moment**, not just naming the objects. What: a day (or two) giving the actual words for "is that a hat?", "is it a knife?", "is that a gang color?" — reassurance plus a ready phrase, modeled on the Sikh Coalition FAQ and the TikTok educators' approach. Where: right after week 1 day 3 (turban+kara) or as y1 days 268–269 (kara, kirpan), adding the outsider-question angle to what's already there. Why: this audience names "explaining my own faith" as a skill they need and don't have. (`AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_SIKHISM.md` §2, §"how the best creators hook") Size: small edit to 2 existing days.

3. **Add a short, careful "when Sikhs are targeted" day, earlier than day 320.** What: name that Sikhs are one of the most-targeted religious groups in several countries, that this has gotten worse in 2026, and respond with chardi kala (rising spirits) rather than fear — paired with concrete grounding (what to do, who to tell) rather than just history. Where: pull part of y1 day 320–321 earlier, perhaps into Camp 3's "chardi kala" day (y1 day 17 in week 1, or near day 150s), and add one more day in year 2's "hard questions" strand for the 2026 wave specifically. Why: this is now the single most-discussed topic on r/Sikh (1,192-comment post), not a background fact. (`research-extra/REDDIT_TRENDING.md` §6) Size: small addition, one new day in year 2.

4. **Add a language-gap reassurance day.** What: name directly that most Sikhs worldwide can't read Gurmukhi fluently, that this app teaches sound-then-meaning on purpose, and that not speaking Punjabi doesn't make anyone less Sikh. Where: new day in week 1 (near day 10–11, Mool Mantar) or early y1 (near day 157, "the Guru's alphabet"). Why: a near-universal, quietly embarrassing experience for this audience that the product's own design already solves but never says out loud. (`AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_SIKHISM.md` §2, §4) Size: small edit, one new day.

5. **Write a "first turban, be gentle with yourself" day**, separate from the factual dastar day. What: the "looks good, brother / practice makes perfect / nobody should judge your pagg" warmth from the Reddit thread, as its own beat — turban-tying as a forgiving skill you grow into, not a test you pass or fail. Where: pair with or replace part of y4 day 1266 or y5 day 1647 (dastar bandi); could also echo back to week 1 day 3. Why: this is the "most wholesome thread" found in the round-2 research and matches the Sunny Osahn content that performs best for this audience. (`research-extra/REDDIT_ROUND2.md` §7) Size: small edit/rewrite of 1 day.

6. **Add a "can you trust that image?" day about fake Guru "photos" and AI content.** What: cameras didn't exist until the 1820s; Sikh teaching also holds God is formless, so even the idea of a "photo of the Guru" is worth questioning; treat devotional art honestly without calling any of it a real likeness. Where: new day, natural fit in year 4's "Sikh thought" block (around days 1362–1419) or year 2's online-life addition. Why: a 2026-trending topic the outline doesn't touch at all, and ties to the broader "online life" lesson recommended for every path. (`research-extra/REDDIT_ROUND2.md` §7, §"new in 2025-26") Size: small addition, one new day.

7. **Move the plain, practical meat/alcohol answer earlier.** What: pull the substance of y4 days 1291/1353 (many Sikhs are vegetarian, others not; the code forbids only ritually slaughtered meat; "different plates") into year 1 or year 2, closer to when the five Ks and rehat are first introduced (y1 days 264–273). Why: an evergreen, frequently asked practical question shouldn't wait until 70% through the curriculum. (`research-extra/REDDIT.md` §6) Size: small edit (move/duplicate two days).

8. **Give the 2026 caste news story a dated, honest echo at y3 day 1036.** What: keep the existing "Gurus rejected caste, yet caste still shows" day, but add the plain admission already modeled in the Reddit reply ("it's a Sikh problem whether we like it or not") so the lesson doesn't read as purely historical. Where: y3 day 1036 (no new day needed). Why: research flags caste as "still alive, including in the diaspora," not a solved historical matter. (`research-extra/REDDIT_ROUND2.md` §7) Size: small edit, same day.

**Recurring patterns to apply across the year:**
- A **"you're not behind" moment** wherever cut hair, amrit, or Punjabi fluency come up (already the right instinct at y1 day 272 and y5 day 1658 — extend the same voice to the new earlier days above).
- A **"Sikhs differ, and that's fine" framing** for sehajdhari/keshdhari/amritdhari, meat, and turban styles — never present one as more correct.
- The **"explain it to a friend" beat**, giving an actual sentence to say back, for every outsider-facing topic (turban, kirpan, "are you Hindu/Muslim").
- The **week-one style** (lived moment → plain meaning → word → date, if at all) should keep shaping new days added anywhere in years 1–5, not just the first 21.

## 4. Sensitive ground

- **1984, Khalistan, Operation Blue Star** (y1 days 306–312): already handled with real care ("go slowly," no side-taking). Keep it there; a Keeper with real expertise in this history must review this week specifically before it's shown to anyone, given how recent and contested it remains in Sikh communities.
- **Caste** (y1 days 50/85/248, y3 day 1036, y5 day 1728): doctrinally simple (the Gurus rejected it) but socially live; any added "caste still happens" framing needs a Keeper check so it reads as an honest admission, not an accusation against any group or region.
- **Cut hair / amrit / who "counts" as Sikh**: not politically sensitive but personally loaded; every day touching it needs the same Keeper eye for tone that a crisis-care line would get — warmth, no ruling, no "test."
- **Hate crimes and the 2026 attack wave**: needs a Keeper (or the owner) to confirm language that's accurate without being frightening, and to keep any new day pointing toward grounding and chardi kala rather than fear or grievance.
- **Gender identity and kesh** (seen in one Reddit thread): outside scope for an early lesson; if it ever comes up in the Guide, answer with warmth and no ruling, per the existing playbook.

## 5. What stays

Week 1's order (Waheguru → langar → turban/kara → Panj Pyare → Ardas → Sikh/Hindu/Muslim → Khanda/Vaisakhi) is exactly right and shouldn't move. The "sound before meaning" design for Gurbani (Roman letters, then translation) across Camp 4's Japji walkthrough is a real strength and matches what diaspora audiences say they wished for. The 1984 week's careful framing is a model for how to handle hard history elsewhere. The Bhagats block (Kabir, Farid) already threads the "truth has many singers" theme the research points to.

## 6. Estimated size of the revision

Mostly small, surgical edits: roughly 6–8 individual days rewritten or added across years 1, 2 and 4 (none in year 3 or 5 need more than the one-line caste tweak), plus moving the substance of 2–3 existing days earlier rather than cutting anything. No camp or block needs restructuring, and no week needs a full rewrite. This is a light pass, not a curriculum overhaul — call it half a day of focused writing once the owner signs off on the list above.
