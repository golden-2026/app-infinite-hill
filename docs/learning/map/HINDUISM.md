# Five-year map: Hinduism

Step 1 only — a plan to review. Nothing in `docs/curriculum/hinduism` or the outline file is touched here.

## 1. What the research says this audience needs

1. They already *do* Hinduism (79% keep a home shrine) but don't know the *why* behind it — "nobody ever told us what it means" is the single strongest emotional comment across sources. (`AUDIENCE_RESEARCH.md` summary; `research-extra/THREADS_AND_COMMENTS.md` Gayatri video comment, 630 likes)
2. Their questions are "why do we…" about things seen at home, not questions about texts or schools of thought. (`AUDIENCE_RESEARCH.md` §2, 53 sourced questions)
3. "Why so many gods, which one is mine" is the single most-repeated worry, including a specific fear that other gods will be "upset" if you focus on one. (`AUDIENCE_RESEARCH.md` top-20 #1; `research-extra/REDDIT.md` #5–7; `research-extra/REDDIT_REPLIES.md` §1)
4. Devotional art and "is my offering/idol good enough" is a live, emotional worry, best answered with Gita 9.26 (a leaf, a flower, a fruit or water with love) and "Ganesha isn't an art critic." (`research-extra/REDDIT_ROUND2.md` §1, top-upvoted thread this round)
5. **Colorism in depictions of the gods is new and large this year** — "how do you feel about media depictions of Krishna or Vishnu?" was the most-discussed r/hinduism post (178 comments); people want the plain fact that Krishna and Rama are described dark-skinned. (`research-extra/REDDIT_ROUND2.md` §1 and "what's new")
6. Chanting without meaning is the generation's specific complaint — Sunday schools and home life teach the sound, not the sense (Gayatri, Hanuman Chalisa, shlokas). (`AUDIENCE_RESEARCH.md` §3; `research-extra/THREADS_AND_COMMENTS.md` §1)
7. Weekly diaspora programs (Bala Vihar, temple Sunday school) front-load deity stories, Ramayana and Hanuman in the early years and save the Gita for teens — the opposite of a "scholarly first" order. (`AUDIENCE_RESEARCH.md` §3)
8. Caste comes up as a hard, specific, outside-pressure moment: a US high-schooler "can't defend caste" to classmates, and it's a top criticism editors flag for youth. (`AUDIENCE_RESEARCH.md` §2 #52; `research-extra/REDDIT.md` #19–20)
9. Periods and temple/puja access is one of the most-asked questions from young women raised Hindu, and it's about *home rules*, not scripture — needs a careful, warm answer. (`research-extra/REDDIT.md` summary point 3)
10. Meat, beef and the cow are repeat identity questions, especially for people living outside India. (`AUDIENCE_RESEARCH.md` §2 #37–38; `research-extra/REDDIT.md` #14–15)
11. Wedding and life-cycle rituals (pheras, sindoor, mangalsutra, kundli matching, mundan) are things people take part in with no explanation, and research explicitly recommends a Year 1 "what the wedding rituals mean" lesson. (`AUDIENCE_RESEARCH.md` §2 #42–46; `research-extra/REDDIT_ROUND2.md` §1 "what this means")
12. Small bodily/home habits — the dot on the forehead (tilak/bindi), touching a book to the forehead after stepping on it, the red thread, the black dot on babies — are near-universal childhood memories with no attached explanation. (`AUDIENCE_RESEARCH.md` §2 #5, #31, #34; top-20 #9, #13, #17)
13. Identity-under-pressure (anti-South-Asian racism, politics, "is this Hindu nationalism") is rising fast in the diaspora community this year and needs dignity-not-hate framing, no politics. (`research-extra/REDDIT_ROUND2.md` §1 "handle with care"; §9 cross-path pattern 2)
14. "Can I be Hindu and not sure about God / is Hinduism one rulebook" — people want permission that doubt and many lineages are normal, not a crisis. (`AUDIENCE_RESEARCH.md` §2 #22; `research-extra/REDDIT_REPLIES.md` §1 "what this means")
15. Simple modern metaphors land hard (Brahman as "the server", soul as "the player account", karma as "in-game actions") — a sign that analogy-first teaching beats term-first teaching for this crowd. (`research-extra/THREADS_AND_COMMENTS.md` §1)

## 3. Proposed changes, ranked by impact

1. **Add a short Krishna-and-the-gods'-colors week to Camp 2.** What: a week on who Krishna is (birth, Govardhan, the flute, why he's blue/dark) *and* why Hindu gods are shown in different colors and skin tones, closing with "how should movies show the gods?" as a discussion game, not a lecture. Where: insert as a new Week 11.5, days 71–77 currently hold "Mahabharata cliffhangers"; add the Krishna/colors week right after Hanuman (days 29–35) and before the Ramayana block, pushing Ramayana/Mahabharata/festival-buffer back by 7 days (new total Camp 2 length 103 days instead of 96 — a one-week stretch). Why: this is this year's single most-discussed r/hinduism post (178 comments) and sits dead last in the current outline (Year 3). Size: new week (7 days).
2. **Add a devotional-art / "is my offering enough" day early in Camp 2 or 3.** What: Gita 9.26 taught as a single warm verse (not part of the full Camp 4 Gita study), paired with "Ganesha isn't an art critic." Where: fits naturally at the end of the Ganesha week (day 28 already closes on modak/prasad) — add one day, renumbering the week to 8 days or swapping out a lighter day. Why: the single top-upvoted Reddit thread this round; matches the owner's "light start" rule since it's one verse, not textual study. Size: small edit (1 day).
3. **Add a wedding-and-life-rituals week.** What: pheras, sindoor, mangalsutra, mundan, kundli matching, explained as "things you've probably already seen" — matches the research's explicit Year 1 recommendation. Where: Camp 3 (Practices, days 97–156) has room after "Body and food" (125–131) and before "Temple and community" (132–138); insert as new days, or fold into a widened "Body, food and life passages" week. Why: explicitly named as a Year 1 gap by two research rounds; currently absent from the entire five-year outline. Size: small rewrite (one week, 7 days).
4. **Add a "things you grew up with, explained" week: tilak/bindi, book-to-forehead, red thread, baby's black dot, nazar.** Where: same stretch as #3, Camp 3 days 97–156, or merged with the Ganesha/Hanuman stories as quick "did you know" bonus days. Why: near-universal, funny, low-sensitivity childhood memories named repeatedly in research (top-20 #9, #13, #17) with zero current coverage. Size: small rewrite (one week, 7 days).
5. **Move a light caste "how to explain it to a friend" card earlier, out of Year 5.** What: not the critical Manusmriti study (keep that in Year 5 for mature readers) — a short, honest, Keeper-reviewed line for a teen asked about caste at school: what it was supposed to be, what it became, and "you don't have to defend what you didn't choose." Where: Camp 5's "Neighbors and the house" week (days 323–329) is the natural home — it already sits at the end of Year 1 and is about explaining the faith outward. Why: a US-high-schooler's need, flagged as urgent in research, currently addressed only at day ~1450+. Size: small edit (reframe 1–2 days of the existing week).
6. **Pull the diaspora "dignity, not hate" identity content out of Year 5 and seed it earlier.** What: a short Year 1 or Year 2 beat (not a deep dive) on being Hindu abroad when people are unkind, with no politics, no "whose civilization is greater" framing. Where: pair with change #5 in the Camp 5 "Neighbors" week, or as a Year 2 Block D festival-track addition (days 670–696). Why: identity-under-pressure is rising sharply *this year* in the diaspora data; Year 5 is roughly four years too late for a need this current. Size: small edit.
7. **Reinforce "many gods, one truth" after week 1, not just at day 6.** What: a short callback day somewhere in Camp 2 or 3 restating ishta devata with the "rain reaching the ocean" verse, since the Reddit data shows this fear (will other gods be upset) recurs past the first week. Where: good fit at the close of the Ganesha/Hanuman run, before the Ramayana's many deities appear. Why: research shows this worry resurfaces, not a one-time question. Size: small edit (1 day).
8. **Flag periods-and-puja as a Guide topic, not a lesson day.** What: add to the Guide/companion playbook (not the curriculum) a warm, non-judgmental answer that this is a home-rule question, families differ, and it isn't scripture-mandated exclusion. Where: `docs/GUIDE_PLAYBOOK.md` / `tests/guide-cases/cases.json`, outside this map's scope but worth flagging to the owner. Why: research is explicit this needs warmth and care, and it's a question people ask privately, which fits the Guide better than a public lesson. Size: small edit, different doc.

## 4. Sensitive ground

- **Caste.** Teach what it was supposed to mean and what it became, honestly, without defending it or shaming the asker; keep the critical read of the Manusmriti in Year 5 as already planned; the Keeper must review any wording used earlier (change #5).
- **Colorism and media depictions of the gods.** Say plainly that Krishna, Rama, Draupadi and Arjuna are described dark-skinned in the texts; don't moralize about any individual movie or artist; Keeper review before publishing the new Camp 2 week (#1).
- **Periods and temple access.** Present as a home-rule variance, never as scripture's final word; no shaming either direction; route to the Guide per #8.
- **Politics and nationalism.** Ram Mandir, "Hindu civilization" pride posts, Zohran Mamdani-style news, anti-South-Asian racism: honor temples and festivals and dignity, never a political project or a claim of national/civilizational superiority. Keep out of lesson text entirely; if the identity-under-pressure beat (#6) ships, a Keeper must check the line doesn't read as grievance politics.
- **Devotional depth (rasa lila, Tantra, Vijnana Bhairava)** — already flagged by the Keeper's open questions in the Year 2–5 doc; no change needed here, just confirming the existing flag stands.
- **Uttara Kanda (Sita's second exile)** — already an open Keeper question in the existing plan; this map doesn't resolve it, just notes it's unresolved.
- **Astrology mixed into practice, Sai Baba's status, meat-eating** — all came up as live Reddit disagreements with no consensus; lessons should note "families differ" rather than rule.

## 5. What stays

- The week 1 rebuild (Ganesha, om, Gayatri, namaste/feet, arti/prasad, ishta devata, karma) already matches the top research hooks almost exactly — keep as is.
- Camp 2's deity-stories-before-texts order (Ganesha → Hanuman → Ramayana → Mahabharata, days 22–96) matches how diaspora Sunday schools actually work and how young adults say they learned.
- Camp 4's full, chapter-by-chapter Gita (days 157–231) is a real strength — no other mainstream app does this; keep its depth and pacing.
- Year 2's seven Upanishads and full Yoga Sutras (days 332–489) are thorough and well-paced; no changes proposed.
- The never-rank-traditions framing already built into Years 3–4 (the six darshanas, Advaita/Dvaita as electives) is exactly right and should carry over into the new, earlier material too.

## 6. Estimated size of the revision

Concentrated almost entirely in **Year 1** (Camps 2 and 3): one new week (Krishna and the gods' colors, #1), two more new or widened weeks (wedding rituals #3, everyday childhood habits #4), and five small single- or two-day edits (#2, #5, #6, #7, plus the Guide flag #8 which isn't curriculum at all). Roughly 21–28 of Year 1's 331 days are touched or added — about 7–8% of year one, well under 1% of the full five-year path. Years 2–5 need no structural change; Year 3's existing Krishna-childhood block (Bhagavata Purana) stays as a deeper second pass once the Year 1 introduction exists. Nothing here requires touching Camp 4's Gita or Year 2's Upanishads/Yoga Sutras blocks.
