# Five-year map: CATHOLIC

Step 1 only — a plan to review. Nothing in `docs/curriculum/catholic` is edited here.

## 1. What the research says this audience needs

1. The "real presence" belief gap is the single biggest known miss: only 31% of US Catholics believe the Church's own
   teaching that the bread and wine become Christ's body and blood; 69% call it symbolic, despite years of First
   Communion prep. (`AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_CATHOLIC.md` §Summary, §2)
2. Mortal sin / scrupulosity is a named fear keeping lapsed Catholics away: "can I still go to Mass if I missed it on
   vacation," "I'd have to confess first." Our research hadn't surfaced this word before this pass. (`REDDIT.md` §3
   "Patterns we hadn't seen" #1)
3. Confession nerves hit twice: before ("terrified, what do I say") and after ("it was awkward, I didn't feel
   anything"). Both moments need words, not just the how-to. (`REDDIT.md` §3 #2)
4. Cradle Catholics can't explain their own practices to others — one was told by their own parent that Catholics
   "worship" saints. The honor-vs-worship distinction is needed for people raised in the faith, not just outsiders.
   (`REDDIT.md` §3 #3; `AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_CATHOLIC.md` Q15–18; `REDDIT_ROUND2.md` §4 "Mary was sinless")
5. The rosary, one decade (~5 min), is the most-asked practice how-to and a natural daily unit. (`REDDIT.md` §3 #4;
   `AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_CATHOLIC.md` Q3–4)
6. "Welcome home" is the single most reassuring phrase for returners and converts, repeated across two rounds of
   Reddit research. First-Mass nerves ("the hardest part is walking through the door," don't receive Communion until
   received into the Church) are a real, common moment. (`REDDIT_ROUND2.md` §4 "first Sunday Mass ever"; `REDDIT_TRENDING.md`
   §3 "Converts arriving")
7. Converts are loud and rising: Gen Z Catholic identification rose from 15% to 21% in a year; conversions from Islam
   and Judaism show up specifically in this research. Welcome-the-convert content should not frame their old path as
   something to escape, since Muslim/Jewish learners read this app too. (`AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_CATHOLIC.md` §Summary;
   `REDDIT_ROUND2.md` §4(d))
8. Immaculate Conception vs. Virgin Birth, and purgatory vs. limbo, are the two most commonly confused term-pairs —
   worth untangling gently, never as gotcha trivia. (`AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_CATHOLIC.md` Q19, Q22–24; "What to avoid")
9. Liturgical colors (why the priest's outfit changes) are a widely-asked, rarely-answered everyday mystery.
   (`AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_CATHOLIC.md` Q6, "Top 20" #13)
10. Confirmation names are a fading practice some young Catholics skip without knowing why it exists — a milestone
    moment worth restoring meaning to. (`AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_CATHOLIC.md` Q7–8, "Top 20" #7)
11. Hispanic and Filipino family practice (quinceañera as sacrament-adjacent milestone, padrinos as lifelong
    mentors, Simbang Gabi, the Santo Niño) is a large shared well of "explain my childhood" moments not yet in the
    outline by name. (`AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_CATHOLIC.md` §2 Q37–40, §4)
12. Why people leave (losing belief 46%, clergy scandal 39%, Church's social/political positions 37%) and why people
    stay or return (spiritual fulfillment 54%, belief in teaching 53%, meaning 48%) — not to be resolved by the app,
    but the knowledge-gap lever (items 1–11 above) is squarely in scope and is the one lever within reach.
    (`AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_CATHOLIC.md` §3, Pew 2025)
13. Comedy-first, personal-story delivery outperforms doctrine-first delivery with this audience: the top Catholic
    TikTok/IG accounts hook with parish-life comedy, song parodies, or a convert's own story, and the teaching rides
    in on the joke. (`SOCIAL.md` §Catholicism; `AUDIENCE_RESEARCH_CATHOLIC.md` §"How the best creators hook people")
14. A "faith and science" lesson (Genesis read alongside evolution, young-earth creationism separated from
    mainstream Catholic teaching) helped at least one real person return to belief — currently absent from year 1.
    (`REDDIT_REPLIES.md` line ~869, ~892)
15. The Eucharist is the most-commented topic on r/Catholicism all year (1,080 comments on one thread) — confirms
    item 1's priority and shows the audience is actively debating it, not just passively confused.
    (`REDDIT_TRENDING.md` §3)
16. Eucharistic miracles (Lanciano, Sokółka) get cited online as "proof" of the Real Presence — a devotional
    tradition the Church allows but doesn't require; needs careful framing as devotion, not evidence, whenever it
    comes up. (`REDDIT_ROUND2.md` §4 "Flag")
17. Market gap: Hallow owns guided prayer but reviewers explicitly ask for *written transcripts and meaning*, not
    just audio to follow along — "why Mass, saints, sacraments, rosary, for those who did them as kids" is named as
    the missing product. (`APP_REVIEWS.md` §Hallow, §Per app "Missing")

## 4. Sensitive ground

- **Mortal sin / scrupulosity (change #1):** needs a Keeper's review before going live — getting the "it's not
  automatically mortal sin" line theologically precise matters, and the app must never sound like it's loosening
  real moral teaching to be reassuring.
- **Abortion and other life issues:** real Catholic teaching, cited as a reason both people leave (37% cite Church's
  social/political positions) and a reason online arguments get heated (`REDDIT_ROUND2.md` §4(d)). Teach with
  compassion, never as a culture-war post; very young users read r/Catholicism-adjacent content, so tone with teens
  must stay gentle. Needs Keeper review before any explicit treatment (not proposed for year 1 here).
- **Clergy abuse scandal:** a top-cited reason for leaving (39%). Not proposed for any near-term change above; if
  ever addressed, it needs its own careful, separately-reviewed treatment per the brief's owner rules, not folded
  into a "why some leave" day casually.
- **Converts from Islam/Judaism:** real and rising (change #7's neighborhood), but must never frame the other faith
  as something escaped, since this app's Muslim and Jewish learners read the same copy. Any convert-story content
  needs a plain check that it reads respectfully to those other doors too.
- **Eucharistic miracles (change #9):** devotional framing only, never proof — flagged above.
- **Regional/ethnic family practice (change #4):** say "in many families…", never present quinceañera, Simbang Gabi,
  or any one group's custom as the single Catholic experience.
- **Latin Mass vs. the newer Mass:** if it ever comes up, describe neutrally; it's a live, sometimes heated
  distinction in the research (`REDDIT_TRENDING.md` §3).

## 5. What stays

- Year 1's shape — walk in the door (camp 1), then stories (camp 2), then practices (camp 3), then text (camp 4),
  then depths (camp 5) — already matches what the research says works: object/moment first, term second, comedy and
  story as the hook.
- The Real Presence, honor-vs-worship, and the rosary (the three biggest named needs) are already covered well and
  early; no structural change needed there, just the smaller additions above.
- Liturgical colors land at the right spot and depth already (day 143).
- Years 2–5's shift into scripture-in-order, church history, the Catechism in full, the schools of prayer, and the
  primary-source classics is a sound deepening arc and doesn't need rework for this pass — the fixes above are
  concentrated in year 1, where the audience research is concentrated.

## 6. Estimated size of the revision

Mostly small, additive edits inside year 1: roughly 8–10 days either added or expanded (changes #1–#7, #9) out of
year 1's 331 days, plus a light tone pass across camp 5 part 5's ~41-day Catechism run (change #8, spot-checking
rather than rewriting). No new camps, no week renumbering beyond absorbing one or two inserted days. Years 2–5
untouched. Two items (mortal sin framing, any life-issues content) need Keeper review before they can go live; the
rest are plain-language additions that fit the existing outline's slots.
