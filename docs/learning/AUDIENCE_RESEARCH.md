# Audience research: young Hindu Americans (18–35)

Researched 6 October 2026 from public web pages only. No logins, no posting, nothing submitted.

## Summary

Young Hindu Americans already *do* a lot of Hinduism: 79% of Asian American Hindus keep a home shrine
([Pew 2023](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2023/10/11/hinduism-among-asian-americans/)), they light diyas, tie
rakhis, touch elders' feet, chant the Gayatri mantra and go to weddings with pheras. What they lack is the *why*. Their
questions are almost all "why do we…" questions about things they've seen at home ("why does every puja start with
Ganesha?", "why do we touch a book to our forehead if we step on it?", "why so many gods?"), not questions about texts or
schools of philosophy. The audiences that grow fastest are reached through **stories and characters** (Krishna, Hanuman,
Ganesha, Karna), short animated or face-to-camera clips, and warm, emotional framing; the biggest English-language
teachers (Gaur Gopal Das, Jay Shetty) wrap the Gita in everyday problems and humor. Diaspora Sunday schools start with
deities, festivals, Ramayana and simple prayers, and save the Gita for teenagers; young adults say temple services felt
"tedious" and "repetitive" and only "found their roots" in college through peers, Diwali, bhajans and Gita discussion
([HSC/UIC study](https://www.hindustudentscouncil.org/uic-research-study-credits-hsc-with-successfully-affirming-a-spiritual-hindu-identity-among-american-youth/)).
So the first lessons should start from a moment in their own life, explain it in a minute with a story, and only later
name the text it comes from.

**Honest gaps up front**

- **Reddit was completely blocked** (r/ABCDesis, r/hinduism, r/IndianAmerican): Reddit refuses this research tool both
  for fetching and for search. I did not try to get around that. The "their own words" questions below come mainly from
  Quora question titles (often asked by people in India, not only Americans), youth-specific sources (Chicago temple
  teens, Hindu Speakers Bureau, campus studies) and essays. A person reading Reddit by hand for an hour would strengthen
  this a lot.
- **Instagram and TikTok engagement was not visible.** I could see follower counts and bios on Instagram, but not likes,
  comments or which posts did best. TikTok pages returned nothing. YouTube showed subscriber counts and recent titles,
  and view counts only for some Shorts.
- Several articles refused access (403): Pluralism Project pieces on Hindu youth, Religion News Service on campus Hindu
  houses (Feb 2026), Beliefnet, Patheos. I used their search-result summaries only where noted.
- YouTube comment threads could not be read.
- "How often it comes up" rankings are my judgment from how many separate sources raise each topic, not a count.

  games ([Hindu Temple Albany](https://www.hindutemplealbany.org/cultural-center/balavihar/)).
- Order of topics ([CM San Jose](https://www.cmsj.org/bala-vihar/curriculum/); [CM Pittsburgh](https://www.cmpittsburgh.org/balavihar-curriculum); summary via search):
  - **Pre-K:** forms of God, daily prayers, bhajans, crafts.
  - **K:** "Alphabet Safari" (A is for Aspiration…; one temple version runs A = AUM to Y = Yajna).
  - **Grade 1:** Bala Ramayana. **Grade 2:** Hanuman and the Hanuman Chalisa. **Grade 3:** Bala Bhagavatam
    (Krishna stories). **Grade 5:** symbolism in Hinduism / Hanuman. **Grades 7–8:** Mahabharata.
  - **Grade 9:** Hindu culture and samskaras (rites of passage). **Grades 10–12:** Bhagavad Gita.
- In short, **the first year is gods' forms, prayers, songs, and the Ramayana and Hanuman stories.** Philosophy (the
  Gita) waits until the teens.

### ISKCON youth and college Bhakti clubs

- Weekly: kirtan (singing), a Gita class and discussion, a round of japa, and shared food (prasadam). One club's year
  opens with "what yoga is and isn't", then "you are not the body but the soul"
  ([Back to Godhead](https://btg.krishna.com/teaching-bhakti-in-a-university-town/)).

### Temple Sunday schools

- Topics: deities, festivals, "cultural cues", Puranas, epics, saints, values, temple worship; very young kids get
  visual, hands-on learning, older kids discussion ([NY Ganesh Temple](https://patasala.nyganeshtemple.org/religion-2/); [DFW Hindu Temple](https://vv.dfwhindutemple.org/religion/)).
- Kindergartners learn chants "with emphasis on… learning the meanings in English"; a coordinator says materials are
  usually "too simple or too complex"; a teacher says "I didn't have this as a Hindu growing up in the US"
  ([Hinduism Today, 2004](https://www.hinduismtoday.com/magazine/july-august-september-2004/2004-07-kids-love-hindu-sunday-school/)).

### Hindu Students Council (college)

- Weekly study circles that "talk about a little portion of the Vedas or the Gita and see how it pertains to our life";
  Diwali, bhajans, service; a "Dharma Dialogues" series opened with "why Hinduism has sooo many Gods"
  ([HSC](https://www.hindustudentscouncil.org/resources/); search summary).
- A UIC study of HSC members: in high school, temple services felt **"tedious" and "repetitive"**; students say "As I
  came to college I found my roots again". What helped most: Gita study about life's purpose, Diwali and bhajan
  sessions, service projects, and simply having peers
  ([HSC/UIC](https://www.hindustudentscouncil.org/uic-research-study-credits-hsc-with-successfully-affirming-a-spiritual-hindu-identity-among-american-youth/)).
- An older Boston University survey: among Indian Americans who'd change their upbringing, **50% wanted more exposure
  to their culture and religion** and 30% wished they'd learned an Indian language
  ([BU survey](https://people.bu.edu/prothero/survey1.html), via search summary).
- Note: HSC has drawn political criticism from some quarters ([Skill Nation](https://skillnation.in/posts/hindu-students-council/));
  noting only.

### Hindu American Foundation education resources

- "Hinduism Basics" goes: oneness and pluralism → six schools of thought → sources (Shruti/Smriti) → historical
  contributions → influence on America ([HAF](https://www.hinduamerican.org/hinduism-basics)). That's a
  teacher/outsider order, and much more "scholarly" than what young Hindus ask for.
- Its animated explainers and festival toolkits (Holi, Diwali) are closer to what this audience wants
  ([HAF explainers](https://www.hinduamerican.org/the-feed/animated-explainers/); [Diwali toolkit](https://www.hinduamerican.org/diwali)).
- HAF is an advocacy group and parts of its feed are political (see Section 1).

### What young adults remember, and what they missed

- **Remember:** the stories (Ramayana, Hanuman, Krishna), the chants they can still recite, the Amar Chitra Katha
  comics ([Pritchett](https://franpritchett.com/00fwp/published/amarchitrakatha.pdf); [GeekMom](https://geekmom.com/2017/04/amar-chitra-katha-revisiting-indian-comic-books/)),
  Diwali and the home shrine.
- **Missed:** what the words mean; why the family does what it does; how to answer friends; how to make it their own as
  adults. Parents assumed kids would absorb it "by osmosis" and often they didn't
  ([Substack comments](https://thenextchaptr.substack.com/p/im-a-bad-hindu/comments)). Teachings were not tied to daily
  life, and language was a barrier
  ([Hinduism Today, Youth 2010](https://www.hinduismtoday.com/magazine/january-february-march-2010/2010-01-youth-exploring-the-hindu-american-identity/)).
- Memorizing without meaning is a deliberate traditional method ("the meaning comes later"), which helps explain why so
  many can chant but not explain ([Medium](https://medium.com/indian-thoughts/why-shlokas-are-part-of-my-childs-daily-routine-5e6ecd703703); [ISKCON Desire Tree](https://iskcondesiretree.com/profiles/blogs/slokas-why-memorize-them-1)).

## 4. Everyday "explain my childhood" moments

- **At the home shrine:** the morning lamp, ringing a small bell, aarti and waving hands over the flame, prasad you
  can't refuse, the Gayatri mantra, "Om Jai Jagdish Hare" (the most common aarti, made famous by a 1970 film,
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Om_Jai_Jagdish_Hare)), the Hanuman Chalisa when scared.
- **Body habits:** touching elders' feet, touching a book to your forehead after stepping on it, shoes off at the
  door and the temple, tilak/bindi, the red thread on the wrist (all sourced in Section 2).
- **Protection habits:** the black dot (kala tika) on babies, nazar rituals with chilies, lemon or mustard-oil
  cotton ([Quora](https://www.quora.com/Is-there-any-scientific-basis-behind-the-Indian-superstition-of-Nazar-lag-jana)).
- **Food:** no beef, vegetarian days, no onion or garlic on fast days or for bhog, fasting on certain weekdays.
- **Festivals:** Diwali (and why there are different stories for it in different regions,
  [Quora](https://www.quora.com/Why-do-we-have-Lakshmi-Puja-on-Diwali-since-Diwali-is-supposed-to-mark-the-return-of-Lord-Rama-to-his-Kingdom-Where-do-the-two-mythologies-intersect)),
  Holi, Navratri/garba, Raksha Bandhan, Janmashtami, Ganesh Chaturthi, Karva Chauth, Maha Shivaratri.
- **Weddings:** haldi, mehndi, sangeet, baraat, garlands, kanyadaan, the seven pheras around the fire, sindoor,
  mangalsutra, kundli matching ([The Knot](https://www.theknot.com/content/hindu-wedding-traditions); [Wikipedia: Sindoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindoor)).
- **Life events:** naming, the mundan haircut, the sacred thread ceremony, death rites (13 days, ashes in the Ganga).
- **Bollywood references:** Karva Chauth, sangeet dances and pheras as seen in films; heroes as
  Ram-like figures ([The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/friday-essay-bollywood-helped-make-me-now-it-projects-modis-indian-nationalism-263722)).
  Many first meet a ritual through a film scene, then wonder what it really means.
- **Outside the home:** yoga studios ending class with "namaste", friends asking "why the dot?", "do you worship
  cows?", "is that a swastika?".

## How the best creators hook people

1. **Start with a "why do we…" from their own home.** HAF titles are literal questions ("How Many Gods Do Hindus
   Believe In?"); Quora's most repeated titles are all "why do we…". Example opener: "You've touched a book to your
   forehead after stepping on it. Here's why."
2. **Tell a story with a character, not a concept.** Hindu Nuggets' whole feed is short devotee-and-God stories ("Shri
   Ram became the cook 😂"); Bala Vihar's first years are Ramayana and Hanuman; @youngndharmic leads with Karna.
3. **Use the myth-buster or surprise.** Project Shivoham's best Shorts: "Mahabharat = 19 Books, NOT just 18!" (77K),
   "Vimanas are NOT Aeroplanes!"; titles like "(Conditions Apply)".
4. **Tie old wisdom to a modern feeling.** Hindu Nuggets' paid Gen Z Gita: "main character energy", "hustle culture",
   loneliness; Gaur Gopal Das and Jay Shetty on stress and relationships.
5. **Use humor and the "Indian mom" frame.** Gaur Gopal Das's jokes; Zarna Garg's 100M+ TikTok views on Indian-mom
   life. The shared "my mom made me do this" laugh opens the door.
6. **Make it visual and short.** 1–2 minute clips (@youngndharmic), animation (HINDU STORIES 1.17M, Hindu Nuggets 3D),
   comics (Amar Chitra Katha, the comic Gita), emoji in titles.
7. **Give one thing to say or do.** The Sanskrit Channel's "learn one shloka" Shorts and morning chants; ISKCON's
   simple weekly rhythm of song, one verse, japa, food.
8. **Invite their questions.** Hindu Nuggets keeps a "Questions" highlight; Hindu Academy runs weekly live Q&A; HSC's
   series started from "why sooo many Gods".

## What to avoid early

- **Opening with scholarly names and systems:** "six schools of thought", Shruti vs Smriti, Advaita, Upanishad names,
  Sanskrit grammar. That's HAF's teacher order and Vedanta NY's long-lecture style; young adults call temple religion
  "tedious" and "repetitive".
- **Leading with the Bhagavad Gita as a text.** Every program the audience grew up with saves it for the teens; when
  creators use it early, it's dressed as life advice (Jay Shetty, the comic and Gen Z Gitas).
- **Chanting without meaning.** They already have that; it's the gap they feel.
- **Politics and grievance:** anti-Hindu narratives, Khalistan, left vs right, nationalism in films. Several popular
  accounts lean this way; it divides and isn't what they asked.
- **Caste, periods and temples, beef** as early lessons. They are real questions, but heavy; they need careful,
  later, reviewed answers.
- **"Scientific reasons" claims** (e.g. kalava "controls nerves", mundan for "vitamin D"), which are common on Quora
  and easy to debunk. Give the meaning and the story; don't oversell.
- **Shaming ("bad Hindu", "lost generation").** Commenters warn their "generation will be lost"; that tone pushes
  people away. Start from "you already do more than you think".
- **One "correct" regional version.** Diwali alone has Rama, Lakshmi and Krishna–Narakasura stories by region; say
  "in many families…".
- **Mixing up stories for effect** (e.g. Radha placed inside Gita conversations). It works on feelings, but our
  reviewed lessons should keep stories accurate.
