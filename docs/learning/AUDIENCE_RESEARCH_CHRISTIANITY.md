# Audience research: young American Christians (18–35), Protestant / non-denominational

Researched 6 October 2026 from public web pages only. No logins, no posting, nothing submitted. This path is
Protestant and non-denominational; Catholics have their own path, so Catholic-specific practice (confession, the
rosary, the Pope, saints) is noted only where it clarifies a Protestant contrast.

## Summary

This audience already has the vocabulary without the meaning: they can sing "This Little Light of Mine," name John
3:16, and recite the sinner's prayer, but when asked to explain the Trinity, why communion uses grape juice and
crackers, or what "born again" really means, many go quiet. Their real questions, drawn from GotQuestions.org's own
top-20 most-asked list ([gotquestions.org/top20.html](https://www.gotquestions.org/top20.html)), skew toward
*edges* — sex, hell, tattoos, speaking in tongues, dinosaurs — because the plain basics (who Jesus is, what the
cross means) were covered early and the edges were what Sunday school dodged. The biggest creators reaching this
audience split into two very different lanes: warm, comedic life-advice pastors with huge social followings
(Steven Furtick 17.4M combined reach, Mike Todd 2M+ Instagram) whose sermon clips get cut for TikTok, and a newer
wave of "data over dogma" scholars and ex-insiders (Dan McClellan, 1.4M+ combined followers) who built an audience
by calmly fact-checking Bible claims for people who no longer trust the pulpit. In between sits a large,
visible "exvangelical" and "deconstruction" movement (#PurityCulture: ~60M TikTok views) processing a specific
childhood: purity rings, altar calls, youth group lock-ins, and the fear that a parent could be "left behind" in
the Rapture while they stayed home. Family-facing teaching (Awana, the Gospel Project, VBS) actually starts gently
— songs, crafts, "God is..." — but kids say it didn't connect to real life and the hard questions got shut down. So
first lessons should open from a concrete memory (a VBS song, a Christianese phrase, a cultural reference) and
only later name the doctrine behind it.

**Honest gaps up front**

- **Reddit could not be read.** `r/Christianity`, `r/exchristian` and similar were unreachable both by direct fetch
  and by `site:reddit.com` search (the fetch returned "unable to fetch from www.reddit.com"; the search returned
  no Reddit posts). The "their own words" questions below come from GotQuestions.org's published top-20 list, Quora
  question titles, and Christianese glossary pages. A person reading `r/exchristian` or `r/Christianity` by hand for
  an hour would meaningfully strengthen this section.
- **Instagram and TikTok engagement (likes, comments, which posts did best) was not visible** except where a
  secondary article reported it (e.g., hashtag view counts, a specific viral post). Follower/subscriber counts were
  visible for most accounts.
- **YouTube comment threads could not be read.**
- Several claims about "what kids remember" come from curriculum-company or ministry blog posts, which have an
  interest in presenting their own programs favorably; I've flagged these as such.
- "How often it comes up" rankings are my judgment from how many separate sources raise each topic, not a count.
- Confirmation is mainly a Catholic and mainline-Protestant (Lutheran, Methodist, Episcopal) practice, not a
  Baptist/evangelical/non-denominational one; where sources mix the two I've noted it.

### The deconstruction / exvangelical movement, as a phenomenon

- A reviewer's caution worth taking seriously for our own content: on TikTok, "dozens of creators [are] confidently

  ([getfaithlock.com](https://www.getfaithlock.com/resources/christian-guide-to-tiktok)). This cuts both ways: it is
  a caution about *our own* lessons being too glib, not just about other creators.

### Reach note: YouVersion (the Bible app), for scale

- 1 billion+ installs total, 160M+ new installs in 2026, 18.5M daily users; a record single day of 19M users on
  Nov 2, 2025 ([YouVersion newsroom](https://www.youversion.com/news/record-breaking-millions-turn-to-scripture-in-the-new-year); [CBN](https://cbn.com/news/world/gods-word-very-alive-and-well-youversion-bible-passes-800-million-downloads-2000)).

  ([search summary](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2025/11/bible-reading-is-up-among-gen-z-millennials/)). This
  is the single largest "quiet," non-social surface this audience already uses daily — worth knowing about even
  though it's not a creator.

**Which posts look most engaged?** Not directly visible (see gaps). Indirectly: a single vulnerable confession
(Steingard) made national news; a satire account (PreachersNSneakers) went from 0 to 100K followers in a month by
puncturing pastor image; a scholar's calm fact-check format (McClellan) built nearly a million TikTok followers
faster than most ministry accounts. The pattern across all three: **debunking, vulnerability, and "actually, here's
what it really says" beat polished devotional content** for this specific, skeptical-of-institutions audience.

## 3. What families and congregations already use to teach

### Awana (the biggest cross-denominational children's/youth program)

- Ages 2–18, graded program: Puggles (2–3), Cubbies (preschool), **Sparks** (K–2), **Truth & Training/T&T** (3–6),
  Trek (middle school), Journey (high school) ([Awana Basics](https://awanabasics.awana.org/)).
- **Sparks (K–2) first year:** "basic truths about God, His Son, salvation and the Bible," built around Scripture
  memory ([Awana Basics curriculum overview](https://awanabasics.awana.org/wp-content/uploads/protected/2021/07/TandTCurriculumOverview.pdf)).
- **T&T (3–6):** two big units — "God Is..." (Creator, Holy, Just, Love, Eternal, Truth, All-Powerful, Everywhere,
  All-Knowing, Three-in-One, Savior, Hope, Advocate, Strength, Peace, Trustworthy, Loving, Faithful, Patient,
  Always-at-Work) and "The Bible..." (true and without error, helps us know God, has books of wisdom and prophecy,
  is God's inspired word, lights our path, is God's story) ([same source](https://awanabasics.awana.org/wp-content/uploads/protected/2021/07/TandTCurriculumOverview.pdf)).
- In short: **the first years are a vocabulary of God's attributes and simple truths about the Bible, taught through
  memorization**, not stories-first the way Hindu Sunday school leads with Ramayana.

### Orange / 252 Kids and The Gospel Project (the two biggest curriculum brands for churches)

- **The Gospel Project** runs "a Christ-centered, chronological journey through the entire Bible from Genesis to
  Revelation" in three-year cycles ([Lifeway](https://www.lifeway.com/en/shop/the-gospel-project)).
- **Orange** (First Look for preschool, 252 Kids for elementary, XP3 for middle/high school) is "more thematic and
  virtue-based" with age-appropriate framing rather than strict chronology ([Playlister comparison](https://www.playlister.app/blog/orange-curriculum-vs-gospel-project-which-is-better)).
- Net: **the two dominant philosophies are "whole Bible story in order" vs. "one virtue or theme at a time."**
  Neither leads with doctrine or church history; both lead with Bible *narrative*.

### Vacation Bible School (VBS) — the most universal shared memory

- A one-week summer program built around songs, crafts and a Bible story arc; songs like "This Little Light of
  Mine" and "I've Got the Joy, Joy, Joy Down in My Heart" are cited as some of the most cherished memories
  ([World Help](https://worldhelp.net/do-you-remember-this-song-from-vbs/)).
- A curriculum publisher's own account (so read with that bias in mind) says teenagers and young adults report that
  a VBS song "helped them recall Scripture they needed at the right time" years later ([Lifeway VBS blog](https://vbs.lifeway.com/2019/03/13/music-matters-how-vbs-songs-help-kids-learn-biblical-truth/)).
- Net: **songs and crafts are what sticks physically; the theology behind them is what fades.**

### Young Life and Fellowship of Christian Athletes (the biggest parachurch, non-curriculum entry points)

- **Young Life:** non-denominational, meets teens "where they are," structure is contact work → weekly club
  (singing, a skit, a short talk about Jesus) → small-group "Campaigners" Bible study → camp ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Life); [GotQuestions](https://www.gotquestions.org/Young-Life.html)).
  It is sometimes informally searched as "Young Life cult" by skeptical teens/parents, suggesting its high-energy
  club format can read as pressure to outsiders ([TikTok discovery](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/young-life-youth-cult)).
- **FCA:** sports-based, since 1954, "use the powerful medium of athletics to impact the world for Jesus Christ"
  ([FCA timeline](https://timeline.fca.org/)).
- Net: **the biggest non-Sunday entry point for teens is relational and social (camp, club, teammates), not
  classroom teaching** — echoing the Hindu report's finding that peers and shared experience, not lecture, is what
  "sticks" socially.

### What young adults say they remember, and what they say they missed

- **Remember:** specific VBS/camp songs, the sinner's prayer wording, bracelet/ring artifacts (WWJD, True Love
  Waits) from the 1990s–2000s purity movement ([Wikipedia: Purity ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purity_ring)),
  youth group lock-ins and camp bonfires with public "testimonies."
- **Missed / resent:** that Sunday school "shut down children's questions rather than take them seriously," leading
  to attrition that becomes a visible "mass exodus" right after Confirmation in mainline traditions (search
  summaries, see Section 2 #44–45); that purity teaching put responsibility for male lust onto girls' clothing and
  behavior ([The Revealer](https://therevealer.org/post-purity-culture-the-new-online-frontier-of-evangelical-sexual-ethics/));
  that nobody ever explained what the Rapture actually is or isn't, despite it shaping real childhood fear
  ([Michael Bird Substack](https://michaelfbird.substack.com/p/rapture-trauma/comments)).
- A useful split emerges: **the delivery mechanisms (songs, crafts, camp, bracelets) were warm and well-loved; the
  content underneath them (what words mean, why we do this, what's negotiable) is the part the audience as adults
  says they never got.** This matches the Hindu report's core finding almost exactly, with a different texture of
  moment (youth group, not temple).

## 4. Everyday "explain my childhood" moments

- **Home and mealtime:** saying grace before dinner, a parent quoting "the Lord helps those who help themselves"
  (not actually in the Bible — a common point of confusion), VeggieTales on in the background.
- **Church-specific body language:** the altar call ("come forward if you want to accept Jesus into your heart")
  ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altar_call)); communion with a small cup of grape juice and a cracker
  or wafer, passed down the pew; raised hands during worship; closing your eyes and bowing your head for prayer.
- **Christianese heard constantly, rarely defined:** "quiet time," "on fire for God," "let go and let God,"
  "backslidden," "convicted," "a hedge of protection," "traveling mercies," "plant a seed," "the Word," "testimony,"
  "fellowship" (as a noun for hanging out) — a glossary that GotQuestions itself felt necessary to publish
  ([gotquestions.org/Christian-jargon-Christianese.html](https://www.gotquestions.org/Christian-jargon-Christianese.html)).
- **1990s–2000s youth-culture artifacts:** WWJD bracelets, True Love Waits purity rings and pledge cards ("Believing
  that true love waits, I make a commitment to God...") ([Wikipedia: Purity ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purity_ring)),
  "I Kissed Dating Goodbye"-era youth group talks, Christian contemporary music played on the way to school.
- **Camp and youth group rituals:** the bonfire "testimony" night, the lock-in, worship songs some critics called
  "Jesus is my boyfriend" music for their romantic-sounding lyrics ([forum discussion/Gospel Coalition summary](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/blogs/trevin-wax/men-orthodoxy-worship-songs/)),
  being asked to "share your testimony" out loud to a group of near-strangers.
- **Fear-based teaching that was rarely explained outright:** being told as a small child that if you didn't believe,
  you (or your unsaved parent) could be "left behind" at the Rapture ([Substack summary](https://endtimes.substack.com/p/your-total-redemption-is-coming/comments)), despite many churches never
  formally teaching what the Rapture is.
- **Pastor-and-money moments:** noticing a pastor's expensive sneakers or car and not knowing whether to feel
  cynical or like you were missing context ([Seattle Times](https://www.seattletimes.com/nation-world/preachers-and-their-5000-sneakers-why-one-man-started-an-instagram-account-showing-churches-wealth/)).
- **Holidays beyond the obvious:** Easter as "Resurrection Sunday" plus egg hunts and bunnies on the same day; Advent
  calendars counting down to Christmas; "See You at the Pole" school-prayer day; Vacation Bible School as a summer
  childcare-and-faith hybrid that often doubled as a neighborhood social event.
- **Pop-culture touchpoints:** VeggieTales characters retelling Bible stories with vegetables; a pastor's sermon clip
  showing up on a friend's Instagram feed with no context; "preacher's kid" as a recognizable character type; the
  shared joke of a parent quoting a Bible verse mid-argument.
- **Outside the home:** a friend asking "wait, you don't believe in evolution?" or "so you think gay people are
  going to hell?" — questions this audience often can't answer confidently because no one walked them through *why*
  their church taught what it taught, only *that* it did.

   denomination questions)

5. **"Once saved, always saved?"** — GotQuestions' #1-ranked practical worry (#4 on their list)
6. **What is the Holy Spirit, and what is "speaking in tongues"?** (GotQuestions #12, Quora)
7. **The Rapture: is it even biblical, and why was I scared of it as a kid?** (Substack ×3, real documented fear)
8. **Christianese decoded** — "quiet time," "on fire," "let go and let God," "testimony" (GotQuestions glossary)
9. **Purity culture: what it taught, and what the Bible actually says vs. what I was told** (GotQuestions #18, The
   Revealer, Wikipedia purity ring)
10. **Why so many denominations — what actually separates Baptist, Methodist, Lutheran, Catholic?** (Quora ×2)
11. **"Is it a sin?" edge questions (tattoos, alcohol, gambling)** — GotQuestions' actual #3, #15, #16 most-searched
12. **What happens after death / do pets go to heaven** (GotQuestions #9, #10 — surprisingly high-traffic)
13. **Tithing: why, and how much, and what it's for** (GotQuestions #11; also pastor-wealth questions)
14. **Pastors and money: why the sneakers, and is that okay?** (PreachersNSneakers, Seattle Times)
15. **"Who was Cain's wife?"** and other Genesis head-scratchers kids actually ask (GotQuestions #7 — a real, oddly
    specific, frequently-asked question)
16. **VBS songs and camp memories: what the words actually mean** (World Help, Lifeway)
17. **What is a "testimony" and why do we say it out loud?** (youth-group/camp pattern across multiple sources)
18. **Women pastors — the Bible's single most-searched question on GotQuestions** (#1 on their list; handle with
    care, see below)
19. **Homosexuality and the Bible — GotQuestions' #2 most-searched question** (handle with care, see below)
20. **Dinosaurs and the Bible** — a genuinely common, almost charming point of confusion (GotQuestions #13)

Close behind: Young Life/FCA ("is that a cult?"), WWJD bracelets and purity rings as artifacts, "Jesus is my
boyfriend" worship music, the sinner's prayer, Easter vs. Resurrection Sunday, "I Kissed Dating Goodbye"-era dating
rules, megachurch vs. "regular" church.

## How the best creators hook people

1. **Fact-check, don't preach.** Dan McClellan's whole format is "here's what the text actually says," delivered
   deadpan — it built nearly 1M followers among people who no longer trust a pulpit ([Liberty Beats](https://libertybeats.substack.com/p/dan-mcclellan-the-biblical-scholar)).
2. **Name the discomfort out loud.** PreachersNSneakers turned a side-eye about pastor wealth into a 321K-follower
   account in weeks by simply pointing a camera at the shoe and the price tag ([Seattle Times](https://www.seattletimes.com/nation-world/preachers-and-their-5000-sneakers-why-one-man-started-an-instagram-account-showing-churches-wealth/)).
3. **Vulnerability travels further than polish.** Jon Steingard's single honest confession post made international
   news; it outperformed years of his band's devotional content ([Variety](https://variety.com/2020/music/news/christian-jonathan-steingard-no-longer-believes-god-hawk-nelson-1234617642/)).
4. **Cut the sermon to the relatable line.** Furtick and Todd's biggest reach comes not from full sermons but from
   30–60 second clips of one punchy, motivational sentence ([ReachRight](https://reachrightstudios.com/pastor-influence-100/steven-furtick/)).
5. **Trace one word or theme at a time, visually.** BibleProject's 5.4M subscribers were built on short animated
   videos that follow a single theme or word through the whole Bible rather than a whole book at once ([HypeAuditor](https://hypeauditor.com/youtube/UCVfwlh9XpX2Y_tQfjeln9QA/)).
6. **Give the question a name before answering it.** GotQuestions' top-20 page works because it states the exact
   question in the exact words people type ("Who was Cain's wife?") rather than a reframed, softened version
   ([gotquestions.org/top20.html](https://www.gotquestions.org/top20.html)).
7. **Hold faith and doubt in the same breath.** The Liturgists and Ask Science Mike built large, loyal audiences
   explicitly for "the spiritually homeless" — people who want to stay curious without pretending they have no
   questions ([Wild Goose Festival](https://wildgoosefestival.org/goosecasts/ask-science-mike/)).
8. **Make the childhood artifact the hook.** Content about purity rings, WWJD bracelets and VBS songs reliably pulls
   this audience in because it's instantly, viscerally recognizable before any doctrine is mentioned
   (#PurityCulture: ~60M TikTok views) ([The Revealer](https://therevealer.org/post-purity-culture-the-new-online-frontier-of-evangelical-sexual-ethics/)).

## What to avoid early

- **Opening with denominational distinctions or church history.** "Baptist vs. Methodist vs. Lutheran polity" is a
  real question (Section 2 #23–24) but it's a *later* question, not an opener — it's about structure, not meaning.
- **Leading with the edge-case "is it a sin?" questions as moral rulings.** They're real and heavily searched
  (tattoos, alcohol, gambling, sex before marriage), but opening there risks sounding like a verdict machine rather
  than a teacher; GotQuestions itself frames them as FAQ, not doctrine.
- **Treating the Rapture, purity culture, or "once saved always saved" lightly.** These carry real, documented
  childhood fear and harm for parts of this audience (Section 2 #31–33, #39–41); they need careful, reviewed
  framing, not a punchy one-liner.
- **Mocking or "gotcha" framing toward any specific living pastor or church.** PreachersNSneakers works because it's
  satire about a *pattern* (wealth optics), not because it's cruel about one person; our lessons should describe
  patterns and history, not target named, living church leaders.
- **Assuming one script covers every denomination.** Baptism, communion frequency, and the Rapture are each taught
  very differently across Baptist, Pentecostal, Methodist, and non-denominational churches; say "in many churches…"
  the way the Hindu report recommends "in many families…" for Diwali.
- **Politics.** Homosexuality, women pastors, and interracial marriage appear at the very top of GotQuestions'
  actual traffic (#1, #2, #6) — proof the audience is asking — but these sit directly on live political and
  denominational fault lines; they need careful, later, explicitly reviewed treatment, never a quick early lesson.
- **Shaming language ("you're not a real Christian if...", "lukewarm faith").** The exvangelical movement's whole
  energy is a reaction against feeling judged as a kid; starting there would repeat the exact thing this audience is
  moving away from.
- **Chanting or reciting without meaning.** The sinner's prayer, memory verses and VBS songs are already memorized;
  repeating them isn't new value — explaining them is (same gap the Hindu report found with shlokas).

## A proposed first week

| Day | Topic | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Communion: what the bread and the cup are actually doing** | Nearly universal memory across denominations, low political risk, a clean container for "what does this symbolize" teaching. |
| 2 | **The Trinity, explained simply** | The single most-cited "I say it but can't explain it" gap (GotQuestions #17, multiple Quora denomination questions); foundational, not an edge case. |
| 3 | **The altar call and "accepting Jesus into your heart"** | A specific remembered ritual; lets us teach what conversion/salvation means through a moment people have actually lived, not an abstract doctrine. |
| 4 | **Baptism: why churches do it, and why they disagree on how** | Directly answers Quora's most-repeated denomination confusion, while modeling "different churches do this differently" instead of picking a side. |
| 5 | **Christianese decoded: "quiet time," "testimony," "on fire for God"** | Pure "explain my childhood" content — everyone has heard these phrases; zero doctrinal risk, immediate recognition and payoff. |
| 6 | **The Holy Spirit and what "speaking in tongues" means** | High-traffic GotQuestions topic (#12) and a real source of quiet anxiety ("does my lack of X mean I'm missing the Spirit?" — Quora #21); teaches a core but under-explained doctrine. |
| 7 | **Where these words come from: the Bible as one story** | Closes the week by giving the "whole picture" view (BibleProject's model) so the earlier days' pieces fit into a single narrative, without yet touching the heavier, later topics (Rapture, purity culture, denominational splits, the edge-case "is it a sin" questions). |
