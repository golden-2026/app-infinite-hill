(a look back, plus 2–3 games) → one breath or practice → make it yours → score. About 4 minutes a day.

## Day 2 · om (from day 2, "the sound the universe hums")
**Hook:** *Your yoga teacher says it. Your grandparents chant it. What's actually inside it?*

**Story**
1. Living room. Dev is following a yoga video on the TV; the instructor (on screen) chants. Dev, flat: *"Ommm… whatever that means."*
2. Anika, upside down on the couch: *"Nani, is om a word? Like, does it mean anything?"*
3. Nani sits between them: *"It's not one sound. Say it slowly. Aaa… uuu… mmm."*
4. Close-up: three mouths, wide open, rounded, closed. Dev, despite himself: *"Okay that's kind of cool."*
5. Nani: *"An old text, the Mandukya Upanishad, says a is waking, u is dreaming, m is deep sleep."*
6. The hum fades; all three sit in the quiet. Nani: *"And the silence after? The text calls it the fourth. That's the self."*

**Cards**
1. **ॐ** (written as one sacred sign) · say it: *OHM (a-u-m)* · 🔊
2. **A map in one breath:** a = waking · u = dreaming · m = deep sleep · the silence = *the fourth*, the self, peaceful. *Mandukya Upanishad*
3. **The first sound.** Om opens prayers, mantras and readings. The Bhagavad Gita says acts of offering, giving and discipline begin with it. *Gita 17.23–24*

**Play**
- *Look back:* "te" in namaste means… **to you** · a bow · peace · the light
- *Myth or fact:* "Om is one unbroken sound." → **myth** (three sounds and a silence)
- *Match:* a ↔ waking · u ↔ dreaming · m ↔ deep sleep · the silence ↔ the fourth
- *Use it:* Dev says om is "just a sound to end yoga." What would Nani add? → **it's a map of a whole day, and of the quiet under it** · it's the oldest word in Sanskrit · it means peace · it's a kind of hello *(all plausible; only the first is the lesson's point)*

**One breath:** a long aaa-uuu-mmm with the circle; then sit in the quiet for one more breath. That's the fourth.

**Make it yours:** right now: one om, out loud or humming · today: notice one moment of quiet after noise · **tonight with your family:** hum one om together before bed, then count to three in the silence.

**Images:** (1) Dev in a yoga pose, TV glow · (2) Anika upside down on the couch · (3) Nani between them, cross-legged · (4) three-mouth close-up triptych · (5) Nani explaining, three glowing shapes (sun, moon, night) above her hand · (6) the three sitting in a quiet, glowing room.

## Day 3 · pranam (from day 5, "why Indian kids touch their grandparents' feet")
**Hook:** *Two seconds at the front door. A whole conversation without words.*

**Story**
1. Front door: Nani's sister (Badi Nani) arrives with bags. Anika rushes over, bends, and touches her feet.
2. Badi Nani's hand rests on Anika's head; she murmurs a blessing. Dev hangs back, awkward, phone in hand.
3. Dev, quietly to Nani: *"Isn't that like bowing to a boss?"*
4. Nani: *"Pranam. Pra, forward. Nam, to bow. Same nam as namaste."*
5. Nani: *"Your head, where the pride lives, meets their feet, which walked the road before you. And watch: a blessing comes back."*
6. Dev puts the phone down, steps forward and bows. Badi Nani's hand on his head. He's smiling.

**Cards**
1. **प्रणाम** · say it: *pra-NAAM* · 🔊 · pra = forward + nam = to bend. Cousin of namaste.
2. **Two halves.** Touching the feet: **charan sparsh** (charan, the feet; sparsh, a touch). The hand on the head: **ashirvad**, a blessing given back. Nobody walks away smaller.
3. **An old promise.** The Manusmriti says that for one who greets and serves elders, four things grow: long life, learning, good name, strength. Some read it as a promise, some as what the habit does to a person. *Manusmriti 2.121*

**Play**
- *Look back:* In om, the silence after the mmm is called… **the fourth** · the end · the breath · peace
- *Myth or fact:* "Pranam and namaste share a root." → **fact** (nam, to bow)
- *Match:* pra ↔ forward · nam ↔ to bow · charan sparsh ↔ touching the feet · ashirvad ↔ the blessing back
- *Use it:* Why does the elder put a hand on the child's head? → **to give a blessing back, so respect goes both ways** · to tell them to stand up · to check they're grown · it's just a hug

**Practice:** bring one person who came before you to mind; bow your head a little for one breath; picture the hand on your head.

**Make it yours:** right now: the one-breath bow · today: call, text or thank one elder · **tonight with your family:** tell your kids one true story about a great-grandparent.

**Images:** (1) doorway arrival, bags · (2) hand on Anika's head, glow · (3) Dev hanging back, phone · (4) Nani explaining, two hands (one bowing, one blessing) · (5) head-meets-feet, gentle diagram-like composition · (6) Dev bowing, Badi Nani's hand, everyone smiling.

## Day 4 · Ganesha (from day 6, "the one at every doorway")
**Hook:** *He's on dashboards, shop counters and wedding invitations. Always first. Why?*

**Story**
1. Papa's new shop, opening morning. Shutters half up. A small Ganesha image by the door.
2. Anika, pointing: *"Why is he always at the door?"*
3. Papa, hanging a marigold string: *"Ask Nani. She knows his names."*
4. Nani: *"Ganesha: lord of the ganas, Shiva's attendants. But his best name is Vighnaharta: the remover of obstacles."*
5. Dev, writing in a notebook for his exam tomorrow: *"So I should write his name at the top?"* Nani: *"Shri ganeshaya namah. Some people do. Hear that last word?"*
6. Anika, lighting up: *"Namah! Like namaste! A bow!"* The shutters roll all the way up.

**Cards**
1. **गणेश** · say it: *ga-NAY-sha* · 🔊 · gana (the attendants) + isha (lord). Also called Ganapati.
2. **Vighnaharta.** vighna = obstacle, harta = one who removes. That's why he's honored first: before ceremonies, weddings, new shops, exams.
3. **Read his body.** One popular reading: big ears, listen more; small eyes, focus; big belly, take in the good and the hard; one broken tusk: one story people tell says he snapped it off to keep writing the Mahabharata as Vyasa spoke. *Mahabharata, Adi Parva (Ganesha as scribe)*

**Play**
- *Look back:* Pranam comes from pra + nam. Nam means… **to bend or bow** · to greet · to name · forward
- *Myth or fact:* "Ganesha's name means 'elephant god'." → **myth** (lord of the ganas)
- *Spot it:* In "shri ganeshaya namah", which word is day one's bow? → **namah**
- *Use it:* Why is Ganesha honored before something new starts? → **he's the remover of obstacles, so people ask for a clear way in** · he's the oldest god · elephants bring luck · he's the god of shops

**Practice:** rest a hand on something that marks a start (a door, a laptop lid, a notebook). Name what's in the way, once. Ask for a clear road.

**Make it yours:** right now: the hand-on-a-start practice · today: before one task, pause at its "doorway" · **tonight with your family:** each person names one thing they're starting this week.

**Images:** ⚠ Ganesha's design needs Panditji's approval before use. (1) shop shutters, Ganesha by the door · (2) Anika pointing · (3) Papa with marigolds · (4) Nani explaining, Ganesha image glowing softly behind · (5) Dev's notebook, a line at the top (no readable text) · (6) shutters up, sunlight, everyone cheering.

## Day 5 · arti (from day 14, "light passed hand to hand")
**Hook:** *A small flame, circled, then carried to every face in the room.*

**Story**
1. Evening, living room. Nani holds a plate with a small flame; a bell rings. Dev is filming on his phone.
2. Nani circles the lamp slowly before the shrine and sings: *"Om jai jagadish hare…"*
3. Anika: *"Victory to you, lord of the world. Right?"* Nani nods, still singing.
4. Nani carries the plate around; Papa passes his hands over the flame and brings his palms to his eyes.
5. It reaches Dev. He lowers the phone, cups his hands toward the flame, palms to his forehead.
6. Nani: *"An old text says everything shines after that one shining. We just pass a little of it around."*

**Cards**
1. **आरती** · say it: *AAR-tee* · 🔊 · the waving of a lamp; the offering of light. It often closes a puja.
2. **Light back to where light comes from.** The Katha Upanishad: the sun doesn't shine there, nor the moon and stars, let alone this little fire; everything shines after that one shining. *Katha Upanishad 2.2.15*
3. **Then it leaves the shrine.** The flame goes to the people; each draws its warmth to their eyes and forehead. Same fire, every face. Nobody gets more.

**Play**
- *Look back:* Ganesha's name Vighnaharta means… **remover of obstacles** · lord of the ganas · the elephant one · the first
- *Myth or fact:* "Arti happens only in temples." → **myth** (temples, riverbanks, living rooms)
- *Order it:* put the arti in order: lamp lit → circled before the shrine while singing → carried to the people → hands to eyes and forehead
- *Use it:* Why is the flame carried to everyone? → **so each person can take in some of the blessing, and nobody gets more** · to put it out safely · so people can light their own lamps · to show it to guests

**Practice:** light a candle (or look at any light), cup your hands toward it, bring them to your eyes, then turn them toward someone you love.

**Make it yours:** right now: the cupped-hands practice · today: share one small good thing with someone · **tonight with your family:** light one candle together and pass your hands over it.

**Images:** (1) evening glow, plate and flame, bell · (2) circling lamp, light trails · (3) Anika singing along · (4) Papa's palms to eyes · (5) Dev lowering phone, hands cupped (hero moment) · (6) the whole room lit by one flame.

## Day 6 · puja (from day 8, "what your grandmother was actually doing")
**Hook:** *Every morning, before the kettle. What is Nani actually doing at that shelf?*

**Story**
1. Early morning. Nani at the home shrine: lamp, marigold, bell, incense, a cup of water, a sweet. Anika in pajamas watching.
2. Anika: *"Can I help?"* Nani hands her the flower.
3. Step by step, close-ups: water poured · flower set down · incense lit · lamp waved · bell rung · sweet offered.
4. Dev, sleepy, eyes the sweet: *"Is that for… God?"* Nani: *"Then it comes back to us. Prasad."*
5. Nani: *"Krishna says in the Gita: a leaf, a flower, a fruit, water. If it's offered with love, he takes it."*
6. All three eating the prasad. Anika: *"So it's not about how big the gift is?"* Nani: *"It's about how much of you is there."*

**Cards**
1. **पूजा** · say it: *POO-jah* · 🔊 · honor, worship: care offered to God, often through the senses.
2. **The steps.** water · flower · incense · lamp · bell · food, which comes back as **prasad**, a gift to eat. Families and regions do it differently.
3. **Patram pushpam phalam toyam.** A leaf, a flower, a fruit, water: whatever is offered with devotion, Krishna says, he accepts. The size was never the point. *Bhagavad Gita 9.26*

**Play**
- *Look back:* In arti, after the lamp is circled… **it's carried to the people** · it's put out · it stays at the shrine · it's given to the priest
- *Myth or fact:* "A puja needs an expensive offering." → **myth** (a leaf, a flower, a fruit, water)
- *Match the senses:* water ↔ touch (cool) · flower ↔ sight · incense ↔ smell · bell ↔ sound · prasad ↔ taste
- *Use it:* Anika has nothing to offer but a dandelion from the yard. Per the Gita, is that enough? → **yes, if it's offered with devotion** · no, it has to be a marigold · only if a priest offers it · only on festival days

**Practice:** honor one ordinary thing near you: eyes, then touch, then eyes closed holding the picture. If you pray, offer the attention.

**Make it yours:** right now: the one-thing practice · today: give one person your full attention for a minute · **tonight with your family:** let the kids pick one thing to honor, and do it together.

**Images:** (1) dawn shrine shelf, still life · (2) Anika in pajamas receiving the flower · (3) six-step close-up grid · (4) Dev eyeing the sweet (comic beat) · (5) Nani speaking, a leaf/flower/fruit/water floating in light · (6) three of them sharing prasad.

## Day 7 · your week (the first unit test)
**Hook:** *Six days ago, namaste was just hello. Let's see what you know now.*

**Story (3 panels):** Family dinner, everyone there. Dev, mock-serious: *"Quiz time."* Nani: *"Go on, test me."* Anika: *"No, test ME."*

**Unit test (8 questions, mixed, harder than daily games; answers shown at the end)**
1. namaste literally means… **I bow to you** · the light in me sees the light in you · peace to you · welcome
2. In om, a, u and m stand for… **waking, dreaming, deep sleep** · past, present, future · body, mind, soul · birth, life, death
3. The silence after om is called… **the fourth** · the end · samadhi · shanti
4. The hand on the head after a pranam is… **a blessing given back (ashirvad)** · a greeting · a way to say stand up · a test
5. Which word appears in both namaste and "shri ganeshaya namah"? **namah/namas: a bow** · shri · te · isha
6. Vighnaharta means… **remover of obstacles** · lord of the ganas · elephant-headed · the first
7. After arti, the flame is… **carried to each person** · put out at the shrine · given to the eldest · kept burning all night
8. Gita 9.26 says what matters in an offering is… **the devotion** · the cost · the priest · the time of day

**Payoff:** score (*"7/8: you know more than most people who grew up with it"*) · **"Seven things you understand now"** card: namaste · om · pranam · Ganesha · arti · puja · and a five-minute habit · shareable to family (no names or scores shown to others).

**Make it yours:** **tonight with your family:** each person teaches one of the six words to someone else at the table.

**What's next (offered, never pushed):** week 2 keeps the same five minutes; set a reminder time.
