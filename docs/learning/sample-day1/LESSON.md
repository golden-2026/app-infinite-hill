# Sample lesson: Hinduism day 1, namaste (new format)

Built only from the existing day-1 script (docs/curriculum/hinduism/scripts/y1/day-0001.json) and its sources
(Chandogya Upanishad 3.13.7; Monier-Williams). No new religious claims. The story frames those facts as a short
conversation between a girl and her grandmother. DRAFT, not yet reviewed by Panditji.

About 5 minutes. One experience for everyone; the only personal line is the "do" at the end.

## 1. Hook (image H, 10 seconds)
Image: three small scenes side by side: a yoga class ending, a shopkeeper in Chennai greeting a customer, a host in
New Jersey welcoming a guest, all with palms pressed together.
> **you've said it a hundred times. do you know what it means?**

## 2. Story (panels 1–6, about a minute, read aloud)
1. Nani opens the front door to a delivery driver and greets him, palms together. — *"Namaste!"*
2. Anika (8), watching from the stairs. — *"Nani, why do you say namaste to everyone? Even the delivery guy?"*
3. Nani kneels to Anika's height, palms together. — *"It's two old Sanskrit words. Namas: a bow. Te: to you."*
4. Anika, thinking. — *"So it just means 'I bow to you'?"* Nani, smiling: *"That's the dictionary. But bow to what?"*
5. Nani touches her own heart, then points gently to Anika's; a soft light glows in both. — *"An old text, the
   Chandogya Upanishad, says the light beyond the sky is the same light inside a person."*
6. Both bow to each other, palms together, glowing. — *"So namaste means: the light in me sees the light in you."*

## 3. Teach (punchy cards, one idea each)
1. **namas** = a bow
2. **te** = to you
3. **namaste** = *I bow to you.* (that's the dictionary, Monier-Williams)
4. bow to what? not your clothes, your job title, or your mood.
5. **the Chandogya Upanishad**: the light beyond the heavens is the same light inside a person. (3.13.7)
6. so the light is **already in there**. you don't earn it. you learn to notice it.
7. big: **the light in me sees the light in you.** *(a reading of namaste, not its dictionary meaning)*

## 4. Play (games that check understanding)
1. **Myth or fact** (existing items)
   - "namaste literally means the light in me sees the light in you": **myth**. Literally it's a bow, to you; the light is a reading.
   - "namaste is made of two Sanskrit words": **fact**.
   - "an Upanishad says the light above the heavens is the light inside a person": **fact**.
2. **Tap the pairs** (existing): namas ↔ a bow · te ↔ to you · palms together, head tipped ↔ the gesture that goes with it · the Chandogya Upanishad ↔ the same light, inside a person.
3. **Apply it** (new): *A classmate is rude to you at lunch. If you greeted them with namaste the way Nani means it, what would you be noticing?*
   - their bad mood ✗ · **the same light that's in you** ✓ · that you're better than them ✗ · nothing, it's just hello ✗
4. **Explain it** (new, AI-checked, kind): *In your own words: what does namaste literally mean, and what's the deeper reading?*
   - counts: says "I bow to you" (or a bow + to you) · names the light-in-everyone reading · optional: says it comes from texts like the Upanishads

## 5. Do (30 seconds)
Press your palms together, breathe in, and as you breathe out tip your head a little: one small bow.
Today, greet one person with namaste (out loud or inside), and see them that way for a second.
*With your kids tonight: greet each other with namaste at dinner, and each say one thing you see in the other.*
