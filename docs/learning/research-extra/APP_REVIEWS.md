# What app-store reviews of rival apps teach us

Research date: 2026-10-06. Read-only. No sign-ins, no accounts, nothing submitted. No reviewer names recorded; all
review content is paraphrased (a few words quoted at most).

## How this was gathered

- **Apple App Store web pages** (apps.apple.com, US store unless noted), main page and the "see all reviews" page for
  each app. These show the star rating, the rating count, and about ten "most helpful" reviews.
- **Review aggregator** justuseapp.com for Hallow, Glorify and Muslim Pro (it summarises complaint themes across many
  more reviews than Apple shows).
- **Google Play** figures only through search results and summaries (Sri Mandir, Tarteel); the Play pages themselves
  were not read in full.
- **Reputable reporting**: Mozilla Foundation "Privacy Not Included" (prayer apps), Vice/Middle East Eye on Muslim
  Pro's data sale, NCR / America Magazine on Hallow's 2026 Lent partnership backlash, Fortune / Tech Times / Inc. on
  Duolingo's 2025 "AI-first" backlash, YouVersion's own support pages and posts on streak restoration, Christian Today
  on AI Bible chatbot bias, Trustpilot summaries for Headspace and Calm billing, CT / Religion News on young-adult
  Bible reading.

**Important limit.** Apple's web page shows only ~10 hand-picked "helpful" reviews, and they skew strongly positive
(Pray.com's ten were all 5 stars). So "how often it came up" below means *out of the ~10 visible reviews*, and
complaints are under-counted. The aggregator and press sources were used to fill in the complaint side.

**Not reached / blocked:** Google Play review text (pages not read directly); Calm review text; Insight Timer US page
(only a small foreign storefront loaded, 45 ratings, no reviews); Basics of Sikhi and a Gita app with real user
volume in the US (US Hindu app pages have few ratings; the Hindu user base is on Play in India); Reddit (covered in
the separate REDDIT files). No CAPTCHAs met.

## Per app

Counts are "n of ~10 visible reviews" unless stated.

### Hallow (Catholic) - 4.9 stars, ~379K ratings
- **Love:** polished, "modern" guided prayer that finally makes a daily habit stick (6/10); big programmes like Bible
  in a Year and Catechism in a Year with a trusted priest (3/10); celebrity voices, especially the actor who plays
  Jesus in *The Chosen*, which is also what brought people in (2/10); family/kids use (1/10); "worth it vs.
  streaming" value (2/10).
- **Quit / complain:** billing and trial-to-annual surprises, hard cancelling, slow support (aggregator: ~3-4% of
  all reviews; 1/10 visible). Press: critics call it "pay-to-pray"; $69.99/yr. Feb 2026 Lent partnership with a
  partisan TV host set off boycott calls; earlier celebrity promos drew political backlash. Some Catholics object
  to it drifting into general Christian branding; one Protestant found it "too Catholic." Rosary/psalm wording not
  matching what people grew up with.
- **Ask for:** written transcripts to learn the prayers (2/10), family sharing, more Liturgy of the Hours, Apple
  Watch.
- **Privacy:** best of the prayer apps Mozilla checked, but still uses data for marketing.

### Pray.com (Christian) - 4.8 stars, ~193K ratings
- **Love:** narrated Bible stories and bedtime stories with voice actors; a calm daily listen; daily prayer emails
  (10/10 visible were 5 stars).
- **Complain:** streak tracking glitches (1/10). Press: Mozilla gave it the "Privacy Not Included" warning: collects
  and can buy data (religion, politics, income) and share it with other "faith-based organizations"; a past data
  leak left unfixed.
- **Lesson:** happy reviews can coexist with a trust time bomb.

### YouVersion Bible App (Christian) - 4.9 stars, ~14M ratings
- **Love:** free; many translations side by side (5/10); reading plans by topic or feeling (3/10); audio for
  multitasking (2/10); verse images and sharing, friends and group plans (4/10); "helped a newcomer feel less alone."
- **Complain:** small bugs - bookmarks, notes hard to find, lost reading position (4/10). Streak loss is a long-running
  pain point (users losing 1,000+ day streaks); YouVersion added a once-a-month "Restore Streak" for streaks of 9+
  days in late 2025.
- **Ask for:** stats on what you've read, achievements, a short-video feed of sermons "to replace social media"
  scrolling.

### Glorify (Christian) - 4.9 stars, ~100K ratings
- **Love:** short structured devotional (10-15 min), audio narration, sleep stories (6/10); a visual reward ("Tree of
  Faith") and streaks (3/10, one with a 200+ day streak).
- **Quit / complain:** paywall arrived after people were hooked; a family of four priced out at about $70/person/yr
  (4/10 on billing). Aggregator: "most frequent" complaint is the paywall/trial feeling misleading, plus annual
  charges without clear consent. Streak breaking on time-zone change or disappearing after an update (2/10). Updates
  removing loved features (daily prayer) (3/10).
- **Ask for:** family plan, subtitles/text for accessibility.

### Abide (Christian) - 4.9 stars, ~122K ratings
- **Love:** Bible meditation and sleep, help in crisis and addiction recovery (6/10).
- **Quit / complain:** aggressive purchase prompts that interrupt a meditation (1/10); too little free content to judge
  it (1/10); asks for a donation model instead (1/10).

### Muslim Pro (Muslim) - 4.7 stars, ~600K ratings
- **Love:** all-in-one: prayer times, Quran with translation and audio, duas, 99 Names, Hijri calendar, trackers
  (4/10).
- **Quit / complain:** ads in a prayer app (5/10 - the top complaint); paying a subscription for basics like the
  adhan (4/10); feature bloat and unwanted notifications (1/10); AI-generated content - "replace AI with human
  creators" (1/10). Press: 2020 reports that location data reached the US military via a broker; users deleted it in
  protest; lawsuits in France. Its rivals win reviews by saying "unlike Muslim Pro."

### Pillars (Muslim prayer times) - 4.8 stars, ~5.6K ratings
- **Love:** no ads, ever (6/10); privacy, Muslim-made (2/10); calm design; prayer tracker and streak (2/10); a
  "pause" for menstruation so the streak isn't broken (1/10).
- **Complain:** putting the widget behind premium (1/10); no Shia calculation method (1/10).
- **Lesson:** "rest" in a streak can be a religious need, not just a convenience.

### Tarteel (Quran memorisation, AI) - 4.7 stars, ~11K ratings (Play 4.6, ~108K)
- **Love:** AI that listens to your recitation and flags mistakes; tracking weak spots.
- **Complain:** AI false alarms when reciting fast or on similar verses (3/10); freezes (2/10); ~$100/yr; feels the
  core learning tool shouldn't be paywalled.
- **Lesson:** AI in a sacred context is judged harshly when it is wrong.

### Sefaria (Jewish texts) - 4.5 stars, ~773 ratings
- **Love:** free, huge linked library, translations, "accessible to all backgrounds" (7/10).
- **Complain:** bugs, Hebrew word tool misses prefixes.
- **Gap:** it is a library, not a teacher: no path, no "start here," no why.

### Aleph Beta (Jewish learning) - 4.8 stars, ~516 ratings
- **Love:** depth and the *why* behind Torah stories, delivered in animated videos (8/10); converts and even
  Christians love it.
- **Complain:** app bugs, missing watch-list/progress (2/10); $8.99-$17.99/month.
- **Lesson:** people will pay for genuine "why" teaching delivered with craft.

### SikhNet Play (Sikh) - 4.9 stars, ~1.9K ratings
- **Love:** huge kirtan library, English translations that let diaspora "understand" (3/10), connection while far from
  a gurdwara.
- **Complain:** crashes, transliteration bugs (2/10).
- **Ask for:** playlists, offline downloads.

### iGurbani (Sikh) - 4.7 stars, ~478 ratings
- **Love:** best Gurbani search, readable pauses.
- **Complain:** updates that deleted saved items or removed commentary/transliteration (3/10).
- **Ask for:** meanings and a dictionary in the reading view, continuous reading with resume, Hindi script.
- **Basics of Sikhi** (leaflets and videos explaining the basics in 20 languages) exists, but has almost no US review
  volume; it is outreach material, not a daily practice.

### Sri Mandir (Hindu) - US 4.3 stars, 283 ratings; Google Play ~4.4, ~140K reviews, 10M+ downloads
- **Love:** a phone "temple," aartis, bhajans, alarms; connection to roots.
- **Quit / complain:** paid pujas at temples "in your name" not delivered, no video proof, wrong gotra, no reply -
  called a scam (2/10 US, both 1 star). Pop-up ads (1/10). Play: updates that removed free features and now charge,
  AI images added, wrong panchang times, "too commercialized."
- **Lesson:** monetizing ritual itself destroys trust fast.

### Bhagavad Gita - Krishna Bhakti (Hindu, JKYog) - 4.9 stars, ~785 ratings
- **Love:** one teacher's plain verse-by-verse commentary that applies the Gita to daily life (5/10); word-by-word
  Sanskrit and audio (1/10); 30-day challenges; AI Q&A and AI videos built on that teacher's work (2/10).
- **Ask for:** sync and bookmarks.
- **Lesson:** AI is welcomed when it is clearly grounded in a named, trusted teacher.
- Note: the store is crowded with near-identical Gita reader apps and an "AI Krishna" chatbot.

### Plum Village (Buddhist) - 5.0 stars, ~3.9K ratings
- **Love:** completely free, no ads, no upsell (10/10); real teachings from Thich Nhat Hanh's community; bells of
  mindfulness.
- **Complain:** none visible.
- **Lesson:** a gift model earns devotion; people switch *to* it from paid apps.

### Waking Up (secular/Buddhist-rooted) - 4.9 stars, ~43K ratings
- **Love:** theory plus practice - understanding *why* meditation works (7/10); structured intro course; free if you
  can't afford it.
- **Complain:** progress feels slow/unclear after 30 sessions (1/10). $149.99/yr.

### Headspace - 4.8 stars, ~974K ratings / Calm - 4.8 stars, ~1.9M ratings
- **Love:** helps with anxiety, breakups, sleep; an AI companion was praised by one user (1/10).
- **Quit / complain:** mandatory paywall (1/10); a favourite teacher's voice leaving (1/10). Trustpilot and roundups:
  free trial silently converting to an annual charge and hard cancelling are the dominant complaints for both; Calm
  ranked worst for auto-renew complaints.

### Duolingo (contrast) - 4.7 stars, ~5.5M ratings
- **Love:** the game feel keeps people longer than planned (3/10); streak plus freezes (4/10, mostly positive); free
  tier is real; characters and voices.
- **Complain:** gem cost of freezes, ads (2/10); pacing too fast for some (1/10). Press: April 2025 "AI-first" memo
  set off a revolt - users publicly deleted 500-900 day streaks and cancelled, calling new content "AI slop"; the CEO
  walked it back.
- **What Duolingo learned (reported):** switching the streak from "earn XP" to "one lesson a day" raised daily users a
  lot; streak freeze cut churn ~21% for at-risk users; making streaks *easier* to keep raised engagement.

## Per path: the gap no app fills for young adults who want the why

- **Hinduism:** apps are temple-in-your-phone (aarti, alarms, paid pujas) or scripture readers. Almost nothing explains
  in plain English *why* we do the rituals families do, for a US-raised young adult. Paid-ritual apps carry scam
  risk; the most-loved app is one teacher's lineage.
- **Buddhism:** meditation is everywhere (Headspace, Calm, Insight Timer); real Buddhist teaching is free at Plum
  Village. Missing: a short, warm path from "I meditate" or "my family is Buddhist" to understanding what the
  teachings actually are and why.
- **Christianity:** saturated with devotionals, audio Bibles, sleep stories. Most tell you *what* to read or pray, not
  *why* Christians believe it. AI Bible chat apps lean one tradition (US evangelical) without saying so.
- **Catholicism:** Hallow owns guided prayer, but people ask for transcripts to *learn* the prayers, and it now carries
  political and "pay-to-pray" baggage. Missing: short, light lessons on why Mass, saints, sacraments, rosary - for
  those who did them as kids.
- **Judaism:** Sefaria is a library, Aleph Beta is deep paid video. Missing: a daily three-minute "why do we do
  this" for people who know the blessings but not the meaning.
- **Islam:** utility apps dominate (prayer times, Quran, qibla) and trust is shaky (ads, data sale). Missing: an
  ad-free, privacy-first daily lesson on the *why* of salah, Ramadan, duas, that respects Sunni/Shia differences.
- **Sikhism:** strong on kirtan and Gurbani search; reviewers explicitly ask for meanings and a dictionary in the
  reading view. Missing: a daily, plain-English path through why Sikhs do what they do.
- **Simply Spiritual:** served by secular meditation and Waking Up-style theory, priced high. Missing: a warm,
  non-preachy daily practice that borrows wisely across traditions without flattening them.

## Cross-cutting lessons

### Retention
1. **Streaks work, and the forgiving version works best.** Duolingo, Pillars, Glorify and YouVersion users love
   streaks; the anger comes when one is lost to a glitch, a time zone, an update or a sign-out. YouVersion had to add
   restore; Duolingo's freezes cut churn; Pillars' menstruation pause is loved.
2. **Short and structured beats a big library.** "Manageable," "fits my morning," "I planned 5 minutes and stayed
   15." Libraries (Sefaria, Insight Timer) are admired but don't build a habit on their own.
3. **Audio matters.** Pray.com, Glorify, Abide, Hallow, YouVersion users repeatedly praise listening while doing
   something else - but others ask for the text (transcripts, subtitles), so offer both.
4. **Reminders are welcome when tied to a real moment** (prayer times, morning); feature-bloat notifications are a
   reason to leave (Muslim Pro).
5. **Paywall timing.** The worst reviews come from a paywall that appears *after* people are attached (Glorify,
   Sri Mandir removing free features), or a trial that silently turns into an annual charge (Headspace, Calm, Hallow
   billing). Family pricing is a repeated ask. Ads in a prayer app are seen as disrespectful.
6. **Updates that remove loved features** (iGurbani, Glorify, Sri Mandir) generate more 1-star anger than missing
   features ever do.
7. **Real voices keep people.** A teacher leaving Headspace cost a user; trusted priests and teachers are why people
   stay with Hallow and the Gita app.

### Trust
1. **Accuracy is sacred.** Wrong panchang times, mismatched daily readings, a rosary that isn't "the one I know,"
   AI false alarms in Quran recitation - all draw 1-2 star reviews.
2. **Named scholars beat anonymous content.** The highest-trust apps tie content to a named teacher or institution.
3. **Privacy is a reason to switch.** Muslim Pro's data scandal still drives reviews toward Pillars; Mozilla flagged
   most prayer apps. "Muslim-made, no tracking" is a selling point users repeat.
4. **AI:** welcomed when grounded in a trusted teacher and plainly labelled (Gita app); rejected when it replaces
   human creators or feels like filler (Muslim Pro, Sri Mandir, Duolingo backlash); criticised when it speaks
   as God/Jesus or defaults to one tradition without saying so.
5. **Politics and celebrity can backfire.** Hallow's celebrity partners drove downloads but also boycotts.
6. **Never monetize the ritual itself.** Paid pujas "in your name" and paywalled adhan are the most resented.

## What this means for Infinite Hill

1. **Lead with the why.** It is the clearest empty space in every path. Reviewers of Aleph Beta, Waking Up and the
   Gita app pay or stay *because* they finally understand. Make "here's why we do this" the core of every lesson.
2. **Keep the streak, make it kind.** One lesson a day counts; rest days and freezes built in; a quiet "pause" for
   travel, illness, grief and religious reasons (e.g., menstruation for Muslim users); a restore option; streaks
   that never break from time zones, sign-outs or updates. Never guilt.
3. **Paywall honesty.** Decide free vs. paid before launch and never take away something that was free. No trial
   that silently becomes an annual charge; clear reminder before any renewal; easy cancel. Offer family pricing.
4. **No ads, ever, and say so.** Ads in a prayer app are the top Muslim Pro complaint and the top praise for Pillars
   and Plum Village.
5. **Privacy as a promise users can repeat.** Don't sell or share data, don't track location, say so plainly in the
   app. Chosen doors and practice history are sensitive (already our rule).
6. **The Guide must name its sources and its limits.** Tie answers to named, reviewed teaching; never speak as God,
   a guru or a prophet; say when traditions or sects differ instead of picking one silently; admit uncertainty.
7. **Respect inner differences.** Sunni/Shia prayer methods, Catholic vs. Protestant wording, Hindu sampradayas -
   reviewers notice and leave when their version is missing. Ask or offer the variant; never imply one is correct.
8. **Accuracy review before anything ships.** Wrong dates, times, readings or prayer wording get 1-star reviews in
   faith apps. Our repeated review passes and Keeper (scholar) sign-off are a real advantage - keep them visible.
9. **Audio plus text, short.** Three minutes, listenable, with the words on screen to learn from. This answers both
   the "listen while busy" love and the "give me transcripts" ask.
10. **Avoid:** selling rituals or blessings; celebrity or political tie-ins that can split the audience; feature
    bloat and noisy notifications; big redesigns that remove loved features; AI-generated filler content; and
    one-tradition defaults presented as the truth.
