# What takes off on Instagram and TikTok, per path

Read on 6 Oct 2026 in the founder's Chrome (read only: nothing liked, followed, commented or saved).

**How this was gathered.** On TikTok I opened each profile and sorted the grid by "Popular", which shows view counts
and the post's caption. TikTok was logged out, so only views are visible there (no likes or comments per post). On
Instagram (logged in) I opened the Reels tab, which shows view counts, and read each top reel's public summary for
likes, comments and caption. **Instagram limit:** the Reels tab only showed the pinned reels plus about the 12–24
most recent ones, so "top" on Instagram means top of pinned + recent, not all-time. Accounts that post several times a
day (Mufti Menk) show only a day or two.

**What I couldn't see.** I didn't watch the videos, so the "hook" is the caption, the on-screen text in the
thumbnail, or the caption's first line. I couldn't measure video length from the grid. Where a caption was only
hashtags, I've said so. Numbers are rounded the way the platforms show them.

Engagement column: views / likes / comments. "—" means the platform didn't show it to me.

**What the winners share**
1. **A child asks Krishna a real-life question** ("why pray?", "why can't I let go?", meat or mercy). Hindu Nuggets'
   three biggest reels are all this format, and they outdo its recent posts 20–50 times over.

   writing). These pull 100K+ on a 270K account.
4. **Numbered series keep people coming back** (Krishna–Karna parts 1–4), but each part reaches fewer people than part 2.
5. Relationship themes (love, letting go, family) beat doctrine everywhere.

**What the winners share**
1. **Answering a claim people are already arguing about wins big.** Nearly all of Dan McClellan's top videos are
   "STITCH INCOMING": he plays someone's viral claim, then calmly corrects it. They're short, it's one claim each, and

   reels reach 100–340K, and its TikToks only a few thousand. Its best Instagram reel was a human, behind-the-scenes story.
4. The two lanes (skeptic-scholar and hype-pastor) both work. What they share is that each post is about **one idea
   only**.

## Across all eight paths

- **One idea, one question, one line.** Every big post is about a single thing.
- **A real-life question beats doctrine**: heartbreak, letting go, prayer when sad, being kind.
- **People asking, a guide answering**: a child asks Krishna, viewers' questions get reply videos, Dan McClellan's
  stitches. The question-and-answer shape fits a mascot that answers.
- **Warmth and humor about insider life** (long homilies, wig shopping, hair untied) reach millions. Explainers made
  only of polished animation (BibleProject) don't.
- **Famous faces and family elders** multiply reach: the actor who plays Jesus, Pogba, a grandmother who survived the
  Holocaust.
- **Comment prompts** ("comment YES", "comment Vaheguru") reliably raise comments.
- Politics and current events spike, but they divide people. Leave them alone.
