# What people say under the threads and videos

Gathered 2026-10-06 in the owner's own Chrome, read only. Nothing was liked, voted, followed, subscribed, saved,
posted or commented. No sign-in or sign-out. One tab of my own, closed at the end. No usernames or profile links are
recorded; comments are paraphrased, with short quotes (under 15 words) only where the wording matters.

## How this was gathered, and what was blocked

- **Reddit: blocked again, so no Reddit replies were read.** In the owner's Chrome the browser extension refuses
  `old.reddit.com`, `www.reddit.com` and `reddit.com` outright ("not allowed due to safety restrictions"). This is
  the same block the built-in browser hit when `REDDIT.md` was made. I did not try mirror sites or other
  work-arounds, because that would be getting around a safety block. So **Task 1 could not be done**: there are no
  vote counts, top replies or locked/removed checks below. Each path's Reddit section says this briefly and points
  back to the thread titles in `REDDIT.md`.
- **YouTube: worked.** For each path I searched YouTube for the creators named in `SOCIAL.md` and the
  `AUDIENCE_RESEARCH*.md` files (Project Shivoham, Gaur Gopal Das, BibleProject, Dan McClellan, Ascension Presents /
  Fr. Mike Schmitz, Unpacked, Yaqeen Institute / Omar Suleiman, Mufti Menk, Basics of Sikhi / Nanak Naam, Doug's
  Dharma, Ajahn Brahm, Daily Stoic), picked their most-viewed videos on the topics young adults ask about, and read
  the first page of comments in YouTube's default "Top comments" order (about 18 per video). Videos weren't played.
  Where a named creator had nothing relevant with real reach, I used a widely watched explainer instead and say so.
- **Two videos had comments turned off** (BimBam's "What is Shabbat?", 739K views, and CrashCourse's "What Does it
  Mean to Be Jewish", 502K). Judaism is the thinnest section as a result.
- **No CAPTCHAs or sign-in prompts** came up on YouTube.
- **Limits.** Top comments skew to older videos, to praise ("thank you, great video") and to viewers from India for
  Hindu and Sikh videos. YouTube commenters are not only 18–35. Like counts are as YouTube showed them (rounded).
  Treat this as "what lands and what repeats", not a survey.
- **Time used:** roughly 45 minutes of browsing, less than budgeted, because Reddit was closed.

## 1. Hinduism

### Reddit
Blocked (see above). Thread titles and the patterns from search snippets are in `REDDIT.md` §1 (Gayatri and
initiation, "will the other gods be upset?", periods and puja, caste questions at school, parents who preach without
explaining).

### YouTube

| Video | Channel | Views | Comments |
|---|---|---|---|
| Is your future already destined? | Gaur Gopal Das | 6.4M | 3,007 |
| Hinduism Explained (not a named creator; the most-watched English explainer) | Cogito | 7.8M | 41,773 |
| Learn the True Meaning of Gayatri Mantra | The Sanskrit Channel | 2.6M | 3,868 |
| What exactly are the Vedas? | Project Shivoham | 1.3M | 2,364 |

**What the top comments say**
- **"Nobody ever told us what it means."** The strongest emotional comment on the Gayatri video (630 likes) is from
  someone made to memorize and chant verses at school with no one explaining them; they say this is why most Hindus
  don't know what their own religion says. Others echo it: "we were so ignorant of our own religion", "beyond what I
  always thought the mantra means". This is the "parents preach without explaining" pattern from `REDDIT.md`, in
  people's own words.
- **The Gayatri is loved across borders and faiths.** Top comments come from Indonesian Hindus, a Buddhist, a
  Westerner who adopted Hinduism, and a Nepali who got the mantra from a guru at age 8 during a thread ceremony. That
  last one is the initiation custom young people worry about on Reddit, told as a warm memory, not a rule.
- **Simple frames click.** On the Cogito video, a "Hinduism for gamers" comment (3.3K likes) maps Brahman to the
  server, the soul to the player account, karma to in-game actions and moksha to the good ending. Another popular line
  calls Hinduism an "open source religion". People love a modern metaphor.
- **Gaur Gopal Das wins on everyday life, not doctrine.** The most-liked lines are a joke about ID-card photos and
  "situations are destined but not responses". Viewers quote one memorable line back to him.
- **Pushback and corrections.** The two biggest corrections on Cogito (18K and 11K likes) fix one detail: Shiva has
  one consort, since Sati was reborn as Parvati. Viewers notice and care about small mistakes in their own stories.
  The caste section draws relief ("caste was meant to be something different from what it became"), which shows the
  topic is live and touchy.
- **Pride in not converting anyone.** Several popular comments say they like that Hinduism doesn't push itself on
  others.

### 5 takeaways for the first week
1. **Answer:** "What does the Gayatri actually mean?" One line at a time, with a warm note that families differ on who
   says it and when, and that saying it since childhood was never "wrong".
2. **Fix the misconception:** "We were never told why." Make every lesson's first card the *why*, not the ritual.
   That is the single biggest hook in the comments.
3. **Hook that clicks:** a modern metaphor (game, phone, server) for big ideas like karma, dharma and moksha, followed
   by the real Sanskrit word.
4. **Get stories exactly right.** Commenters pounce on small errors (Sati/Parvati). Keeper review matters for trust.
5. **Use one quotable line per lesson,** Gaur Gopal Das style ("situations are destined, responses are not"), not a
   lecture.

## 2. Christianity

### Reddit
Blocked. See `REDDIT.md` §2 (doubts after a Christian upbringing, "I feel nothing when I pray", is therapy a sin,
fear of hell after leaving, "I tried reading the Bible and got lost").

### YouTube

| Video | Channel | Views | Comments |
|---|---|---|---|
| What is the Bible? [Animated Explainer] | BibleProject | 5.5M | 5,519 |
| How to Read the Bible Overview | BibleProject | 682K | 192 |
| The Bible Doesn't Say So (TEDx) | TEDx Talks (Dan McClellan) | 115K | 1,172 |

**What the top comments say**
- **Conversion and crisis stories rise to the top.** The most-liked comments on "What is the Bible?" are personal
  turning points: someone who "used to hate god" now reading the Bible (5.6K likes), a teen with a serious diagnosis,
  a parent who prayed for a sick newborn. Many prayer requests sit near the top. People come to Bible content in hard
  moments.
- **"It's a library, not a book."** A popular comment on the how-to-read video says that idea finally made it make
  sense. A Muslim viewer says the video cleared up Old vs New Testament. A dyslexic reader says videos help where
  reading alone didn't.
- **Beginners are intimidated.** A newcomer says it was "not as intimidating as I thought". Another just started
  Genesis and feels at peace. The fear of starting is real; reassurance works.
- **Dan McClellan's audience is the other half.** Top comments cheer critical scholarship and his credentials; one
  argues that the hundreds of Christian denominations prove his point that "the Bible says" really means "my reading
  says". A highly replied comment says people pick when to read literally and when to call it metaphor. These are
  young people working out how to read the Bible honestly, often after leaving church.

### 5 takeaways for the first week
1. **Answer:** "Where do I even start?" Day one: the Bible is a library of many kinds of writing; start with one short
   Gospel scene, not Genesis to Leviticus.
2. **Fix the misconception:** that you have to read it front to back or alone. Short guided readings with a picture or
   a summary help people who've failed before.
3. **Hook that clicks:** "a library, not a book" and "many authors, one story" are the lines people repeat.
4. **Expect people in pain.** Many top comments are prayer requests or crises. The mascot should notice hurt, and
   the 988 path must be one tap away.
5. **Respect the questioners.** A big share of young viewers want honest answers about how the Bible was written.
   Don't pretend there are no hard questions; say "good question, here's what scholars and believers each say".

## 3. Catholicism

### Reddit
Blocked. See `REDDIT.md` §3 (first confession after years, missing Mass and mortal sin, why pray to saints and Mary,
how to pray the rosary).

### YouTube

| Video | Channel | Views | Comments |
|---|---|---|---|
| Making a Good Confession | Ascension Presents (Fr. Mike Schmitz) | 1.4M | 1,615 |
| Going to Confession for the First Time in a Long Time | Ascension Presents | 629K | 911 |
| Do Catholics Worship Saints? | Ascension Presents | 494K | 4,782 |

**What the top comments say**
- **Fear before confession is the biggest emotion.** The most-liked comment on "Making a Good Confession" (2.2K likes,
  190 replies) is someone who feels physically ill at the thought of going back after years and asks for courage.
  On the second video, the top-liked worry (1K likes) is embarrassment so strong it stops them going.
- **Relief after is the biggest payoff.** Dozens of comments report going back after 12, 13, 35, 46, even 50+ years:
  tears "in the most positive way", "liberating", "nowhere near as intimidating as I thought". Several mention a
  patient priest who talked with them first.
- **The tips that helped, in viewers' words:** write your sins on paper and tear it up afterward (762 likes);
  "the priest has probably heard it before"; just go and don't wait. A summary comment lists Fr. Mike's four tools:
  an examination of conscience for your stage of life, the Litany of Humility, the Beatitudes and the Daily Examen.
- **One quote that keeps getting shared:** in confession you give God something he doesn't already have, your
  broken heart (1.8K likes).
- **Saints: the "honor vs. worship" distinction is what clicks.** The top explanatory comment lays out the old terms
  (honor for saints, more honor for Mary, worship for God alone). One cradle Catholic says they were raised in the
  Church and still find it hard to explain to Protestant friends. Another popular analogy: saints are like a Hall of
  Fame. Asking a saint to pray for you is like asking a friend to pray for you.
- **Pushback:** Protestant viewers say they'd rather pray straight to Jesus; some quote Scripture against it. Many
  Protestant and former atheist viewers also say Fr. Mike helped them.

### 5 takeaways for the first week
1. **Answer:** "Can I come back after years?" Yes, and here's what happens in confession, step by step, including
   what to say first. This is the strongest emotional moment on this path.
2. **Fix the misconception:** "Catholics worship saints." Teach honor vs. worship and "asking a friend to pray for
   you", so cradle Catholics can explain it to friends.
3. **Hook that clicks:** the "write it down, then tear it up" practice and "the priest has heard it all". Concrete,
   kind and a little funny.
4. **Name the fear out loud.** The mascot can say "nervous is normal" before any confession lesson. Fear, not
   doctrine, keeps people away.
5. **Offer the Daily Examen** as the tiny daily habit that prepares for confession; it is already a beloved tool.

   practice, not a belief test. Everyone is welcome.
2. **Fix the misconception:** that Judaism is "just a religion" like Christianity minus Jesus. Use the "family /
   people / practice" frame.
3. **Hook that clicks:** the Shema as the six words many Jews learned first and say last. Short, emotional and
   memorable.
4. **Don't require belief.** "Doing comes first" fits; a lesson can be about practice and still welcome doubters.
5. **Keep Jesus out of week one.** It draws debate, not learning, from both sides.

## 5. Islam

### Reddit
Blocked. See `REDDIT.md` §5 (praying salah consistently, Arabic you don't understand, low iman, convert overwhelm).

### YouTube

| Video | Channel | Views | Comments |
|---|---|---|---|
| When You Hit Your Lowest Point (khutbah) | Yaqeen Institute (Omar Suleiman) | 2.5M | 1,355 |
| Do you struggle to pray? Practical steps | Mufti Menk | 750K | 1,597 |
| Why do we HAVE to pray in Arabic? | Mufti Menk | 75K | 298 |

**What the top comments say**
- **The lowest point is very literal.** The top comment on Omar Suleiman's khutbah (2.4K likes) describes depression
  and suicidal feelings; another (1.1K likes) says they feel they're losing their faith and are scared. Several more
  are grief and loneliness. Many say "I needed this today" and that his voice is "therapy". This audience brings real
  crisis to faith content.
- **Salah guilt is the everyday struggle.** On Mufti Menk's video: concentration is "one of my biggest problems"
  (2.8K likes); someone who'd gone weeks or months without praying came back two years later to say they'd improved
  (1.6K likes); someone hears the adhan, feels guilty and still doesn't go (1.1K likes); an 18-year-old says they
  haven't prayed in years and feel like "a bad Muslim".
- **What helps, in their words:** salah is only 5 to 15 minutes, about 1% of your day; friends who pray with you; and
  a line that "clicked" for one viewer: if you beat the whispers here you can beat them anywhere. An autistic teen says
  praying when the room is quiet works for them.
- **Arabic: the distinction that clicks** is that du'a (personal prayer) can be in any language, any time, while
  salah is in Arabic. A new revert says Al-Fatiha feels more powerful in Arabic. Pushback: some ask why the Qur'an
  doesn't say salah must be in Arabic and how they can pray without knowing what they're saying.
- **Fear-based lines appear too** (going to sleep without Isha and waking in the grave). It's popular with some, but it
  sits badly next to comments from people already drowning in guilt.

### 5 takeaways for the first week
1. **Answer:** "I keep stopping and starting. Am I a bad Muslim?" No. Start with one prayer, come back after a break,
   and the streak's rest days are there for this.
2. **Fix the misconception:** that you must understand everything before you pray, or must pray in English to mean
   it. Teach the salah / du'a difference: talk to God in your own language any time.
3. **Hook that clicks:** one phrase of Al-Fatiha a day with its meaning, so the Arabic gains meaning while you say it.
4. **Never use fear or guilt.** The comments are already full of shame; the mascot should be the warm voice that
   says come back.
5. **Crisis care is not optional here.** The most-liked comments on the biggest video are suicidal and grieving
   people. 988 and local help must be easy to find in every language.

- **Cut hair: viewers want permission, not a ruling.** The comments that land on "Is cutting hair a sin?" are relief:

  admits she doesn't have the courage to keep facial hair because society taught her it's ugly.

  outside India say they use it to teach their kids and ask for Punjabi and Hindi versions. One calls it "my comfort
  channel".

1. **Answer:** "Can I belong if I cut my hair?" Lead with welcome: the Guru doesn't make anyone feel small. Leave
   rulings to Keepers.
2. **Fix the misconception:** that Sikhs worship Guru Nanak. Day one: Ik Onkar, One Creator, and the Gurus as guides.
3. **Hook that clicks:** langar and seva. Everyone, even outsiders, already has a story about Sikh kindness. Start
   there, then explain why.
4. **Teach the Mool Mantar one phrase a day,** with meaning. Parents want it for kids too, which is a natural
   "share with family" moment.
5. **Get the Gurbani exactly right** (where lines start and end). Commenters notice.

## 7. Buddhism

### Reddit
Blocked. See `REDDIT.md` §7 (rebirth doubts, non-attachment vs. loving your family, bowing and offerings, "how do I
actually start?", heritage Buddhists).

### YouTube

| Video | Channel | Views | Comments |
|---|---|---|---|
| How To Love Without Attachment (not a named creator; on-topic and widely watched) | Buddhism In English | 2.4M | 1,600 |
| Buddhism for Beginners | Doug's Dharma | 620K | 1,554 |
| "If There Is No Self, What Is Reborn?" | Doug's Dharma | 94K | 743 |

**What the top comments say**
- **Non-attachment lands best as a love lesson.** On the attachment video, the top lines are short and quotable: love
  wants the other person to be happy, while attachment wants them to make *you* happy (1.5K likes). Others: "if you like
  a flower you pick it, if you love it you water it". Many people apply it to a breakup or to a childhood without
  security. So "non-attachment" isn't heard as coldness when it's taught as better love.
- **Ex-Catholic and ex-religious seekers are the core beginner audience.** On Doug's Dharma: "basically a guide to
  being a genuinely good person" (3.3K likes); several raised Catholic, now atheist or agnostic, looking for a
  framework "without worshipping anything"; teens of 15 and 16 exploring. A practitioner of 62 years says they are
  always a beginner (2.2K likes).
- **A common misconception gets airtime:** "it cuts out the God crap" and "there is no God in Buddhism". It's popular,
  but it flattens a tradition many heritage Buddhists practice with devotion, offerings and ancestors.
- **Rebirth clicks through images:** a flame passed from candle to candle (and nirvana literally means "blown out");
  a wave that isn't separate from the ocean; "not *no* self, just no *permanent* self" ("that's a big difference").
- **No temple nearby** is a real barrier for beginners who want community.

### 5 takeaways for the first week
1. **Answer:** "Does non-attachment mean I can't love my family?" No. Love wants their happiness; attachment needs them
   to supply yours. This is the most-shared idea in the comments.
2. **Fix the misconception:** "Buddhism is just a philosophy with no devotion." Gently show bowing, offerings and
   ancestor customs as real practice, for heritage users and seekers alike.
3. **Hook that clicks:** the candle flame for rebirth, and "no permanent self" rather than "no self".
4. **Welcome the ex-religious seeker** without bashing where they came from. Many arrive from Catholicism.
5. **Fill the "no temple near me" gap** with a tiny daily practice and, later, Together circles.

2. **Fix the misconception:** that meditation needs silence, an hour and an empty mind. One minute, sounds around

3. **Hook that clicks:** humor. The most-liked meditation comments are jokes about the busy mind. The mascot can

4. **Give a concrete Stoic morning-and-evening pair** (one quote, one question). People want "something concrete to
   hold onto".
5. **Stay anti-hype.** No manifesting promises; spam sits next to real seekers, and they notice the difference.

## Across all eight paths

1. **People bring real pain to faith content.** On the biggest Muslim, Christian and meditation videos, the most-liked
   comments include suicidal feelings, grief, cancer and depression. Crisis care (988 or local help, every language)
   and a warm mascot are core, not edge cases.
2. **"Nobody explained it to me"** is the strongest hook for people raised in a faith: Hindus chanting without meaning,
   cradle Catholics who can't explain saints, Sikh parents abroad looking for teaching tools, Muslims praying in
   Arabic they don't understand. "Here's the *why*, in one minute" is the product.
3. **Fear and guilt keep people away; relief brings them back.** Confession dread, salah guilt and "am I failing at
   meditation" all point to the same lesson pattern: name the fear, give one tiny step, celebrate coming back.
4. **Short, quotable lines spread.** Commenters quote one line back: "situations are destined, responses are not",
   "love vs. attachment", "you give God your broken heart", "a library, not a book". Every lesson should have one line
   worth repeating.
5. **Simple images make hard ideas click:** a game server for Hinduism, a candle flame for rebirth, a Hall of Fame for
   saints, the pause between words for meditation.
6. **Belonging comes before belief:** "is being Jewish belief or family?", "can I be Sikh with cut hair?", "is a God
   I can understand too small?". Welcome first; rulings go to Keepers.
7. **Get the details right.** Viewers correct small errors with thousands of likes (Sati and Parvati, where the Mool
   Mantar ends). Accuracy is trust.
8. **Outsiders are watching too.** Many commenters are from other faiths, curious and kind (Sikh langar stories,
   Christians moved by Sikh and Buddhist teaching), and some come to argue (Christians under Jewish videos). Lessons
   should welcome visitors and avoid debate bait in week one.
