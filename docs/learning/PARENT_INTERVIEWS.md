# Parent interviews: day one test (5 parents, ~20 minutes each)

Who: Hindu parents with kids about 5–12, ideally from Panditji's community and friends of friends. At least one who
practices regularly, one who "grew up with it but can't explain it", one in a mixed-faith home.
What you need: the day-one link (https://claude.ai/artifact/XtAQCXBy79PxdviHbEPo5q, shared to them first) and their phone.
Your job: mostly listen. Don't explain the app or defend it. Note exact words.

## 1. Their world (5 min)
1. Tell me about your kids. How old? What do they know about being Hindu?
2. What do your kids ask you about it that you find hard to answer? (Get one real example.)
3. What do you use today for this, if anything? (Books, IG accounts like Hindu Nuggets, temple classes, grandparents, YouTube.) What do you like about it? What's missing?
4. When would five minutes a day realistically happen in your house? Who would be holding the phone?

## 2. Watch them use it (8 min)
Hand them the link: "Go through this however you'd naturally use it. Think out loud." Don't help.
Watch for: where they slow down or skip · whether they read the cards · the face at the shared-light picture · whether they get the games right · whether they tap 🔊 · what they say at the score screen.

## 3. Their reaction (5 min)
5. What was that, in your own words?
6. What was your favorite moment? What was boring or confusing?
7. Would you do this with your kids? Which kid? Where, and when?
8. Would you open day two tomorrow without a reminder? What would make you come back?
9. Did anything feel off, inaccurate, or not how your family does it?
10. The characters (Nani, Anika, Dev): do they feel like your family? What would make them feel more real?
11. If this were in the app every day, what should it never do?

## 4. Close (2 min)
12. Can I send you days two to seven next week to try with your kids? (A yes is the strongest signal.)
13. Who else should I talk to?

## After each call (write down within an hour)
- Would they come back tomorrow? yes / maybe / no, and why in their words
- The one moment they loved, and the one that lost them
- Their kids' ages and the question their kids ask
- Anything about accuracy or "not how we do it"

After five, look for patterns. Three or more saying the same thing is a finding; one is an anecdote.
