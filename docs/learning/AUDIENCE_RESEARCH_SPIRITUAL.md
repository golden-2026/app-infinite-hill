# Audience research: "my own path" (spiritual but not religious), American 18–35

Researched 6 October 2026 from public web pages only. No logins, no posting, nothing submitted.

## Summary

This audience is not a blank slate: it already has a daily spiritual vocabulary, borrowed from everywhere, that it
mostly cannot trace. They say "everything happens for a reason," post "manifesting" on a vision board, check their
"big three" placements, light a candle for "good energy," do a "mercury retrograde" joke, keep a gratitude journal
because Headspace told them to, and have a tattoo that says *memento mori* without necessarily knowing it is Marcus
Aurelius's line, not a vibe. About 22% of US adults call themselves spiritual-but-not-religious and a full 70% call
themselves "spiritual" in some sense ([Pew 2023–24](https://www.pewresearch.org/religious-landscape-study/religious-denomination/spiritual-but-not-religious/)); 25% of that SBNR group is 18–29
([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/religious-landscape-study/religious-denomination/spiritual-but-not-religious/)), and 88% of Gen Z practices *something* — prayer, meditation, astrology, tarot — even though only
33% call themselves religious ([The Inner Path](https://theinnerpath.in/gen-z-spirituality/)). The pull is less curiosity than need: 67% of Gen Z report feeling
lonely, the highest of any generation, and burnout and uncertainty are explicitly what is pushing people toward
"calmness, self-awareness, and emotional grounding" ([Grow Therapy](https://growtherapy.com/blog/gen-z-mental-health-statistics/); [The Inner Path](https://theinnerpath.in/gen-z-spirituality/)). The biggest teachers
(Jay Shetty, Gaur Gopal Das, Ryan Holiday, Mel Robbins, Sadhguru, Eckhart Tolle) all do the same thing: take one
old idea, strip the vocabulary, and land it on a modern feeling (burnout, a breakup, "main character energy") in
under two minutes. The honest complication for this door specifically: the audience already associates "spiritual
content" with influencers who oversell (manifesting as a guarantee, "the universe" as a vending machine, wellness
grift) — so the opening lessons need to teach the *real* history and practice behind a phrase they already use, be
honest about what is psychology versus belief, and never need a "trust me" to land.

**Honest gaps up front**

- **Reddit was completely blocked** (r/Stoicism, r/spirituality, r/Buddhism, r/Astrology): WebFetch refused
  `reddit.com` outright and web search returned no indexed Reddit thread content, only third-party summaries and
  Goodreads/Substack pages quoting Reddit-adjacent advice. This is the single biggest gap: Reddit is where this
  audience is most candid and least curated, and I could not read it directly.
- **YouTube comment threads could not be read**, and TikTok/Instagram engagement (likes, comments, which specific
  post did best) was not visible for any creator — only follower/subscriber counts and bios, same limit as the
  Hindu-path research.
- **Quora results skewed toward generic "what is X" definitional questions** rather than the sharper personal
  questions people ask on Reddit or in YouTube comments; I have tried to mark which of the 40+ questions below are
  closer to real lived questions versus encyclopedia-style ones.
- Several creator "most-engaged post" claims below are my inference from which topics a site's own listicle or
  the creator's own description highlighted, not a measured engagement number.
- "How often it comes up" rankings in the Top 20 list are judgment based on how many independent sources raised
  each topic, not a count.

**What the backlash looks like.** This space has a visible critic culture: pieces on "spiritual grifters" flag red
flags like shaming people who want to leave, accumulating wealth without transparency, or telling people negative
feelings are "low vibration" ([Liberating Motherhood](https://zawn.substack.com/p/spiritualityself-help-influencer); [Enodia Therapies](https://www.enodiatherapies.com/blog/the-toxic-positivity-bullshit-in-wellness-culture-thats-making-your-anxiety-worse)). media in 2025 ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2025/02/20/nx-s1-5277087/wellness-influencers-science-creators-maha-rfk-jr)). This matters for us: this audience is simultaneously drawn to this content and *wary* of
being sold to or gaslit about their feelings — a trust gap a classroom-style app can close by being straight about
what's known, what's belief, and what's just useful regardless.

## 3. What people already use to learn

### Meditation and mindfulness apps

- **Headspace**: opens with a structured 10-session "Basics" course; session 1 is breath awareness
  ([search summary of Headspace's own flow](https://neurosity.co/guides/calm-vs-headspace-vs-waking-up-2026)). Headspace's own retention research found that getting a user to return daily for
  **10 days running** is the habit-formation threshold they design around, and onboarding deliberately asks *why*
  someone is there (stress, sleep, focus) before teaching anything, so later nudges can be personalized
  ([How They Grow](https://www.howtheygrow.co/p/how-headspace-grows-the-monk-who)). Streak tracking is explicit and visible from day one.
- **Calm**: similar structure (daily "Calm" session, Sleep Stories, music); first-session content wasn't clearly
  documented in what I could reach, a gap to note.
- **Waking Up** (Sam Harris): the *Introductory Course* starts like the others (focused attention) but moves
  quickly into "non-dual awareness" and "the illusory nature of the self" — i.e., it does **not** wait to go
  philosophical, unlike Headspace/Calm ([search summary](https://neurosity.co/guides/calm-vs-headspace-vs-waking-up-2026)). This is a useful contrast: Waking Up's own fans like the
  depth, but it's explicitly the "more intense" option relative to Headspace/Calm.
- **Stoic (app)**: Day 1 uses the "What If Technique" to find the *source* of a user's anxiety — i.e., it opens
  with a practical emotional tool, not a history lesson on Zeno or the Stoa ([search summary](https://whatisstoicism.com/stoicism-resources/how-to-meditate-like-a-stoic/)). The app has a 4.9-star
  rating (623 Google Play reviews) but draws some complaints about a $20/year paywall and that Ryan Holiday's own
  commentary can't be removed without paying ([Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.adrienblc.dailystoic&hl=en_US)).
- **Insight Timer**: the largest by user count — **25 million users**, 100,000+ free guided meditations from
  14,000+ teachers, 4.9★ (439K App Store reviews) ([search summary](https://driftinward.com/articles/discover/insight-timer-alternative-2026/)). Its scale and free-first model (versus Headspace/Calm's
  subscription-first model) is itself a signal about what this audience will tolerate paying for versus not.
- **Ten Percent Happier** (Dan Harris, now rebranded "Happier"): smaller (500K+ Google Play downloads) but notable
  for explicitly marketing itself to skeptics — "meditation for fidgety skeptics" is its known positioning (not
  independently re-verified here, carried from brand reputation).

### Books and "start here" guides

- **The Daily Stoic** (Ryan Holiday) format is the clearest model for a short-daily product in this space: one
  quote + short reflection per day, 366 days, explicitly designed so "you can start at any point" — a reader
  literally asked on Goodreads whether they had to start from day 1, and the answer was no
  ([Goodreads Q&A](https://www.goodreads.com/questions/1841922-is-it-necessary-to-start-reading-the-book/answers/1017495-you-can-start-at-any)). The newsletter version of this (free daily email) is the on-ramp that built Holiday's whole
  audience, growing from an email list to 320K+ subscribers by consistency alone ([Kit case study](https://kit.com/resources/blog/daily-stoic-case-study)).
- **The Secret** (Rhonda Byrne, 2006): sold 30M+ copies worldwide, 2M+ in the US alone ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Secret_(Byrne_book))) — still the
  most culturally dominant single reference point for "manifesting," even for people who've never read it. It
  draws on 19th/early-20th-century New Thought writers (Wallace Wattles, William Walker Atkinson, Prentice Mulford,
  Robert Collier) that almost no one in this audience has heard of, despite the vocabulary being everywhere
  ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Secret_(Byrne_book))).
- **To Be Magnetic** (Lacy Phillips): the most "credentialized" manifesting brand — reframes manifesting using
  therapy language (attachment theory, nervous-system regulation, "limiting beliefs") rather than pure cosmic
  claims, and runs a paid structured "Pathway" program with 100K+ members ([Kajabi case study](https://www.kajabi.com/creator-stories/to-be-magnetic); [Vice coverage](https://www.vice.com/en/article/to-be-magnetic-manifestation-business-moves-past-positive-thinking-and-into-science/)). This is a model worth
  studying for "teach it without overclaiming": it keeps the comforting ritual while grounding the mechanism in
  psychology, which is close to what this app needs to do.

### What keeps people going, and what makes them quit

- **Going**: visible streaks and a "why am I here" personalization step (Headspace); starting wherever you are, no
  prerequisite reading (Daily Stoic); a single daily action rather than a long session (every app above).
- **Quitting**: feeling like you're "doing it wrong" because your mind won't go blank — the single most repeated
  meditation complaint across every beginner-advice page found ([Headspace's own help page devotes itself to this](https://www.headspace.com/meditation-101/trouble-meditating); [Gassho](https://gassho.info/blog-page/why-cant-i-stop-thinking-during-meditation/)); subscription cost friction (Stoic app reviews
  mention uninstalling over a $20/year paywall, [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.adrienblc.dailystoic&hl=en_US)); feeling preached at or "sold to" rather than taught (the
  wellness-grift backlash, [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2025/02/20/nx-s1-5277087/wellness-influencers-science-creators-maha-rfk-jr); [Liberating Motherhood](https://zawn.substack.com/p/spiritualityself-help-influencer)).

## 4. Everyday moments and pop-culture ideas used without knowing the source

- **"Namaste"** — used as a yoga-class sign-off or ironic meme ("namaste in bed"), but literally "I bow to you," an
  everyday Indian greeting, not an exotic mantra ([Merriam-Webster](https://www.merriam-webster.com/wordplay/the-history-of-namaste); [Buddha Weekly](https://buddhaweekly.com/namaste-respect-overcomes-pride/)).
- **"Karma"** — used to mean fate or "instant payback" ("karma's gonna get them"), but in its Rig Vedic and
  Buddhist origins it means something closer to "action and its long consequence," not luck, and the Buddha
  specifically pushed back on the "luck" reading ([Tricycle](https://tricycle.org/magazine/karma-meaning/); [Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/karma)).
- **"Zen"** — used for anything calm ("keep it zen," a "zen" apartment aesthetic), but is the Japanese word for a
  specific Buddhist meditation lineage (Dhyana → Chan → Zen) that reached China via the monk Bodhidharma and only
  later Japan ([Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/what-is-zen-buddhism/)).
- **"Mindfulness"** — presented as a neutral, clinical technique (apps, workplaces, schools), but its modern form
  (MBSR) was built in 1979 by Jon Kabat-Zinn directly out of Buddhist meditation taught to him by a Zen teacher and
  Thich Nhat Hanh, with the religious language deliberately stripped out — something mindfulness researchers
  themselves have called "stealth Buddhism" ([Tandfonline](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14639947.2018.1572329); [Mindful Leader](https://www.mindfulleader.org/jon-kabat-zinn)).
- **"Manifesting" / "the universe has a plan"** — treated as self-evident common sense, but traces to 19th–20th
  century American "New Thought" writers (Prentice Mulford, William Walker Atkinson, Wallace Wattles) and was
  mass-popularized by *The Secret* in 2006; psychologically, belief in a guiding plan functions as a way to reduce
  anxiety about things we can't control, which is real and useful to name, separate from whether it is literally
  true ([Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/finding-purpose/201807/why-we-think-that-everything-happens-for-a-reason); [Wikipedia: The Secret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Secret_(Byrne_book))).
- **Memento mori tattoos** (skull, hourglass, wilted flower) — a visible pop-culture trend (Harry Styles, Rihanna,
  Miley Cyrus and others reportedly have one, per tattoo-industry writeups), from Stoic daily practice via Marcus
  Aurelius, Seneca and Epictetus, later Renaissance "vanitas" art — not originally a goth or morbid symbol but a
  reminder to use a finite life well ([Daily Stoic](https://dailystoic.com/memento-mori-tattoo/); [Life Countdown](https://www.lifecountdown.app/blog/memento-mori-tattoo-meaning)).
- **Astrology "big three," mercury retrograde, horoscopes** — treated as entertainment-meets-insight, with real
  billions of TikTok views (#manifestation 15B+, #lawofattraction 1B+ views, [Nylon](https://www.nylon.com/life/tiktok-manifestation-trend)) and dedicated creators discussing
  real relationship and communication stress through the lens of planetary movement — a modern vocabulary for very
  old anxieties about timing and control.
- **Gratitude journaling** — a now-default self-help habit (popularized via meditation apps and figures like Mel
  Robbins and Oprah) with roots in both Stoic practice (reviewing the day) and positive psychology research, rarely
  presented with either lineage attached.
- **"The obstacle is the way" / "that which stands in the way becomes the way"** — a widely shared motivational
  phrase (book title, countless Instagram graphics) that is a direct paraphrase of Marcus Aurelius's *Meditations*,
  popularized specifically by Ryan Holiday's book of the same name.
- **Shadow work, "higher self," "ego death"** — now common TikTok/Instagram self-help vocabulary, actually drawn
  from Carl Jung's psychology (the "shadow," individuation) and from psychedelic/mystical-experience research (true
  "ego dissolution"), frequently used more loosely online than in either original context
  ([Scott Jeffrey's explainer notes this gap directly](https://scottjeffrey.com/shadow-work/); [Rediscovering Sacredness on ego death](https://rediscoveringsacredness.com/ego-death-spiritual-awakening-whats-it-all-about/)).
- **"Toxic positivity," "trigger," "boundaries," "holding space"** — pop-therapy phrases now used in ordinary
  arguments and breakups, loosely descended from clinical psychology, frequently flagged by therapists themselves
  as diluted or misused online ([Enodia Therapies](https://www.enodiatherapies.com/blog/the-toxic-positivity-bullshit-in-wellness-culture-thats-making-your-anxiety-worse)).

## How the best creators hook people

1. **Open with the modern feeling, not the old name.** Jay Shetty and Gaur Gopal Das lead with burnout, breakups,
   career stress — Marcus Aurelius or Krishna shows up two sentences later, if at all.
2. **Use a punchy, concrete hook in the first line.** Mel Robbins: "Let Them." Ryan Holiday: "The obstacle is the
   way." Short enough to tattoo, which people also literally do.
3. **Name the thing they already do, then explain it.** Daily Stoic's whole model is "you already know this quote
   or have heard this idea — here's where it's from and what it actually means today."
4. **Reframe woo in credible-sounding language without lying about evidence.** To Be Magnetic's edge over pure
   "ask the universe" content is wrapping manifesting in nervous-system and attachment-theory language — it feels
   more serious, whether or not the underlying mechanism checks out ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/to-be-magnetic-manifestation-business-moves-past-positive-thinking-and-into-science/)).
5. **Normalize the struggle instantly.** Every meditation app's top support content says, in effect, "you're not
   doing it wrong" — this reassurance is itself a hook that keeps people from quitting on day one.
6. **Keep it visually and temporally small.** A one-quote email (Daily Stoic), a 60–90 second video (Jay Shetty), a
   single daily session with a visible streak (Headspace) — the format itself signals "this won't take over your
   life."
7. **Let people self-select their reason for being there.** Headspace's onboarding explicitly asks why you're
   there (stress, sleep, focus) before teaching anything, then tailors language to that answer.
8. **Earn trust by being candid about what's uncertain.** The creators and apps with the most durable reputations
   (Sam Harris/Waking Up, Insight Timer's free-first model) lean into "here's what we actually know" rather than
   promising outcomes — a contrast to the manifesting space, where overclaiming is the most common criticism.

## What to avoid early

- **Opening with a philosopher's name or a school of thought** ("Stoicism holds that...", "In Jungian psychology...").
  Every successful creator above leads with the feeling or the familiar phrase, and introduces the name, if at all,
  after the idea has already landed.
- **Presenting manifesting, astrology or "the universe has a plan" as literally true.** It's fine, and genuinely
  interesting, to explain the psychology of why these beliefs help (reduced anxiety, a sense of agency) and their
  real history (New Thought, Vedic/Buddhist karma, Jungian shadow) — without asserting they are scientifically
  established, per the project's own rule against promoting pseudoscience as fact.
- **Promising meditation will "clear your mind."** This is the single most common cause of early quitting across
  every app and forum reviewed; the correct framing (noticing thoughts, returning gently) should appear in lesson
  one, not buried later.
- **Borrowing a sacred word (namaste, zen, karma, Om) as pure aesthetic without its source.** The audience already
  does this unconsciously; teaching the real meaning is the value-add, but it should be done respectfully and
  without implying the learner was "doing it wrong" to use it casually before.
- **Sounding like a sales funnel.** This audience has absorbed real skepticism of "spiritual" content that leads to
  a paid program, a $20/year unremovable-ad subscription complaint, or guru self-promotion; lessons should never
  feel like the on-ramp to an upsell.
- **Referencing Jay Shetty's "monk" framing uncritically**, given the plagiarism/fabrication reporting — it's fine
  to use well-known phrases or formats from this space, but a model teacher reference should be chosen carefully.
- **Diagnostic or clinical-sounding language** (trauma, nervous system, attachment style) presented as settled fact
  — these terms are popular in this exact audience (To Be Magnetic, "trigger," "holding space") but are often used
  more loosely than their clinical origin; use them if at all with care and real grounding, not as borrowed
  authority.
- **Toxic-positivity framing** ("just raise your vibration," "good vibes only") — this is the exact pattern this
  audience's own critics (and some of its own members) call out as harmful; psychology on this app should make
  room for difficult feelings, not paper over them.

## A proposed first week

| Day | Topic | Why this, here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"Everything happens for a reason" — why we think that, and what's actually going on** | Nearly everyone in this audience has said or thought this line; opening with it (rather than a philosopher) meets them exactly where question #11 and the Top 20 #1 sit, and sets the tone of "we'll explain what you already feel," not "we'll correct you." |
| 2 | **One deep breath, no blank mind required** | Directly defuses the #1 reason people quit meditation apps (thinking they're "doing it wrong"); teaches the real technique (notice, return, repeat) on day two, before any bigger claim is made. |
| 3 | **Memento mori: the death reminder that's really about today** | A concrete, visual, slightly edgy hook (it's already a tattoo trend) that teaches real Stoic practice (Marcus Aurelius, Seneca) through something they've already seen, without needing the word "Stoicism" yet. |
| 4 | **What "namaste" and "karma" actually mean** | Targets two of the most borrowed, least understood words in this audience's own daily vocabulary (Top 20 #5, #6); shows the app teaches real meaning, not just vibes, early and gently. |
| 5 | **Manifesting, honestly: what's psychology and what's belief** | The most commercially loud and most criticized corner of this space (The Secret, To Be Magnetic, astrology); doing this early and straight — explaining the real history and the real psychological comfort it offers, without asserting it's literally true — is exactly the founder's "teach, don't fluff" test. |
| 6 | **Shadow work and the "higher self," minus the jargon** | Introduces real psychological ideas (Jung's shadow) behind TikTok's most borrowed inner-work vocabulary (Top 20 #11), continuing the "you already use this word, here's where it's from" pattern. |
| 7 | **One thing to carry into your week** | Closes the first week the way the strongest daily-practice products do (Daily Stoic, Headspace) — one small, concrete action, not a reading list, so the habit (not the doctrine) is what sticks. |
