For the Keeper. 29 lessons were written or changed on 2026-10-06, from audience research (Reddit, YouTube, app reviews) and a five-year map. Each lists what the writer asks you to confirm. Nothing here is final until you sign off; mark any line you'd change.

## Day 1 · instant karma?
Word: **karma** · Line: *I am the owner of my actions*

- New lesson, written 2026-10-06 for the rebuilt week one (audience research: karma is the single most-corrected idea, so it opens the path). Replaces metta, which moved to day 15.
- 'Intention is what I call karma' paraphrases AN 6.63 (cetanaham, bhikkhave, kammam vadami). Confirm.
- The seed-and-fruit picture is cited to SN 11.10; the image is common across the discourses. Confirm a fitting reference.
- SN 36.21 (Sivaka): the Buddha lists bile, phlegm, wind, their combination, change of seasons, careless behavior, assault and kamma as sources of feeling; the script simplifies to 'illness, weather, accidents and other people's choices'. Confirm the simplification is fair.
- The five reflections (AN 5.57) are chanted daily in many Theravada monasteries and in Plum Village; the script says 'in many temples and monasteries'. 'I am the owner of my actions, heir to my actions' is the script's own rendering, not a quoted translation. Confirm.
- Akkosa Sutta (SN 7.2): Akkosaka Bharadvaja abuses the Buddha; the guest-and-food question; he later goes forth. Told as a story; confirm the telling.
- Schools differ on how karma ripens across lives; the script stays with what the schools share (intention, ripening, not everything is karma) and leaves rebirth to day 7.
- Week one rebuilt 2026-10-06 from the audience research; confirm with the Keeper.

Sources: Nibbedhika Sutta (Anguttara Nikaya 6.63): intention is kamma; Samuddaka Sutta (Samyutta Nikaya 11.10): as the seed is sown, so is the fruit; Sivaka Sutta (Samyutta Nikaya 36.21): not all that is felt comes from past kamma; Upajjhatthana Sutta (Anguttara Nikaya 5.57): the five reflections, 'owner of my actions, heir to my actions'; Akkosa Sutta (Samyutta Nikaya 7.2): the story
