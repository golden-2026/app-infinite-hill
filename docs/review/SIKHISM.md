# Sikhism: lessons to review

For the Keeper. 28 lessons were written or changed on 2026-10-06, from audience research (Reddit, YouTube, app reviews) and a five-year map. Each lists what the writer asks you to confirm. Nothing here is final until you sign off; mark any line you'd change.

How to read it: **day · title** — today's word / the line people carry. Then the writer's notes, then the sources.

## Day 4 · five who said yes

- The tent: the script says 'the tellings differ' and does not describe blood or killing (some tellings say goats were sacrificed; others hold the five were tested in other ways). Confirm this is gentle enough for day 4 (owner: don't freak people out early).
- The five's origins and trades follow the common telling (day 85 uses the same list): Daya Ram, a khatri shopkeeper of Lahore; Dharam Das, a Jat farmer from near Delhi; Himmat Rai, a water-carrier from Puri; Mohkam Chand, a washerman or cloth printer from Dwarka; Sahib Chand, a barber from Bidar. Confirm.
- Kaur: the script says only that 'Sikh women carry the name Kaur'; historians discuss when Kaur became universal for Sikh women, so the script doesn't say it was given in 1699. Confirm. 'Two names that rank no one' reflects the Singh/Kaur ideal; many Sikhs also keep family surnames.
- Story: the Guru receiving amrit from the five; 'wah wah Gobind Singh aape gur chela' is attributed to Bhai Gurdas II (not the Bhai Gurdas of the Adi Granth). Sugar added to the water is told on day 86 (tradition credits the Guru's wife; versions differ on her name), so the story mentions sugar without naming her. Confirm.

Sources: Sikh tradition of the Vaisakhi of 1699 at Anandpur (the call, the five, the first amrit); the Panj Pyare: Bhai Daya Singh (Lahore), Bhai Dharam Singh (Hastinapur, near Delhi), Bhai Himmat Singh (Jagannath Puri), Bhai Mohkam Singh (Dwarka), Bhai Sahib Singh (Bidar); Sikh Rehat Maryada (the Panj Pyare at amrit initiation; the names Singh and Kaur); 'vah vah Gobind Singh aape gur chela' (var attributed to Bhai Gurdas II)
