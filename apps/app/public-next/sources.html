Sources and how we write

old words, quoted honestly.

When a lesson quotes scripture, it quotes an old translation that is in the public domain: free for anyone to use, and trusted by the people who have read it for generations. The King James Bible, the 1917 Jewish Publication Society Tanakh, Pickthall's Qur'an.

When we can't quote an old translation word for word, we don't borrow a modern one. We say it in our own words and tell you we did. Every written lesson ends with a short list of the texts it drew on.

How lessons are made

from the text, back to the text.

  1. Start from the text

    Each lesson begins with one passage, word or story from the path's own texts, listed above.

  2. Written with AI help

    We write lessons with the help of AI, working from those texts, in a plain voice that fits in five minutes.

  3. Checked against the text

    Every quotation is matched to its public-domain source, every paraphrase is marked, and every reference goes in the lesson's sources list.

  4. Reviewed by Keepers

    Before public release, lessons are reviewed by Keepers: scholars from each tradition who check the teaching, not just the spelling.

Where the texts are silent or scholars disagree, the lesson says so. infinite hill is not affiliated with any religious institution and is not a substitute for a congregation or a teacher of your tradition.
