research.

does it work? here’s the method, the evidence, the studies we’re running, and what we won’t claim.

the short version: we drifted away from the oldest practice there is. we're not okay. the people who kept it tend to do better. and most of us lost the way back in.

1 · we drifted away
47%
of Americans belonged to a church, synagogue or mosque in 2020, down from 70% in 1999. about 3 in 10 now have no religion at all.
Gallup, 2021 · Pew, 2025
2 · we're not okay
1 in 2
American adults say they feel lonely, and loneliness raises the risk of heart disease and stroke. and nearly 3 in 5 teen girls felt persistently sad or hopeless.
U.S. Surgeon General, 2023 · CDC, 2023
3 · practice helps
⅓
less likely to die, over a 16-year study, for women who went to services more than once a week.
Harvard · JAMA Internal Medicine, 2016
4 · we lost the way in
14 of 32
basic questions about religion, answered right by the average American, including about their own faith.
Pew, 2019
5 · young people aren’t okay
18–24
year-olds report the lowest flourishing of any age group in many countries.
they’re also america’s most diverse generation yet. open-minded, and ready to lead.
Global Flourishing Study · Nature Mental Health, 2025 · PRRI Gen Z survey, 2024

so we make it easy to start again: a few minutes a day, in your own tradition or many. (these studies show links, not proof, and none were about an app. that's why we measure our own, below.)

the method

six principles. every lesson, every path.

a real story, in 60 seconds
you don’t get a lecture. you get a short film, what the faith actually teaches, and one thing to carry. by the end it’s yours.
learn by doing
guess before you’re told. rebuild the line. put the story in order. every lesson starts with you slightly wrong and ends with you right — the pattern the brain keeps.
native, and checked
your path, from your own texts, in your own words. a scholar of your tradition checks every lesson before it goes live. nothing blended. nothing ranked.
the hill, one story at a time
day one is one short story. the days grow over weeks, never in a jump. a streak that grows one lesson a day, with rest days built in, because every tradition knows rest. no guilt, no hearts. a short check at the start places you anywhere on the path, and if it misjudged, the path offers to walk you back — nothing you've done is lost.
a voice you already listen to
the reason a kid opens the app. celebrity guides, licensed, with permission, narrating only what a Keeper has checked. it never improvises.
a lantern for a friend
finish a day and light a lantern for someone. they get a link, walk a day, and can send one back. or walk as a circle: a teacher, a temple, a church, a mosque, a synagogue, a gurdwara or a family, with no feed and no followers.
what’s known

the evidence for practice is decades deep.

Harvard · JAMA Psychiatry, 2016
weekly attendance was associated with a roughly five-fold lower rate of suicide.
Li, VanderWeele et al. · Nurses’ Health Study
Harvard · American Journal of Epidemiology, 2018
adolescents with a weekly practice were, years later, more likely to report high life satisfaction and to volunteer, and less likely to use drugs.
Chen & VanderWeele · 5,000+ youth

whether this app helps is the question we’re paying to answer.

what we’re testing

our own studies, pre-registered, published whatever they say.

the pilot
a private beta: all eight paths. year one is ready. the rest is being written and checked. a handful of anonymous, opt-in events — never your answers.
the 100-day study
the WHO-5 well-being index is built into the app itself: your 30-second check-in in your first few days, then at day 21, 50 and 100. a pre-registered cohort adds the UCLA Loneliness Scale and PSS-10 at the same points, with an outside academic co-author who doesn’t work for us.
the literacy study, 2027
does a first chapter change what someone can explain about their own tradition? a before/after on Pew’s religious-knowledge questions, one path at a time.
the classroom study, 2027
a semester in a world-religions class: literacy, attitudes toward other traditions, and whether kids keep the practice after the grade is in.
for researchers

if you study religion, adolescence, loneliness or learning and want to work with the data or the cohort: we'll post a research address right here. every figure on this page goes through the Keeper Desk's research seat and is re-checked against its source each year.

what we won’t claim

infinite hill is not therapy, medicine, or a substitute for a congregation, a clergy member or a doctor. it’s a daily practice. if you’re in a hard place, please talk to a person you trust. you don’t have to carry it alone, and we’ll still be here.
